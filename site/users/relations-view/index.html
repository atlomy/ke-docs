The Relations view: seeing and managing a record's connections

Purpose​

Every relation field on a record creates a connection to a record in another collection — an author to a text, a passage to a term, a manuscript to an edition. The Relations view, one of the workspace's view tabs alongside Table, Details, and Publish, shows all of those connections for one record at once: the links it makes, the links other records make to it, and what the platform knows about each one — its status, its confidence, and where it came from. Read this page when you want to audit a record's connections, review links that arrived from an import or an automated step, or remove a link with its full context in front of you. For quick link editing while you work on a record's fields, the Table and Details views are usually faster — see Link one record to another.

Features​

  • Connections in both directions (Active) — for a focused record, the view lists its outgoing links per relation field and, in a separate Incoming section, every link other records make to it.
  • Three layouts (Active) — Graph, Matrix, and Stream modes present the same links as a centered overview, a field-by-field grid, or a flat list with status, confidence, anchor, and provenance columns.
  • Lifecycle and confidence filters (Active) — narrow the picture to one link status, or hide links below a confidence level you set. The filters apply to outgoing and incoming links alike.
  • Add a link by search-and-pick (Active) — search the relation field's target table and click a match to link it. Links you add are saved immediately and recorded under your name.
  • Inspector with safe unlink (Active) — select any link to see its status, confidence, provenance, and anchor detail, and remove it behind a hold-to-confirm step.
  • Approve and Reject buttons — visible in the Inspector but not yet available; changing a link's status from this view is not possible.

Usability — how you use it​

Focusing a record​

Open a table and switch to the Relations tab. The left-hand Rows list shows the table's records; click one to focus it. A record you already opened in the Table or Details view stays focused when you switch to Relations, so the usual path is: find the record where you are, then switch tabs. For a large table the Rows list shows only the first stretch of records — focus the record first and bring it with you.

Beside the rows, the Predicate panel lists the table's relation fields — each with its label and the collection it points to. Pick a field to see the focused record's links through it. If the view reports No relation fields in this table, the table has no relation fields yet; see Build a knowledge environment from scratch for creating one between two collections.

Reading the picture: outgoing and incoming​

The central panel counts the visible links — the view calls each connection an edge — and offers three modes:

  • Graph places the focused record at the center and lists each link with the connected record's label, its status, its confidence, and an A marker when the link points at an anchor.
  • Matrix crosses the linked records against the table's relation fields (the first five), a dot marking which field carries each link, with its confidence beside it.
  • Stream is a flat list: one row per link, with Target, Status, Confidence, Anchor, and Provenance columns.

Below the modes, the Incoming section lists the links that point at the focused record from elsewhere. Each entry shows the other record, the relation field it used, and the link's status. These links belong to the other record's relation fields — Relations is the one place you can see them gathered on the receiving side.

What link statuses mean for your work​

Not every link is made by hand: imports create links between the records they bring in, and automated analysis can suggest connections it found in your material. The status tells you which links are settled:

  • approved — a settled connection. Links you add by hand are approved immediately, under your name; links created by an import arrive approved.
  • draft — a link made while its record was still a draft row. It becomes approved automatically when the record is activated.
  • proposed — suggested by an automated step rather than a person, and not yet settled.
  • rejected — a connection that was rejected. It stays listed under its own status rather than disappearing, so the decision remains visible.

To review what is unsettled, use the Lifecycle filter in the Predicate panel: each status shows a count of the chosen field's links in that state. The Confidence floor slider beneath it hides links below the level you set. Every link can carry a confidence score recorded by whatever created it; a link with no recorded score counts as full confidence when approved and zero otherwise. Confirming or rejecting a link from this view is not yet available — the Inspector's Approve and Reject buttons are visible but not yet active.

Adding a link​

  1. Focus the record in the Rows list and pick the relation field in the Predicate panel.
  2. Under Add target, type part of the name of the record you want to connect. The search runs against the field's target table and shows the closest matches (up to five).
  3. Click a match. The link is created immediately — approved, under your name — with no separate save step.

Choosing a record that is already linked does not create a second link. Both ends must be active records: a draft row cannot be linked from here until it is activated — while drafting, use the record's own relation field instead, as described in Link one record to another. The search box stays unavailable until a record is focused and the chosen relation field declares a target table.

Inspecting a link​

Select any link — outgoing or incoming — and the Inspector on the right shows its relation field, status, the connected record on each side, confidence, and provenance (where the link came from: the source recorded by the process that created it, or the person's name). An Anchor card states whether the link points at a specific place inside the connected record or at the whole record. Below that, the Inspector shows the link's full recorded detail, useful when you need to check exactly what an import or an analysis wrote.

Removing a link — and what happens to the other record​

  1. Select the link in any mode, or in the Incoming section.
  2. In the Inspector, tick Hold-to-confirm unlink.
  3. Click Unlink edge.

Only the connection is removed; both records stay exactly as they are. There is one connection between the two records, seen from two sides — so the link disappears from the other record's relation field at the same time, and an incoming link you unlink is removed from the record that made it. Adding and removing links requires edit access in your environment; if an attempt is declined, contact your environment administrator.

How Relations differs from the Table and Details views​

The Table and Details views edit links field by field, as chips on the record — the fastest way to add or remove a link while editing, covered in Link one record to another. Relations shows the whole connection picture for one record: both directions, statuses, confidence, provenance, and anchor detail — and it is the only view that shows incoming links. Use Table and Details to make links as you work; use Relations to audit them.

Troubleshooting​

SymptomLikely causeFix
The view says no relation fields exist in this table.The table's fields do not include a relation field.Create a relation field between the two collections — see Build a knowledge environment from scratch — or contact your environment administrator.
No links show, with a message about the predicate, lifecycle, and confidence filter.The filters are narrower than the data.Set the Lifecycle filter to All, slide the Confidence floor to 0%, and check that the right relation field is selected.
The Add target search box cannot be typed in.No record is focused, or the chosen relation field does not declare a target table.Pick a record in the Rows list and a relation field that points at a collection. If the field should have one, contact your environment administrator.
Adding a link is declined with a message about draft records.One of the two records is still a draft.Activate the draft first — see Adding and editing rows. To link a row you are still drafting, use its relation field in the Details view.
The search shows no matching records.The target collection has no record matching your term.Try a shorter or different term, or add the record to the target collection first.
Unlink edge stays gray.The hold-to-confirm box is not ticked.Tick Hold-to-confirm unlink, then click Unlink edge.
Linked records show as "Unnamed record" or as strings of letters and numbers.The connected collection has no display field configured, so labels fall back to an internal identifier.Contact your environment administrator and ask them to set a display field for that collection.
Adding or unlinking fails with a permissions message.Your role does not include edit access for this environment.Contact your environment administrator.
