Citations you can trust: stability and styles

Purpose​

You are about to cite something from your knowledge environment in a paper — a published record, a passage, a region of an image, a named part of a 3D model. This page explains what the platform guarantees about that citation: what stays fixed once you have cited it, what an Edition is and why it matters before anything goes to a peer-reviewed venue, and what a citation style is — including how a style gets changed when the active one does not suit your venue. Read it before a platform citation goes into print, or when you want to understand why a citation reads the way it does.

For the steps of actually copying a citation, see Copy a citation for a record or media item.

Features​

  • Stable web addresses for published records — every published record keeps a stable address that stays valid even if your project later reorganizes its pages. Maturity: Active. Limit: stability is per record; links to individual sections within a page are best-effort.
  • Frozen, citable Editions for media items — a media item's version is either a working copy that can still change, or an Edition: frozen and safe to cite in print. Maturity: Active. Limit: freezing a version as an Edition is a deliberate administrator action, never automatic.
  • Refusal of re-processing against an Edition — an automatic re-processing run that would change an Edition's data is refused outright; it stops before anything is touched. Maturity: Active.
  • Rejections that stay rejected — when a reviewer in your project rejects a wrong label that automatic processing proposed, the rejection is recorded permanently; later re-processing does not bring the label back. Maturity: Active.
  • Citations rendered in your project's citation style — published entries carry citations rendered in the style your project has adopted, ready to select and copy. Maturity: Active. Limit: how complete a citation reads depends on the fields your project has filled in.
  • A shipped catalog of standard styles — more than seventy standard citation styles ship with the platform, Chicago and APA among them. A project adopts one active style for how its pages are cited, readers can re-render a page's citation into standard formats right on the page, and the project's own reference conventions (how rows of evidence read) carry their own declared styles. Maturity: Built. Limit: a style outside the catalog can be installed on request through your environment administrator.
  • Reviewed style changes — a style change passes through a proposal built from sample citations, a measured risk report, and a deliberate apply by an environment administrator. No proposal takes effect without that review of its measured reach. Maturity: Active.
  • Citations readable by reference managers — every published entry's citation is also available in Citation Style Language JSON, the format reference managers such as Zotero read. Maturity: Built.

Usability — how you use it​

The promise, in plain language​

Nothing changes underneath your citation by accident. The address you cite keeps resolving, and a published record's content changes only when someone in your project changes the record — no background run rewrites published content on its own. For media items, the guarantee is stronger still: data frozen as an Edition cannot change at all, and the platform enforces that refusal itself rather than relying on anyone's caution.

What "Edition" and stable identity mean before you cite​

A published record's stable web address is one half of citable identity. The other half matters when you cite a media item — a 3D model, an image, an audio or video segment.

The platform can propose labels connecting your project's terms to regions of a media item — a named part of a 3D model, a region of an image. Reviewers in your project approve, amend, or reject each one. That reviewed state lives on a version of the media item, and a version is one of two things:

  • A working copy — annotation on it can still change as your project reviews and refines.
  • An Edition — frozen. Once an environment administrator promotes a version to an Edition, its data is protected: the platform refuses any re-processing run that would alter it.

Before a media citation goes to a peer-reviewed venue, the version you cite should be an Edition. If your venue or repository needs a DOI, that DOI is minted by your institution's library or repository service against the Edition's stable identity — the platform itself does not mint DOIs, it provides the stable target. A citation never exposes the platform's internal identifiers; locations are described in the item's own terms, such as the name of a model part or a region of an image.

In the references rows of a published entry, "edition" usually means a published edition of a source text — a different thing. The Edition on this page is a frozen, citable version of a media item held on the platform.

What stays fixed across model updates and re-processing​

Three things hold after you cite:

  • File updates do not touch your citation. When the underlying model or image file is updated, the new file comes in as a new working copy under its own version label. The Edition you cited — and any DOI adopted for it — keeps its data intact. The new version earns its own review and can become its own Edition later.
  • Automatic re-processing cannot alter an Edition. A re-processing run against an Edition is refused before any data changes. This is not a warning someone could click past; the platform stops the run.
  • Review decisions survive re-processing. A rejection a reviewer made is recorded permanently and is not re-proposed by later runs. Labels a reviewer has approved or amended are likewise preserved — even while the version is still a working copy. Every review action keeps a record of who acted and when, stored with the data.

What a citation style is​

A citation style is the set of rules for how a citation reads: the order of author, title, and date; the punctuation; the abbreviations. The platform defines styles in Citation Style Language — an open format used by reference managers such as Zotero and by thousands of journals — so a style is data your environment holds, not something hand-edited into each record. A catalog of more than seventy standard styles ships with the platform; your project adopts one active style for its pages, and its own reference conventions carry their declared styles.

To tell whether the active style suits your venue, read a rendered citation against the venue's style guide or a recent issue: open a published entry, or its draft preview, and compare. The preview shows the same rendering before anything goes public — if a citation will read roughly in print, it reads roughly in the preview. If you are unsure which style is active, ask your environment administrator; the active style and its version are recorded in your environment.

Requesting a style change​

Style changes run on the administrative side — there is no self-service style control in your workspace. Your part is the request and the evidence:

  1. Collect sample citations formatted the way your venue requires — real examples covering the kinds of material you typically cite.
  2. Send them to your environment administrator with the request. For a standard venue, the administrator may adopt a matching style straight from the shipped catalog.
  3. A proposal is drafted from your samples and measured against your environment's pinned example citations. The proposal carries a count of existing citations it would change and a risk level, so reviewers see the consequences before anything is decided. A proposal may be drafted with machine assistance, but no proposal — machine-drafted or not — takes effect on its own.
  4. A scholar reviews the proposal. The platform refuses to apply a proposal that has not passed this review.
  5. An environment administrator applies the reviewed style. Styles are versioned, the record of who applied which version is kept, and published citations render in the applied style — you do not edit any record to pick up the change.

Troubleshooting​

SymptomLikely causeFix
A citation reads wrong for your venue — punctuation, ordering, or abbreviations are off.The active style does not match the venue, or no style has been adopted yet for that collection.Collect sample citations in the form the venue requires and send them to your environment administrator with a request to adopt or refine the style.
A citation shows placeholder text or is missing pieces.The fields the citation draws on are incomplete on that record or media item.Complete those fields on the record — see Copy a citation for a record or media item for the details.
You want to cite a 3D model or image in print and worry a later update will change it.The version you are citing may still be a working copy.Ask your environment administrator to confirm the version is an Edition — or to freeze one — before the citation goes to press.
A wrong label your project rejected has reappeared after re-processing.This should not happen — a recorded rejection is permanent across re-processing.Report it to your environment administrator, naming the media item and the label. A recurrence is a platform defect, not something you mis-clicked.
Your venue or repository asks for a DOI.The platform does not mint DOIs itself.Ask your environment administrator — DOI minting is handled by your institution's library or repository service against the frozen Edition's identity.
