Reviewing your team's work

Purpose​

This page is for the researcher who oversees others — a project director, a senior scholar checking a junior colleague's entries, anyone who reads and signs off on work before it reaches the public. It walks the review loop the platform actually supports today: read what your colleagues have drafted, tell a draft from a finished record, check the connections between records, and use publication as the deliberate checkpoint it was built to be. Read it when you take on a supervisory role, or when you want to know exactly which parts of review the platform handles for you and which stay a matter of talking to your team.

A note before the steps: the platform gives you a workspace where every stage of a record is visible, and a publication gate that you control through who holds publish access. It does not give you a separate "submit for review" queue or an in-record comment thread. Where a mechanism exists, this page names it; where one does not, it says so plainly rather than implying a workflow that is not there.

What you can review, and how​

  • Read every stage in one place (Active) — drafts, finished records, and published records all sit in the same workspace table. Nothing your team works on is hidden from a colleague with read access; review starts by reading what is already in front of you.
  • Tell a draft from a finished record (Active) — a new record is created as a draft and stays one until every required field is filled and it is activated. The two stages behave differently, and the difference is what tells you a record is finished.
  • Review the connections between records (Active) — the Relations view gathers every link a record makes and every link made to it, with each link's status, so you can audit how your team has connected the corpus.
  • Publication as the review checkpoint (Active) — publishing is a separate, deliberate act, and publish access belongs to your environment's administrators. Composing roles so that only reviewers hold publish access turns "publish" into a real sign-off step.
  • A separate review queue or in-record comments — not part of the platform. Feedback happens through the record stages, the link statuses, and conversation outside the platform, not through a built-in review inbox.

Usability — how you use it​

Reading what your team has drafted​

Drafts are not hidden. A new record is created as a draft the moment a colleague asks for one, and it appears in the workspace table alongside finished records straight away. Any colleague with read access can open it, read its fields, and follow its connections. So the first move in reviewing work is to open the relevant collection's table and read.

There is no separate filter that isolates "only drafts" or "only finished records" in the table today. You tell the stages apart by behavior rather than by a status column — described next. To narrow a large table down to the records you care about while you review, use the ordinary filters and saved views covered in Searching and reading.

Telling a draft, a finished record, and a published record apart​

A record travels through three stages, and each one behaves differently — that behavior is how you read where a record stands:

  • Draft. A work in progress. It is visible to your project in the workspace, it cannot be linked to other records yet, and it never reaches a public reader. Required fields may still be empty.
  • Finished (activated). Every required field is filled and the record has been activated. It is now a full part of the collection — it can be linked to other records, and it is ready to be published. It is still visible only inside the workspace.
  • Published. The record has a public page anyone with the address can read.

When you open a record and find a required field empty, or find that it refuses to link to another record, that record is still a draft — not yet finished. A record that links freely and offers itself to the Publish view has been activated. The full lifecycle is described from the editor's side in Adding and editing rows and from the publisher's side in Public reader.

Reviewing the connections between records​

Connections are part of the work, and the Relations view is where you audit them for one record at a time. Open a collection, switch to the Relations tab, and focus the record you are checking. The view shows the links that record makes to others and — in a separate Incoming section — the links other records make to it. Each link carries a status:

  • approved — a settled connection. A link a colleague adds by hand is approved immediately under their name; links that arrive from an import are approved.
  • draft — a link made while its record was still a draft; it becomes approved automatically once the record is activated.
  • proposed — suggested by an automated step rather than by a person, and not yet settled. This is the status to watch when you want to know what a machine suggested and a person has not yet confirmed.
  • rejected — a connection that was turned down. It stays listed under its own status so the decision remains visible.

Use the Lifecycle filter in the Relations view to show only one status — setting it to proposed is the fastest way to see what automated analysis has suggested but no one has yet acted on. One caution on what you can do from there: the Inspector shows Approve and Reject buttons, but they are not yet available — confirming or rejecting a proposed link from this view is not possible today. What you can do is read the proposal, its confidence, and where it came from, and remove a link you judge wrong, behind a hold-to-confirm step. The full Relations workflow is covered in The Relations view.

Publication as the review checkpoint​

Activation and publication are deliberately separate steps, and that separation is what lets publication act as a review gate. A record can sit at the finished (activated) stage for as long as your project needs — through internal checking, peer reading, or waiting on a companion record — and nothing reaches the public until someone publishes it.

Publishing requires publish access, and publish access belongs to your environment's administrators. Researchers whose roles include only edit access can add and activate records but cannot publish them. This is the lever that makes review real: when roles are composed so that contributors can finish records but only reviewers hold publish access, publication becomes a sign-off that a reviewer performs after reading the work. Your environment administrator composes those roles; the permissions panel page describes what each role covers and how custom roles are built. Publishing itself — including what happens when you publish a whole table at once — is covered in Publish a record.

Giving feedback​

The platform does not carry a built-in review queue, an approval workflow you route a record through, or a comment thread attached to a record. Feedback in the platform happens through the things that are visible to the whole project: the stage a record sits at, the links and their statuses, and the values in the fields themselves. A reviewer reads the work, and the decision to publish (or not yet) is the recorded outcome.

Conversation about the work — "this field needs a source", "hold this record until the companion table is ready" — happens between you and your colleagues outside the platform. Keep that in mind when you set up a review practice: the platform records the state of the work and who published it, not the discussion around it.

Watching what changed after a re-import​

When your team re-imports its source data, the platform protects the edits your colleagues made and shows only what is new, changed, or different from the last import. As a reviewer this is the moment to check that an automated refresh did not undo deliberate work. Two pages cover what to look at: Understand what changed after a re-import explains the delta — the new, changed, removed, and carried-forward items — and confirms that labels, settings, and other edits your team made are never overwritten; Saved views and re-imports explains what happens to the views your team relies on when fields change.

Troubleshooting​

SymptomLikely causeFix
I cannot find a "review" or "submitted" filter to see only records awaiting sign-off.The platform has no separate review queue or record-state filter; drafts and finished records share the table.Read the records directly. Use ordinary filters and a saved view to narrow the table — see Searching and reading.
A record I want to check refuses to link to another record.The record is still a draft; drafts cannot be linked.That is the signal the record is unfinished. It can be linked once every required field is filled and it is activated — see Adding and editing rows.
The Approve and Reject buttons on a proposed link do nothing.Changing a link's status from the Relations view is not yet available.Read the proposal's status, confidence, and provenance to judge it. You can remove a link you decide is wrong; see The Relations view.
A colleague published a record I had not finished reviewing.Their role includes publish access.Ask your environment administrator to compose roles so that only reviewers hold publish access — see the permissions panel.
I want to leave a comment on a record for a colleague.There is no in-record comment or feedback thread.The platform records record state and who published; discuss the work with your colleague directly, and use the record's stage and links as the shared reference.

A note on permissions​

What you can see and do as a reviewer reflects your role. Read access lets you open and read every stage of your team's records and follow their links; edit access adds activating and linking; publish access — the gate that makes review a real checkpoint — belongs to your environment's administrators. Roles are composed and assigned by your environment administrator; the permissions panel page describes the building blocks each role is made from.
