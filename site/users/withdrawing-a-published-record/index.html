Withdrawing a published record

Purpose​

Sometimes a record that has a public page should not be public any more — it was published by mistake, a rights question came up, or it needs substantial rework away from public view. Withdrawing a record takes its public page off the web while leaving the record itself untouched in your workspace. This page explains when withdrawal is the right tool, what it does, what a reader sees afterwards, and how to bring a withdrawn record back.

Withdrawal is the reverse of publishing, and like publishing it is held by your environment's administrators. Your data is never deleted by a withdrawal — only the public face is removed.

Features​

  • Whole-record withdrawal — withdrawing a record removes its public page everywhere the record is published, in one action. Maturity: Active.
  • Public search and index removal — a withdrawn record also leaves public search results and the browsable index. Maturity: Active. Limit: the search listing refresh can lag.
  • Workspace data untouched — withdrawal changes nothing inside your workspace: the record stays in your collection, activated, linkable, and editable. Maturity: Active.
  • Reversible — a withdrawn record can be published again; an unchanged record returns at the same web address. Maturity: Active.
  • Withdrawn is remembered as withdrawn — the platform keeps the difference between a record that was withdrawn and one that was never published, and withdrawing the same record twice causes no harm. Maturity: Active.

Usability — how you use it​

When to withdraw — and when to edit and publish again​

Withdrawal is for taking a record off the public web entirely. It is not needed for corrections:

  • The public page has an error — edit the record in your workspace and publish again. The public page shows your corrected version after the next publish. See Publish a record.
  • The record should not be public at all — published by mistake, an unresolved rights or permissions question, content that needs review away from public view — withdraw it.

If you are unsure, withdraw first: it is reversible, and the record loses nothing while it is withdrawn.

Who can withdraw​

Withdrawing, like publishing, requires publish access, which belongs to your environment's administrators. It is not one of the workspace publishing controls — to withdraw a record:

  1. Confirm that withdrawal is what you need, not an edit followed by publishing again (see above).
  2. Contact the administrator of your environment and name the record to withdraw.
  3. After the withdrawal, open the record's public address and confirm it no longer returns the record.

If you do not know who administers your environment, ask the colleague who handles publishing in your project.

What withdrawal does​

When a record is withdrawn:

  • Its public page is removed — everywhere the record was published. If your project publishes the same record on more than one public surface, withdrawal covers all of them together.
  • It leaves public search and the browsable index — readers can no longer find it by searching or browsing.
  • Your workspace is unaffected — the record keeps its place in your collection, its links to other records, and its full editing history. Colleagues can keep working on it as before.

What a reader sees afterwards​

A reader who opens a bookmarked address to a withdrawn record no longer receives the record. The platform stops serving the withdrawn page — its text, its citations, and the links it offered to any media. The address is not reassigned to a different record.

One thing withdrawal does not do: recall a media file someone already has. Withdrawal stops the platform from handing out a record's media, but a large file such as a 3D model or a scan is delivered to the reader's browser by a direct file address. Anyone who already saved that exact file address — or a service that cached the file — may still be able to open the file itself, even though the record's page is gone.

This matters when you are withdrawing for a rights or permissions reason rather than for editorial rework. Withdrawal is the right first step, but if a file must become genuinely unreachable, tell your environment administrator so the stored file can be dealt with directly.

A citation a reader copied earlier keeps its text, but its web address stops resolving while the record is withdrawn. Publishing the record again makes the cited address work again.

Publishing again after a withdrawal​

A withdrawn record can be published again at any time by anyone with publish access — the same way as any publication. The public page returns, and the record reappears in public search and the index.

  • An unchanged record returns at the same web address, so earlier citations and bookmarks work again.
  • If the record's identifying details were edited while it was withdrawn, its address can change with them — check the address of the new public page after publishing.

A whole-table publish includes withdrawn records. When your project next publishes the entire table — for example after a period of internal review — a withdrawn record in that table is published again along with the rest. If a record must stay off the public web, tell whoever runs your project's publishing, so it is not swept back in by the next table-wide publish.

Troubleshooting​

SymptomLikely causeFix
A withdrawn record is back on the public site.The whole table was published since the withdrawal; a table-wide publish includes every activated record, withdrawn ones included.Ask your environment administrator to withdraw it again, and agree with whoever publishes that this record stays out of table-wide publishes.
You withdrew a record but its public page still shows.Your browser may be showing a stored copy, or the withdrawal did not cover this record.Reload the page. If it still shows the record, contact your environment administrator.
A withdrawn record still appears in public search results.The search listing refreshes when a record is withdrawn, but the refresh can lag.Re-check after a short interval. If the result persists, contact your environment administrator.
You want a public page corrected, not removed.Withdrawal is not needed for corrections — the public page updates when the record is published again after your edit.Edit the record and publish again. See Publish a record.
You cannot find a withdraw option in your workspace.Withdrawal is held by your environment's administrators; it is not one of the workspace publishing controls.Contact the administrator of your environment.
A reader reports that a cited address returns nothing.The record is withdrawn.If the withdrawal was intended, no action is needed — the address works again when the record is re-published. If it was not intended, ask your environment administrator to publish the record again.
