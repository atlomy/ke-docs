Privacy and Safety

Purpose​

This page explains what the platform records about you while you work, what it deliberately does not record, and the safety rules that occasionally hold content back. The bias throughout is to keep your project's content inside your project and to minimize what flows anywhere else. Read it when you want to know what the platform sees, when a feature seems to be holding a number back from you, or when a new chat panel appears and you want to understand what it is doing.

Features​

  • Your data stays inside your environment (Active) — your project's records, search queries, and edits live inside your environment's boundary. Researchers in other environments cannot see across it, and the boundary is enforced at the data layer, not by convention.
  • Content-free activity records (Active) — when the platform records that an action happened (a search ran, an import finished, an assistant answered), it stores numbers and short labels — duration, row count, success flag — never the content itself.
  • One-way redaction of sensitive text (Active) — where the platform must keep a record involving text you wrote, the text is replaced with a one-way fingerprint keyed to your environment; the original cannot be reconstructed from it. Text that looks like an email address, a phone number, or an identification number is dropped entirely — not even a fingerprint is kept.
  • Small-sample protection on cross-environment statistics (Active) — a statistic computed across environments is published only when enough separate environments contributed to it that no single environment or small team could be identified from it. Below that threshold, the number is withheld.
  • Tamper-evident activity records (Active) — the platform's activity records cannot be silently edited or deleted; the platform-level log links each entry cryptographically to the one before it, so any alteration becomes visible during routine verification by the platform operator.
  • Chat conversations are not kept (Active) — a chat assistant keeps your conversation only for your current working session, or reads it once to extract the structured details you confirmed and then discards the raw text. No chat surface keeps conversations longer (see below).
  • Data-protection assessment tracking (Active) — when deeper activity recording is switched on for an environment, the platform records that the environment's Data Protection Impact Assessment must be refreshed, and tracks that refresh to completion.
  • Per-environment usage statistics (Active) — whether your environment records usage statistics about features such as in-form tips is a per-environment setting, not a platform-wide constant. Your environment administrator can tell you whether it is on.

Usability — how you use it​

At a glance​

If you have only a minute:

  • Your project's data and the queries you type stay inside your project. They are not visible to researchers in other environments, and the platform operator sees only redacted aggregate numbers, never your content.
  • Statistics computed across environments are published only when enough separate environments contributed that no single environment or small team of researchers could be identified from them.
  • Chat panels keep your conversation only for your current session, or read it once to pull out the details you confirmed. No chat surface keeps conversations longer today.
  • The record of who edited what stays inside your environment. It exists to give your editors and your administrator a clear history, not to surveil you.

What is recorded about your activity​

  • Your researcher account. Sign-in identity, display name, role, permissions. Stays inside your project.
  • Your edits. Drafts you create, fields you change, rows you activate. Attributable to you within your project, so editors and reviewers can see who did what.
  • Chat conversations, within limits. A chat assistant follows the retention behaviors described below; the default keeps nothing beyond your session.

What is deliberately not recorded or transmitted​

  • Your search queries do not leave your environment. Searches are matched inside your project. The platform-wide measurement layer never receives the words you typed — by design, it can only hold counts, timings, and one-way fingerprints.
  • No content leaves your workspace. Platform-level activity records hold numbers and short labels, not the content of searches, imports, or conversations.
  • Small-sample statistics are withheld. A cross-environment number with too few contributing environments is not published at all.
  • Sensitive strings are redacted before storage. One-way fingerprint, keyed to your environment; text matching high-risk patterns (email addresses, phone numbers, identification numbers) is dropped entirely.
  • The platform's activity records are tamper-evident. A background guarantee; no action needed from you.

Much of this measurement is also switched off unless the platform operator deliberately turns it on — the platform's default posture is not to collect.

When the workspace holds content back​

Two situations:

  • Publication readiness. A draft, a row missing a required field, or a row in a review queue is not eligible for the public reader. The publish view shows what a record still needs before it can go live — see Preview an entry before publishing.
  • Small-sample statistics. Where a precise cross-environment number could identify the behavior of a small group, the platform withholds it. This rule applies to platform-published numbers; it never hides your own data from you inside your workspace.

Your chat history​

A chat assistant on the platform follows one of these retention behaviors:

  • Session only (the default). The conversation exists for your current working session and disappears when the session ends. Nothing goes into durable storage.
  • Extract only. The conversation is read once by an extractor that pulls out structured details you confirmed (for example, a summary of what you said you were importing); the raw conversation is then discarded immediately.
  • Nothing longer. No chat surface keeps your conversation beyond the two behaviors above. Longer retention is not available today.

The chat panel itself tells you what it records: it carries a visible notice stating that questions may be included in usage records and that no workflow changes happen through chat. The assistant can answer questions and explain what it sees, but it cannot change your data at all — it cannot approve, apply, publish, or delete anything. Actions happen only through the regular controls you click.

One practical note: records belong to the project, not to you personally. Removing a researcher account does not remove the project's published records. If you are leaving a project and want to know exactly what account removal covers, ask your environment administrator.

What to ask your environment administrator​

  • Which chat surfaces are enabled, and what each one retains.
  • Whether usage statistics — such as tip acceptance numbers — are being collected in your environment (see Efficiency tips for environment administrators).
  • Whether a Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) has been completed for the project. A DPIA names who can see what, for how long, under what conditions. Your administrator can find out its status from the platform operator and tell you what it covers.

A worked example: suppose you notice a chat panel today that was not there last week. The right question to your administrator is two-part — "What does this panel retain?" and "Is the DPIA updated to cover it?" If both have clean answers, the surface is operating inside the platform's normal posture. If either is open, the platform's default — nothing kept beyond the session — is what applies until the answers land.

What the environment boundary means in practice​

Your project's database records, search index, and change history all live in their own slice of the platform. Operations that touch your project read from your slice. Operations that produce platform-wide numbers read from a separate measurement layer that holds counts and timings, never content. When you type into search, the query is matched inside your slice; the platform-wide layer can learn at most that a search happened, how long it took, and how many results it returned. Multiply by every action on every surface and you get the platform operator's view: a picture of motion without content. Researchers in another environment see less — they cannot see across the boundary at all.

A statistic you expected shows no value, or is missing entirely.Small-sample protection withheld it: too few environments contributed for the number to be published safely.This is intentional. The number appears when more environments contribute.Your environment administrator; Tips and guidance.
A new chat panel appeared and you do not know what it retains.The panel was enabled for your environment.Read the panel's own notice about what it records; if it is unclear, ask your administrator what the panel retains and whether the DPIA covers it.Your environment administrator.
