Privacy and Safety

Audience: Researcher Last aligned with platform state: 2026-05-25 Platform version: V2 (pre-1.0)

Purpose​

This page explains what the platform records about you while you work, what it deliberately does not record, and the safety rules that occasionally hold content back. The bias throughout is to keep your project's content inside your project and to minimize what flows anywhere else. Read it when you want to know what the platform sees, when a feature seems to be holding a number back from you, or when a new chat or telemetry panel appears and you want to understand what it is doing.

Features​

  • Tenant-scoped data and queries — your project's data, search queries, and edits stay inside your project's boundary; cross-tenant readers cannot see across. Substrate: ADR-016 (docs/developer/architecture/adr/016-telemetry-substrate.md), telemetry/services/_redact.py. Maturity: Active. Limit: the boundary is enforced per-row at the data layer; tenant configuration controls what is exposed within the boundary.
  • Payload-free aggregates — when the platform records that an action happened (a search ran, an import finished, an assistant answered), it stores numbers and short labels — duration, row count, success flag — not the content. Substrate: ADR-016, Spec 0018 Sprint C platform telemetry (commit e2702a7f). Maturity: Active. Limit: the constraint is enforced by the telemetry envelope; any new producer must conform.
  • Per-tenant HMAC redaction of sensitive strings — anywhere the platform must keep a record involving text you authored (audit log, telemetry strings), the text is hashed with a per-tenant secret key. The hash is one-way. Substrate: telemetry/services/_redact.py, shared helper extraction (commit c37e80da, 2026-05-25). Maturity: Active. Limit: the per-tenant key is rotated by the platform operator; rotation invalidates prior hash continuity.
  • K-anonymity floor on cross-tenant statistics — any cross-tenant number is clamped: if fewer than TIP_ROLLUP_MIN_K records contributed, the platform substitutes a neutral placeholder. Substrate: ADR-044 (docs/developer/architecture/adr/044-voice-and-vocabulary-substrate.md), R4 absorption (commit 9b7d2cff). K-anonymity is a privacy floor that requires at least N records before a statistic is published. Maturity: Active. Limit: TIP_ROLLUP_MIN_K is configurable per rollup; default is conservative.
  • Tamper-evident audit chain — each audit entry is cryptographically linked to the one before it; tampering breaks the chain visibly during routine verification. Substrate: Spec 0018 Sprint C (commit 28b33c65), receipt audit indexing (commit 16fd5b47). Maturity: Active. Limit: detection is via verification, not prevention; the chain shows tampering but cannot prevent storage-layer access.
  • Four-tier ambient chat retention — session only (default), extract only, 30-day verbatim with redaction, or research-consented. The chat surface always announces which tier is in effect. Substrate: ADR-045 (docs/developer/architecture/adr/045-chat-transcript-retention-regime.md), ambient chat (PR #324, commit f5d9c08c). Maturity: Active. Limit: tiers above session-only require a Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA).
  • DPIA gate on higher retention tiers — moving a chat surface above session-only requires the tenant to sign a Data Protection Impact Assessment naming who can see what, for how long, under what conditions. Substrate: Spec 0018 Sprint C DPIA ops log (commit 0f37856a), ADR-045. Maturity: Active. Limit: the DPIA is a documentation gate, signed by the tenant administrator.
  • Tip telemetry opt-in axis — tips_telemetry_enabled is a per-tenant axis; default is off. Substrate: Spec 0030 Phase 6.E (commit f325571e), ADR-044. Maturity: Active. Limit: opt-in only; the platform does not collect tip telemetry from tenants that have not enabled it.

Usability — how you use it​

At a glance​

If you have only a minute:

  • Your project's data and the queries you type stay inside your project. They are not visible to other tenants on the platform, and the platform team sees only redacted aggregate numbers, never your content.
  • The platform applies a K-anonymity floor so that no cross-tenant number can be used to identify the behaviour of a small group of researchers.
  • Ambient chat panels default to "session only" — no transcript is stored beyond your current sign-in unless your tenant administrator deliberately enabled a longer regime, and the panel always tells you which is in effect.
  • The audit log of who changed what is tamper-evident and tenant-scoped. It exists to give your editors and your administrator a clear history, not to surveil you.

What is recorded about your activity​

  • Your researcher account. Sign-in identity, display name, role, permissions. Stays inside your project.
  • Your edits. Drafts you create, fields you change, rows you activate. Attributable to you within your project, so editors and reviewers can see who did what.
  • Search activity within your workspace. Queries go into a short-lived, in-project log used to power your recent-search list and quick-search suggestions. Tenant-scoped; retention controlled by your administrator.
  • Optional chat transcripts. Ambient-chat surfaces follow one of the four tiers above (default: session only).

What is deliberately not recorded or transmitted​

  • Raw queries do not cross the tenant boundary. When the platform team reviews search performance across all tenants, they see counts, hit rates, response times — never query strings.
  • No payload leaves your workspace. Telemetry stores numbers and short labels, not the content of searches, imports, or conversations.
  • Small-sample statistics are clamped. Cross-tenant numbers are subject to the K-anonymity floor.
  • Sensitive strings are redacted before storage. Per-tenant HMAC hash; one-way.
  • The audit chain is tamper-evident. Background guarantee; no action needed from you.
  • No tenant schema names cross the boundary. Internal field and record-type names stay inside the tenant.

When the workspace holds content back​

Two situations:

  • Publication readiness. A draft, a row missing a required field, or a row in a review queue is not eligible for the public reader. The workspace surfaces what is blocking.
  • Cross-tenant statistics clamped at the K-anonymity floor. Where a precise number would be misleading or identifying, the platform substitutes "not enough data to compare". This rule applies to platform-emitted numbers and to publication eligibility; it does not redact your own data inside your workspace.

Your ambient chat history​

The four retention behaviours:

  • Session only (default). Conversation lives in your browser session and disappears when the session ends. Nothing in durable storage.
  • Extract only. The conversation is read briefly by an extractor that pulls out structured details (e.g., a summary of what you said you were importing); the raw conversation is then discarded.
  • 30-day verbatim. Stored 30 days with redaction applied to sensitive strings, then summarised and discarded. Turned on only when a specific operational need is documented (DPIA-gated).
  • Research consented. Stored longer under an explicit, signed research-consent flow. Never the default; requires opt-in.

Whichever applies, the chat surface tells you which regime is in effect — there is no hidden retention. The assistant in an ambient chat can answer questions and offer suggestions, but it cannot change your data on its own — anything that would write to the workspace requires you to click a confirmation. Even a stored transcript records a conversation, not silent actions.

Two practical notes:

  • You can clear an in-session chat at any time. Even under "session only", the panel exposes a clear-and-restart action.
  • Deleting an account does not delete published rows. Records belong to the project, not to you personally. Your account, draft-in-progress data, and session-only chat history are removed.

What to ask your tenant administrator​

  • How long workspace search history is kept.
  • Which chat surfaces are enabled, and which retention tier they use.
  • Whether efficiency-tip telemetry is enabled (the tips_telemetry_enabled axis is per-tenant opt-in; see Tenant admin efficiency tips).
  • Whether a Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) has been completed for the project. A DPIA names who can see what, for how long, under what conditions. Your administrator has a copy and can share it on request.

A worked example: suppose you notice an ambient chat panel today that was not there last week. The right question to your administrator is two-part — "Which retention tier is it set to?" and "Is the DPIA updated to cover it?" If both have clean answers, the surface is operating inside the platform's normal posture. If either is open, the platform's default — session-only retention — is what is in effect until the answers land.

What the tenant boundary means in practice​

Your project's database rows, search index, media files, and audit log all live in their own slice of the platform. Operations that touch your project read from your slice. Operations that produce platform-wide numbers read from a separate metrics layer that holds counts and timings, never content. When you type into search, the query string is matched inside your slice; the platform-wide layer is told only "a search happened, it took n milliseconds, it returned k hits." Multiply by every action on every surface and you get the platform team's view: a dashboard of motion without payload. Researchers in another tenant see less — they cannot see across the boundary at all.
