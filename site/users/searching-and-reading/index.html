Searching and Reading

Purpose​

This page covers two things that go together: how you find records in your knowledge environment, and how you read what you find — fields, related records, media, citations, and a preview of what a published record looks like to a reader outside your project. Read it when search is not finding what you expect, when you need a scholarly citation from a record, or when you want to check how a record will read before it is published.

What is available depends on how your environment is configured. The sections below describe what the platform supports; your project may use all of it or a subset.

Features​

  • Quick search — a search panel that finds records across every collection you can access. Results are grouped by collection and show which fields matched; selecting a result opens the record. Scope it to the current table, to everything you can access, or to a single collection. Maturity: Active.
  • Search & Read — a full search page for browsing, filtering, and reading results in place. Scope the search to a collection and add conditions on field values; the page address encodes your whole search, so it can be shared. Maturity: Active.
  • Current-table filtering — a toolbar box that narrows the rows already loaded in the active table, and a filter builder for column conditions. Maturity: Active.
  • Details panel — every field of a record, including fields the table does not display as columns. A separate Relations view shows the record's connections. Maturity: Active.
  • Reader preview — the Details panel includes a preview pane rendered with the same engine as the public reader, so what you see is what a reader would see. Maturity: Active.
  • Media and citations — a record's reading view lists its attached media, and citations are rendered in your project's citation style (defined in Citation Style Language, the format reference managers like Zotero understand). Maturity: Active. Limit: which media appear depends on what your project attached.

Usability — how you use it​

Quick search — find a record anywhere you can access​

Open quick search from the Search database… control at the top of the workspace, or press Ctrl+Shift+F (⌘⇧F on Mac). Type a term. Results appear grouped by collection, each showing the record's label and the fields where your term matched. Select a result to open that record.

The panel starts scoped to Current table when a table is active. Switch to All workspace to search everything you can access, or narrow the search to a single collection. This is the right tool for "find this person, source, or term, wherever it lives."

Quick search matches against the fields your environment includes in its search configuration — by default, the text fields of each record. Matching is forgiving about diacritics: in a polytonic-Greek project, a term typed in modern monotonic Greek finds its polytonic spellings — breathings, iota subscripts, and circumflex and grave accents are all ignored — and Arabic vowel marks are ignored the same way. The acute accent and the diaeresis still count, in Greek and in Latin script alike: a term typed with no accents at all can miss a record whose stored form carries one. If your project keeps a native-script field, a transliteration, and a translation side by side, a term in any of them finds the record, provided those fields are searchable. Which fields those are is set by your environment administrator.

Search & Read — browse, filter, and read in place​

When you want to look at many records rather than jump to one, open Search & Read: select Open full Search & Read at the bottom of the quick search panel — your typed term carries over — or choose Search & Read from the command palette (Ctrl+K / ⌘K).

Search & Read is built for dwelling. Switch to Advanced mode to scope the search to one collection and add conditions on field values — each condition names a field, a match type (contains, equals, or starts with), and a value, and can be negated; conditions combine with Match all or with any. Sort results by relevance, by recently modified, or by label, and page through them. For any result you can expand its full fields inline, open it in a reading pane beside the list, or click through to the record itself. If a collection needs a review access you do not hold, the page tells you how many are hidden rather than failing silently.

The page address encodes your query, filters, page, and sort order. Paste the address to a colleague and they see the same results — see Share a search. For a side-by-side comparison of the two search tools, see Choose between the quick search bar and Search & Read.

Filtering the current table​

The table toolbar gives you two ways to narrow what is in front of you:

  • Filter visible rows… — type a term and the table narrows to the loaded rows that contain it, in whichever script the text is written. This box matches text as written in the row; it works on what is already loaded and does not search the rest of the workspace.
  • Filter — opens the filter builder for column conditions. Active filters appear as chips on the toolbar; remove one, or clear them all.

You can combine the two — apply column filters, then type in the filter box — or use either alone. Filter-only browsing, with no typed term, is a good way to survey a slice of your corpus.

Reading a record's Details​

Click a row to open its Details panel. Every field of the record is shown, including fields the table does not display as columns. Relation fields are marked as such; the Relations view shows the record's connections in one place.

Alongside the fields, a Saved reader preview pane renders the record as a reader outside your project would see it — same layout, same fonts, same citations, same media rendering — without making anything public. Read it, spot anything that looks wrong, and fix it in the fields beside it. Public reader covers the full path from draft to published page.

Media and citations on a record​

A record's reading view lists its attached media — images, audio, video, or 3D models, whichever your project attached. Each media item is itself a record, with its own description and its own citation. Where a dedicated viewer exists for a media type, the item opens in that viewer.

Citations are rendered in your project's citation style and are ready to copy into a bibliography — the same structured form reference managers understand. The same applies to source records: opening one shows a citation you can reuse. How citations are composed and how they appear to outside readers is covered in Public reader.

Troubleshooting​

SymptomLikely causeFix
Quick search returns nothing for a record you know exists.Your term appears only in a field your environment does not include in its search configuration, or your term is missing an acute accent the stored form carries.Type the term with its accents, or try a word from a different field of the record. If it still does not appear, contact your environment administrator to review which fields are searchable.
The search panel reports "Search isn't enabled for this workspace yet."Search has not been switched on for your environment.The toolbar filter box still works on the active table. Contact your environment administrator.
A transliterated search misses a record whose native-script form you know is there.The transliteration field is empty on that record, or it is not marked searchable.Try the native-script form. If that succeeds, contact your environment administrator so the transliteration field can be reviewed.
The toolbar filter box does not find a record you expect.The box only narrows rows already loaded in the active table; the record may live in another collection.Use quick search instead — it searches every collection you can access.
Search results show strings of letters and numbers instead of names.The collection has no display field configured, so results fall back to an internal identifier.Contact your environment administrator and ask them to set a display field for that collection.
A citation under a media item looks incomplete or shows placeholders.The citation fields are missing on the media record itself.Open the media record's Details and complete the citation fields. If the field requirements look wrong for your project, contact your environment administrator.
