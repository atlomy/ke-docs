Searching as a public reader

Purpose​

This page explains how search works on the public face of a knowledge environment — the published site that anyone can visit, with no account and no sign-in. Read it when you want to know what a visitor can find, when you are checking your own project's public face, or when search behaves differently in public than it does in your workspace. Searching inside the workspace is a different tool with different controls — that is covered in Searching and reading.

The public site is configurable, so what a reader sees depends on your project. The sections below describe how the platform behaves; your environment may use all of it or a subset.

Features​

  • Public full-text search — a search box on the public site that searches the records your project has published. Available to anyone; no account needed. Maturity: Active. Limit: which fields are searched is set by your project's search configuration.
  • Suggestions as you type — after a few characters, a list of matching records appears beneath the search box; selecting one goes straight to that record. Maturity: Active. Limit: a project decides whether suggestions are on; when they are not, the panel says so instead of staying empty.
  • Result filters — menus on the results page that narrow results by values your project defined, drawn from the current result set. Maturity: Active. Limit: which filters exist — by author or by chronology, for example — is set by your project.
  • Result groupings — tabs that reorganize the same results: a flat list of matching records, or grouped views under headings. Maturity: Active. Limit: which groupings appear and what they are called come from your project's configuration.
  • Explained unavailability — a filter or suggestion source that is not available shows a reason rather than failing silently with empty results. Maturity: Active.
  • Results-page guided tour — a short walkthrough of the results page, shown once automatically and replayable on demand. Maturity: Active.

Usability — how you use it​

The public search bar​

The search box appears on the public site's front page and at the top of most other public pages. Nobody needs to sign in to use it.

It searches what your project has published — the same read-only published layer the rest of the public site reads from — in the fields your project includes in its public search configuration. Unpublished drafts and workspace-only records are not in it.

The box takes a single term or phrase. There are no operator or per-field controls on the public search bar; refinement happens on the results page, with the filter menus. How forgiving the match is — accents, alternate scripts — follows your project's search configuration.

Type a term and press Enter, or select the Search button — it activates once you have typed something. The results page opens.

Suggestions as you type​

Once you have typed at least three characters and pause briefly, a suggestion panel can open beneath the box. Each suggestion shows the record's label, with a secondary line where the record has one. Selecting a suggestion opens that record's page directly, skipping the results page.

The panel always tells you what it is doing:

  • While it is looking, it shows Searching....
  • When nothing matches what you typed, it shows No suggestions.
  • When your project has not switched suggestions on, it shows Suggestions unavailable with a short explanation.

Suggestions never block the search itself. Whatever the panel shows, pressing Enter runs the full search.

When a filter or control is unavailable, the page says so​

The public site is built not to fail silently:

  • A filter menu that is configured but currently unavailable appears disabled, reading unavailable or No … values in place of its choices, and carries the reason — shown as a tooltip, and read aloud by screen readers.
  • If the address you arrive by carries a filter the public site does not offer — an old bookmark, for example — the page shows a Filter unavailable notice naming the filter, instead of a misleading empty result list. Remove that filter, or start a fresh search from the search bar.

The results page​

The results page shows how many records matched, ten results per page, with numbered page buttons at the bottom. The page address carries your search term, so a shared or bookmarked address repeats the search. When nothing matches, the page says No results found.

Each result shows the record's title — a link to its page — a secondary line, a short excerpt ending in Read more, and a handful of labeled field values from the record. Records with attached media list them on the result.

Beside the list, a summary panel previews the first result — its title, description, and key values — with a control in its corner that opens that record in a new browser tab.

Above the results, a row labeled Filters: holds one menu per filter your project defined. Each menu starts at Any … and lists the values present in the current result set; choosing a value narrows the results immediately. When any filter is active, a RESET button appears and clears them all. Which menus exist — by author, by chronology, by some other field — is decided by your project; treat those as examples, not platform universals.

Switching groupings​

A row labeled Sort by: offers one tab per grouping your project configured. The first tab is a flat list of the matching records, named for what your project calls its entries. The other tabs reorganize the same results under headings — one heading per author, per period, or per whatever value the grouping uses.

Different organizations suit different reading tasks: the flat list is best for scanning the strongest matches alongside the summary panel; a grouping by author surveys what each author contributes to the result set; a grouping by chronology reads the results period by period. A record with no value for the current grouping appears under Not Specified.

From a result to a record page​

Selecting a result's title opens that record's page at its own stable address — the same address anyone can cite or share.

The page you land on is the published record: what your project last published, not anyone's working copy. If you are a researcher checking your own project's public face and a recent edit is missing, that edit has not been published yet. To see unpublished work as a reader would, use the preview in your workspace — see Preview an entry before publishing — and Public reader for the full path from draft to published page.

The results-page guided tour​

The first time your browser lands on a results page with results, a short guided tour opens by itself and walks through the filter menus, the grouping tabs, and a result; where results carry related media, the tour points those out too. The browser remembers that you have seen it, so it does not repeat.

To replay it, select the help button on the results page. The button is inactive when there are no results to tour.

Troubleshooting​

SymptomLikely causeFix
No suggestion panel appears while typing.Fewer than three characters typed, or no pause yet — suggestions wait for both.Keep typing, or press Enter to run the full search without suggestions.
The panel says "Suggestions unavailable."Your project has not switched suggestions on for the public site.The full search still works — press Enter or select Search. If suggestions should be on, contact your environment administrator.
A search that should match a record returns nothing.The record is not published, or your term appears only in a field your project does not include in its public search.If it is your project, check the record's publication state in the workspace. Otherwise, try a term from a different part of the record, or contact the project's environment administrator.
A filter menu is grayed out and cannot be opened.That filter is not currently available on the public site, or no values exist for this search.Hover over the menu to read the reason. Narrow the search with the other menus. If the filter should be available, contact your environment administrator.
The page shows "Filter unavailable" instead of results.The address carries a filter the public site does not offer — often an outdated bookmark or shared link.Remove the unavailable filter, or start a fresh search from the search bar and reapply the filters the page offers.
Records pile up under "Not Specified" in a grouped view.Those records have no value for the field the grouping uses.Switch to the flat list grouping. If it is your project's data, fill in the missing field in the workspace.
The guided tour does not open.It opens by itself only on the first visit — your browser remembers it — and it needs at least one result.Run a search that returns results, then select the help button on the results page to replay the tour.
