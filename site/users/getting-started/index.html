Getting started: your first week

Purpose​

You have just joined a knowledge environment. This page is a guided first week — a sequence to follow, not a reference to memorize. By the end you will have signed in, found your bearings, read a few records, made a safe first edit, previewed it as a reader would see it, and learned where to turn next. Every step links to the page that covers it in full; this tutorial puts those pages in order so nothing surprises you.

Work through it once, in order. After that, the Researcher Guide is your map back to any single piece.

Day 1: sign in and look around​

When your environment administrator gives you access, you sign in and land in your workspace. The screen has a stable shape you will see everywhere:

  • a left sidebar listing the tables you can work with — figures, terms, manuscript editions, whatever your project tracks;
  • a top bar with a search control and a command palette;
  • a toolbar of table controls; and
  • view tabs that switch between Table, Details, Relations, and Publish. Media tables also show a Media view.

Click a table in the sidebar. You land in that table's workspace at an address like /app/workspace/<record-type>/, and you can bookmark or share that address. If your project has several record types, each has its own workspace.

Do not try to set anything up yet. Just open two or three tables and notice that they all open into the same shell. The full tour of every control is in the Workspace walkthrough — read it when you are ready, but you do not need it to keep going here.

What you are looking at is your team's shared workspace. Other researchers see the same tables; you are not alone in here.

Find the view your team starts from​

Above the table sit view tabs. A view is a saved arrangement — which filters are on, how the rows are sorted, which columns show, how tall the rows are, and which view kind opens first. Your environment may have set one view as the default: the shared view and view kind the table opens with when you do not follow a link to another view.

For now, stay on whichever view opens first. Look at the columns and ask yourself what each record represents. You are reading the shape of your team's data — what a "record" is here, and what fields describe it. You will arrange your own views later; Workspace walkthrough covers saving and switching them when you get there.

Read before you write​

The instinct on a new project is to start adding records. Resist it for a day. Reading first tells you the conventions your team already follows.

You have two ways to find records:

  1. Quick search, for jumping to one record. Click Search database… in the top bar (or press Ctrl+Shift+F, ⌘⇧F on Mac), type a term, and pick a result. Results group by collection and show which field matched. This is the right tool for "find this person, source, or term, wherever it lives."
  2. Search & Read, for dwelling among many records. From the quick search panel, choose Open full Search & Read — your typed term carries over — or pick Search & Read from the command palette (Ctrl+K / ⌘K). The full page lets you filter, page through results, and open a record in a reading pane beside the list. Its address encodes your whole search, so you can bookmark or share it.

Search matches across the scripts your records carry — original script, transliteration, and translation — for the fields your environment marks searchable. The full behavior, including how diacritics are handled, is in Searching and reading. For now, search for something you expect to exist, open it, and read its fields.

Find help without leaving your work​

You do not have to come back to these pages to get help. The platform carries the same guidance inside the workspace, in two places.

  • A small ? sits beside many controls. Click it and a help drawer opens beside your work, showing the guide for that exact control — so you can read it without losing your place. Inside the drawer, Related guides are one click away, and a breadcrumb with a Back button lets you return to where you started. Press Escape to close it.
  • The Help page at /app/workspace/help lists every guide grouped by topic. Open one to read it in place. When a guide is tied to a control that is always on screen, its Show me where button jumps you to the workspace and highlights the matching ? spot.

When you are stuck mid-task, reach for the nearest ? first; when you want to browse, open the Help page. Find help on a topic covers both.

Your first edits are safe​

Editing here is built so you cannot lose work or publish by accident. Three things are true before you touch anything:

  • A new row is a draft the moment you create it. Click + New row in the toolbar and the row joins your table immediately as a draft. Drafts are visible to your project and never shown to public readers.
  • Every change saves as you finish each field. There is no Save button to forget. Click out of a text field or press Enter, and the value is stored; choices and checkboxes save the moment you pick them. You can leave and come back another day — your draft waits in the table.
  • Undo exists. The toolbar's Undo and Redo step your view changes — filters, sort, columns, density — backward and forward.

Required fields can stay empty while you draft; they only need filling before the row can leave the draft stage and become an active record. Nothing reaches the public reading site until a record is completed and published, and that is a deliberate step you take, never an accident. The full lifecycle — drafts, activation, linking, and what each badge means — is in Adding and editing rows.

Make one small edit to a draft you create yourself and watch it save. If you want to practice Undo, change the view instead — hide a column, change density, or add a harmless filter — then use Undo view change. Record-value edits are fixed by editing the value again, not by the view Undo button.

A first full session, end to end​

Now string the pieces together into one loop you will repeat for the rest of the project:

  1. Search for the area you are working in — a person, a source, a term.
  2. Read a few neighbouring records to learn the conventions.
  3. Edit or add a draft, letting each field save as you finish it.
  4. Preview the record. Open its Details panel: alongside the fields, a reader-preview pane renders the record exactly as a reader outside your project would see it — same layout, same fonts, same citations — without publishing anything. Read it, spot anything wrong, and fix it in the fields beside it.

That loop — search, read, edit, preview — is the daily rhythm of the workspace. Searching and reading covers the search and preview ends of it; Adding and editing rows covers the editing end.
