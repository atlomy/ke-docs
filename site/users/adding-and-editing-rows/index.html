Adding and Editing Rows

Audience: Researcher Last aligned with platform state: 2026-05-25 Platform version: V2 (pre-1.0)

Purpose​

This page covers creating new records and editing existing ones in your workspace. The model is: you start a draft, the system autosaves as you go, you can leave and come back, and you decide when the draft is ready to become a real record. You do not lose work, you do not save accidentally, and you do not have to fill everything in at once. Read this when you create your first record, when you return to an abandoned draft, or when an edit is not behaving as you expect.

Features​

  • New row drawer (local mode → autosave → activate) — slides in from the right with a blank form; lives client-side until the first required field is filled and blurred. Substrate: spa/src/apps/tenant_admin/workspace/NewRowDrawer.tsx, spa/src/apps/tenant_admin/workspace/NewRowFsmTypes.ts, Spec 0025 (docs/superpowers/specs/0025-workspace-ui-backend-contracts/). Maturity: Active. Limit: only one drawer is open at a time per workspace.
  • Autosave-on-blur — once a required field is filled and the focus leaves it, the draft is persisted; every subsequent field also autosaves on blur. Substrate: Spec 0025, draft lifecycle contracts (commit 99926f56). Maturity: Active. Limit: no manual Save button; relies on browser focus events.
  • Resume abandoned drafts — drafts you closed remain on the server and reappear via a resume affordance in the toolbar / banner. Substrate: Spec 0025 §"resume abandoned workspace drafts" (commit 86848413). Maturity: Active. Limit: multiple drafts per workspace are supported, each with its own resume entry.
  • Discard with cleanup — discarding a draft removes the local state and any server-side autosaved fields. Substrate: spa/src/apps/tenant_admin/workspace/NewRowDiscardModal.tsx, draft discard contract (commits a03271c2, f10f0f23 idempotency fix). Maturity: Active. Limit: discard is idempotent — calling it twice does no harm.
  • Activate — promotes the draft to a real record visible in the table, in search results, and (per the project's workflow) eligible for the public reader. Substrate: Spec 0025. Maturity: Active. Limit: activation may enter a review queue rather than going straight live, depending on the project's workflow.
  • Inline edit on existing records — click a field in the Details panel, change it, blur to save. Same autosave-on-blur semantics as drafts. Substrate: spa/src/apps/tenant_admin/workspace/WorkspaceDetailsView.tsx, a11y commit cda2f4cd. Maturity: Active. Limit: some fields are intentionally non-inline (media attachment, complex relations).
  • Draft relation gating — relation fields (links to other records) are gated while the record is still a draft. Substrate: Spec 0027 (docs/superpowers/specs/0027-workspace-draft-relation-dependencies/), PR #411. Maturity: Active (gated). Limit: gating intentional until the draft-relation capability ships per Spec 0027; the form shows the gate state inline.
  • Relation picker for activated records — once activated, relation fields are filled by searching the related record type from inside the field. Substrate: spa/src/apps/tenant_admin/workspace/NewRowRelationPicker.tsx. Maturity: Active. Limit: searches the related record type's marked-searchable fields only.

Usability — how you use it​

Creating a new record​

  1. Open the New row drawer. In any workspace, click + New row in the toolbar. The drawer slides in with a blank form. You are now in local mode — nothing has been saved to the server yet. You can close, navigate away, or refresh and the system will not have created anything.
  2. Fill in a required field. Each record type has one or more required fields (a "name" for a person, a "title" for a manuscript). As soon as you fill in a required field and click out of it (a "blur" — meaning the focus leaves that field), the draft autosaves to the server. From this moment on, your work is persisted. You see a small indicator confirming the save. The draft is real, but it is still a draft — not in the main table, not in search results.
  3. Keep filling in fields. Every field you change autosaves on blur. You can Tab between fields, leave fields blank and come back, close the drawer and come back to the draft later.
  4. Activate the draft. When ready, click Activate. The draft is promoted to a record. Depending on your role and the project's workflow, it may go straight live or enter a review queue.
  5. Or discard. Click Discard. The draft and any autosaved fields are removed.

Coming back to an abandoned draft​

You do not have to finish a draft in one sitting. When you next open the workspace, you see a hint that you have an unactivated draft — typically a banner or a chip in the toolbar. Click it to resume. The drawer reopens with your fields exactly as you left them. You can have more than one draft in progress; the resume affordance shows each. To abandon a draft permanently, discard it.

Editing an existing record​

  1. Open the record's Details panel by clicking its row in the table.
  2. Click into a field. If you have edit permissions, it becomes editable.
  3. Change the value, click outside (or press Tab). Autosave fires on blur, same as drafts.
  4. A small indicator confirms the save.

Some fields are intentionally non-inline — typically media attachment or complex relation editing. The Details panel guides you to the right tool when this is the case.

Relations on drafts versus activated records​

For an activated record, you fill in a relation by searching the related record type from inside the field — type, pick a suggestion, blur to save.

For a draft, the relation field is currently gated: it renders a hint that says it will become editable once the record is activated (or once the draft-relation capability ships per Spec 0027). Finish the draft's text and number fields, activate, and add relations on the resulting record. The gate hints are keyboard-focusable and announced by screen readers — see Accessibility.

A note on permissions​

What you can create, edit, activate, or discard depends on your role in the project. Common patterns: read-only researchers search, browse, view Details, but cannot add or edit; contributing researchers add records as drafts and edit their own work; editors / reviewers can edit any researcher's drafts and activate them; tenant administrators can do all of the above plus configure the environment. Your role may be a custom mix — the buttons and fields you see reflect it.
