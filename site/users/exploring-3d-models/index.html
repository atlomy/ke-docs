Exploring 3D models

Purpose​

Some environments pair their records with interactive 3D models — an anatomical structure, a scanned artifact, a molecular form. The model viewer lets you and your readers turn a model, name its parts, and open the scholarly entry behind each part. This page walks through opening a model, reading what a part means, shaping the view so you see what matters, working through a model group by group, and what to expect from attachments that have no built-in viewer.

In your workspace — the Media view​

When a table holds media, a Media view joins Table / Details / Relations / Publish in the view bar. It appears only on media tables. The Media view shows your media records as a gallery of cards — each card carries the record's title and a media-type badge (for example 3D model). Opening a 3D-model card renders the model right there in a detail pane, where you can orbit and zoom to inspect it, alongside a rail of tabs for the model's parts, files, viewer framing, info, and versions. Maturity: Active. From the Mesh→term tab you review which part of the model is matched to which term and approve, reassign, or strip those matches; environment admins use the Viewer tab to set the framing the model opens at. Two researcher how-to guides cover this in the workspace: See which mesh maps to which term and Tune the 3D camera view. When a record has no file yet, its detail pane shows Attach a file first; editors and admins use Attach media there to reference a URL or upload a file — see Work with media in the workspace. The reader experience below — the public viewer your readers see — is described separately in this page.

Features​

  • Click a part, read its entry — hovering over the model names the part under your pointer; clicking it opens a panel with the entry's headword, translations, transliteration, description, and a link to the full entry. Maturity: Active. Limit: a part shows entry details only where your project has linked that part to an entry.
  • View shaping, part by part — fade, hide, isolate, or zoom to any part. Every shaping action raises an on-screen bar with its own undo, and one control resets the whole view. Maturity: Active.
  • Group-by-group exploration — a strip labeled LAYERS lists the model's groups; each group opens a list of its parts with individual show/hide toggles. Maturity: Active.
  • View controls — on-screen orbit, zoom, and pan controls plus a reset; scroll-wheel zoom and middle-button pan with the mouse; touch gestures on phones and tablets. Maturity: Active.
  • Built-in walkthrough — a short guided tour starts on its own the first time you open a model in a browser, and can be rerun from the Help button. Maturity: Active.
  • Typed attachment cards — a record page lists its attachments as labeled cards (3D model, Image, Audio, Video, Molecular). A 3D model card opens this viewer; cards of other types open only when the attachment carries its own viewing address. Maturity: Active. Limit: the built-in viewer exists for 3D models today; other media types display as cards and rely on the address the attachment provides.

Usability — how you use it​

Opening a model​

  1. Open a record that has a 3D model attached. Its attachments appear as a strip of cards, each labeled with its type.
  2. Select the card labeled 3D model. The viewer opens with the model's name in the top bar.
  3. To leave, use the back arrow beside the model's name — it returns you to the page you came from, or to the reading site's opening page if you arrived by direct link.

Your environment's reading site may also feature its models directly — for example on its opening page — and selecting a model there opens the same viewer.

Beside the model's name sits Model Info. If the model carries an overview, it unfolds in place, with a Read Full Model Info link to the model's full information page; if there is no overview, the button takes you straight to that page. The overview folds away when you select a part. If the model includes an animation, a play/pause control appears near the top of the view.

Reading what a part means​

  • Hover over the model (on a computer): the part under your pointer is highlighted and its name appears beside it.
  • Click a part: a detail panel opens. The heading is the entry's headword, with its translations in parentheses; the transliteration sits underneath; below come the description and the part's place in the model's structure, from the largest group down to the part itself.
  • Full Details, at the bottom of the panel, opens the part's full entry page in a new tab.
  • Click empty space to close the panel and clear the selection, or use the panel's close control.

When a part has not been linked to an entry, the panel falls back to the part's name as written in the model, and a notice reports that no entry details were found for it. Linking parts to entries is part of how your project prepares its models — if a part you need is unlinked, tell your environment administrator.

Shaping the view: fade, hide, isolate, zoom​

Right-click a part (on a computer) to open a circular menu around your pointer. On a touch screen, tap the part: a small card names it, offers See Details, and unfolds the same actions from its menu control. The menu offers four ways to shape the view, each marked with its keyboard letter:

  • Fade (F) — the part turns translucent, so you can see past it while keeping it in place.
  • Hide (H) — the part is removed from view.
  • Isolate (I) — the part is shown on its own.
  • Zoom (Z) — the camera moves in on the part.

Each state raises a bar at the edge of the model area naming the affected part:

  • A faded part's bar reads Part Faded, with Invert — the faded part returns to full strength and everything else fades instead — and Reset. With several parts faded, the bar shows their count and their names, with Invert All and Reset All.
  • A hidden part's bar reads Part Hidden, with Unhide. With several hidden, the bar shows the count and a list; picking a part from the list brings it back, and Unhide All brings back everything.
  • An isolated part's bar reads Isolated, with Exit.
  • A zoomed part's bar reads Zoom, with Exit.

To start over entirely, use the round Reset the view control among the view controls, or press Alt+R.

The circular menu also carries the part's name — selecting it opens the detail panel — and Cite, which opens the page's citation form.

Moving the camera​

On a computer, the pointer is reserved for parts — hovering names them, clicking selects them — so moving the view goes through dedicated controls:

  • The round controls at the edge of the model area: Orbit the view (two turn buttons; press and hold to keep turning), Zoom in/out in the view (press and hold to keep zooming), Pan the view (four direction arrows), and Reset the view.
  • The mouse: scroll to zoom; drag with the middle button to pan.

On a touch screen, drag with one finger to turn the model, pinch with two fingers to zoom, and drag with two fingers to pan.

Working through the model in groups​

The strip along the bottom of the screen, labeled LAYERS, lists the model's groups. Select a group: its parts light up and a list opens showing every part in the group, each with an eye toggle.

  • Toggle a part's eye to show or hide that part.
  • Hide all / Show all at the top of the list switches the whole group at once.

Moving the pointer away from the strip clears the group highlight. This is the steady way through a dense model: open a group, hide what is in the way, study what remains, then move to the next group.

The built-in walkthrough​

The first time you open a model in a given browser, a short walkthrough starts on its own once the model has loaded. It tours navigation, part selection, the view-shaping menu, the groups strip, the model information, and the page's cite, help, and feedback buttons.

To run it again, use the round Help button at the edge of the page. The walkthrough is remembered per browser, not per account — on a new device or browser it introduces itself again. The neighboring round buttons offer Cite — a citation for the page, formatted in common styles (APA, MLA, Chicago) and ready to copy — and Feedback, which opens a form for sending your impressions to the team behind the site.

Keyboard interaction, honestly​

With a part selected, four shortcuts act on it: F fades, H hides, I isolates, Z zooms. Alt+R resets the view at any time.

Two honest limits:

  • Selecting a part requires a pointer — a mouse click or a tap. There is no keyboard-only way to move through the model's parts, so the shortcuts extend pointer work rather than replace it.
  • The model area itself is not part of the keyboard tab order.

If a keyboard-only path matters to your work, tell your environment administrator — an accessibility gap is a bug to report, not a setting to find.

The model view follows the background color chosen in the accessibility settings on your environment's reading pages: a dark background there gives the model a dark backdrop here.

Attachments without a built-in viewer​

A record's attachments appear as cards in a strip, each labeled with its type — 3D model, Image, Audio, Video, Molecular, or the type name your project recorded. A card shows the attachment's image where one is catalogued (a type placeholder otherwise), its title, and its creator where catalogued.

  • A 3D model card opens this viewer.
  • A card of any other type opens only when the attachment carries its own viewing address; selecting it then leads to that address.
  • A card with no viewing address is a labeled reference: it tells you the attachment exists and what it is, without leading anywhere.

Troubleshooting​

SymptomLikely causeFix
Clicking a part shows its name, but a notice says no entry details were found.The part is not yet linked to an entry.The rest of the model still works. Ask your environment administrator to link the part if you need its entry.
Parts of the model seem to be missing.Parts are hidden, or one part is isolated.Check the bars at the edge of the model area — Unhide All or Exit — or reset the view with the Reset the view control or Alt+R.
Dragging with the mouse does not turn the model.On a computer, the pointer selects parts; turning the model goes through the view controls.Use the Orbit the view control; scroll to zoom; drag with the middle button to pan. On a touch screen, a one-finger drag turns the model.
The keyboard shortcuts (F, H, I, Z) do nothing.No part is selected — the shortcuts act on the selected part.Click a part first, then press the shortcut.
The walkthrough did not appear.It starts on its own only on your first visit in a given browser, and only once the model has loaded.Use the Help button at the edge of the page after the model finishes loading.
An Image, Audio, or Video card on a record page does not open anything.The attachment carries no viewing address of its own; only 3D model cards have a built-in viewer.The card still documents the attachment. If it should open somewhere, ask your environment administrator to add a viewing address to the attachment.
The model never finishes loading.A large model file, or a connection problem.Wait — larger models take longer — then reload the page. If the problem persists, contact your environment administrator.
