Public Reader

Audience: Researcher Last aligned with platform state: 2026-06-12 Platform version: V2 (pre-1.0)

Purpose​

When somebody outside your project reads one of your records on the web, they are using the public reader — the reading-facing face of your knowledge environment, with stable URLs, scholarly citations, clean typography, and no editing controls. This page explains what the reader is, how a row gets there, and how to preview a row before it is actually public. Read it when you want to know what a reader sees, when you are about to publish, or when you want to share a finished record with someone outside the project.

The reader is configurable, so what your readers see depends on your project. The mechanics below describe how it works; your environment may use all of it or a subset.

Features​

  • Projection-first published entry layer — the reader reads from a separate read-only snapshot of your data, refreshed when your project publishes something new. Substrate: ADR-029 (docs/developer/architecture/adr/029-projection-first-published-entry-layer.md). Maturity: Active. Limit: refresh is automatic on publishable change; manual refresh is operator-only.
  • Stable, citable URLs — every published row has a stable URL that preserves citation continuity even if sections are renamed or the page is restructured. Substrate: ADR-029, Spec 0022 (docs/superpowers/specs/0022-atlomy-atlas-public-reader-parity/). Maturity: Active. Limit: stability is per-row identity, not per-section; section anchors are best-effort.
  • SectionNav (side TOC) — a side navigation table of contents down the page; highlights where the reader is as they scroll. Substrate: Spec 0022 reader-parity work (commit 8c3cce3c). Maturity: Active. Limit: section granularity depends on the row's markup.
  • Real-renderer draft preview — in-workspace preview that renders exactly what a reader would see, with no mockup substitution. Substrate: Spec 0021 (docs/superpowers/specs/0021-public-reader-preview-real-rendering/), draft reader preview (commits 4c5ce186, 0677fcbe). Maturity: Active. Limit: preview is in-workspace; not a public URL.
  • CSL-format media citations — under any media item, a structured citation in CSL — copy directly into a bibliography, or export to BibTeX / RIS where the tenant enabled it. Substrate: Spec 0008 (docs/superpowers/specs/0008-citation-framework-csl/). Maturity: Active. Limit: BibTeX / RIS export is per-tenant opt-in.
  • Anchor-resolved provenance citations — citations on the row itself, built by tracing where each piece came from (source manuscript, prior publication, contributor) and rendering that chain. Substrate: Spec 0008 anchor-resolution work (commits 9c3659b9, 83c2b93a). Maturity: Active. Limit: fidelity depends on the tenant's import declaring source links.
  • References section — one row per piece of evidence — every occurrence of the record in the sources gets its own row. Where the project has catalogued a modern and an early edition of the same source, the row bridges them into one scholarly reference (modern citation, then the early edition's locator and abbreviated editor); where the project has full citation styling configured, the row carries a formatted citation; and an occurrence with no published edition at all — a catalogued manuscript or fragment — still appears, shown by its own label. Quotes catalogued on an occurrence display beneath its row, and source citations the rows did not absorb appear once each, deduplicated, below the evidence list. Substrate: ADR-061 reference rendering contract, Spec 0008. Maturity: Active. Limit: how rich each row reads depends on what the project catalogued — locators, editions, quotes, and abbreviations are project data.
  • Browsable indexes, switchable per project — beside search, readers can browse the whole corpus from an alphabetised index, and a project can publish more than one (for a multilingual corpus, e.g. by transliteration or by original script) switchable from the reader. Index rows are rich (transliteration + original-script form + a short gloss together); a character rail jumps to a letter bucket; large prefixes page responsively. Substrate: Spec 0022 (public-reader parity), Spec 0009 (index controls). Maturity: Active. Limit: which indexes exist, the default, and what each row shows are project-configured.
  • Media carousel with type variants — flip between 2D images, audio, video, 3D models, molecular visualisations attached to the same anchored point. Substrate: Spec 0016 (docs/superpowers/specs/0016-multi-media-expansion/), PRs #330 (variants), #334 (type metadata), #335 (3D viewer config). Maturity: Active. Limit: only variants the tenant attached appear.
  • Dark mode and reading-first layout — selectable text, scholarly typeface, reader-side dark-mode toggle (remembered for the session). Substrate: Spec 0022 reader parity. Maturity: Active. Limit: dark-mode preference is browser-scoped, not account-scoped (readers are not signed in).

Usability — how you use it​

What the reader is, in one paragraph​

The reader is a separate layer from your workspace. The workspace is where you and your colleagues add, edit, review, and approve. The reader is where the world reads what your project has decided to publish. The platform builds the reader from a published projection — a separate read-only snapshot of your data, refreshed when your project publishes something new. A reader visiting a page does not reach into your workspace; they read from the snapshot. Your editing speed is not affected by outside traffic, and a reader cannot accidentally see a draft.

Browsing the corpus by index​

Readers do not have to search to explore. The reader offers a browsable index — an alphabetised list of every published entry — and a project can publish more than one. A multilingual environment commonly offers the same entries by transliteration and by original script; the reader switches between them from the controls at the top of the list. Each index row can show the transliterated form, the original-script form, and a short gloss together, so an entry is recognisable without opening it, and a character rail jumps straight to a letter. Which indexes exist and which is the default are set by your project when it publishes. (Researcher help: "Browse entries by index".)

How a row gets to the reader​

  1. Draft. You created it; the workspace knows about it; nobody else sees it (unless you opt into preview, below).
  2. Activated. You clicked Activate. The row enters the main table and search results; other researchers can see, link, and cite it. Depending on the project's workflow, this may also make the row reader-visible — or it may not.
  3. Approved for publication. Some projects insert a separate review step. An editor opens the row, examines the staged preview, and clicks Approve. Only then does the row reach the public projection.

Your project's workflow tells the platform which step controls publication. The projection refreshes automatically when something publishable changes — a new approval, a media item added to a published row, a typo fix to a published citation. You do not trigger the refresh manually.

Previewing before publication​

Use it to see exactly what a reader will see, before committing:

  1. Open the row in the Details panel (a draft, an activated row, or anything in your workspace).
  2. Click Preview in reader. A side panel renders the row exactly as a reader would see it — same layout, same fonts, same citations, same media rendering.
  3. Read it. Spot anything awkward.
  4. Close the panel. The row has not moved; you have only previewed it.

The preview is a real rendering, not a mockup. If a citation will look ugly in the reader, it looks ugly in the preview too — that is the point. A few tenants opt into a richer preview that lets editors see staged-but-unpublished drafts with a "draft" banner across the top, for validating the final reader output before approval.

What a reader can do, and what they cannot​

A reader visiting a published page can:

  • Read and select text — lines are individually selectable for quoting and note-taking.
  • Deep-link to a section, citation, or media item through the projection's stable anchors.
  • Copy citations in CSL format, BibTeX, or RIS — depending on the tenant configuration.
  • Flip through media variants in the carousel.
  • Toggle dark mode (remembered for the reader's session).

A reader cannot edit, comment, or add anything. The reader is read-only by design. If the project wants reader feedback, that is a separate surface the administrator configures; it does not change the projection.

Differences between draft preview and published​

Draft previewPublished
Who can see itYou (and anyone you share the workspace with)Anyone with the public URL
URLNot a public URL — preview-onlyStable, citable URL
"Draft" bannerVisible across the top (when richer preview is enabled)Not present
Indexing by search enginesNoYes
Citation exportWorks, but cites the preview URLWorks and cites the stable public URL
Reflects editsImmediately — re-open the preview after a changeOnly after publication / projection refresh

In short: the preview lets you see the reader's view without committing to it. The published view is what the world actually sees.

Troubleshooting​

SymptomLikely causeFixWhere to escalate
A row was activated but does not appear in the reader.The project's workflow has a separate approval step between activation and publication.Confirm the row is in the editor's review queue; an editor needs to approve.Tenant admin; ADR-029.
Preview in reader shows different formatting than the table view.The reader renders from the published projection's layout, not from the editing surface. Some fields render differently.This is intended — the preview is the truthful render. Adjust the row to match the intended reader layout.Spec 0022 if the difference seems like a bug.
A citation appears under media but with placeholder values.The media record's CSL fields are missing or incomplete.Open the media record and complete its CSL metadata.Tenant admin; Spec 0008.
The carousel shows fewer variants in the reader than in the workspace.The tenant has not promoted some variants to publication-eligible, or some are still draft.Confirm in the workspace that the variants are activated/approved per workflow.Tenant admin; Spec 0016.
You changed a published row but the change has not surfaced in the reader.The projection refresh is on its automatic cadence; small changes are not instant.Wait a short interval; re-check. If the change is overdue, ask the operator to verify projection health.Super admin; ADR-029.
A reader reports a broken section deep-link they previously cited.The row was restructured; the projection preserves row-level URL identity but per-section anchors are best-effort.Add a redirect or update the section anchor; advise the citer of the new anchor.Tenant admin; Spec 0022.
Preview in reader is blank or shows a loading state forever.The projection has not yet built the staged preview for this row, or the preview surface is failing to fetch.Refresh; try again. If persistent, file a bug.Platform team; Spec 0021.
