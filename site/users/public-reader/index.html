Public reader

Purpose​

When somebody outside your project reads one of your records on the web, they are using the public reader — the reading-facing face of your knowledge environment, with stable URLs, scholarly citations, clean typography, and no editing controls. This page explains what the reader is, how a record reaches it, and how to preview a record before it is public. Read it when you want to know what a reader sees, when you are about to publish, or when you want to share a finished record with someone outside the project.

The reader is configurable, so what your readers see depends on your project. The sections below describe how it works; your environment may use all of it or a subset.

Features​

  • Published reading layer — the reader reads from a separate, read-only snapshot of your data, refreshed when your project publishes something new. Readers never touch your working data. Maturity: Active.
  • Stable, citable URLs — every published record has a stable web address that keeps citations valid even if the page is later reorganized. Maturity: Active. Limit: stability is per record; links to individual sections within a page are best-effort.
  • Side table of contents — a navigation list down the side of the page that highlights where the reader is as they scroll. Maturity: Active. Limit: how fine-grained it is depends on how the record is sectioned.
  • True-to-life preview — an in-workspace preview that renders a record exactly as a reader would see it, with no mockup substitution. Maturity: Active. Limit: the preview lives in your workspace; it is not a public address.
  • Citations for media items — a media item can carry a structured citation in your project's citation style (defined in Citation Style Language, the same format reference managers use), rendered with the record's citations and ready to copy into a bibliography. Maturity: Active.
  • Source-traced citations — citations on the record itself, built by tracing where each piece came from (source manuscript, prior publication, contributor) and rendering that chain. Maturity: Active. Limit: fidelity depends on the source links your project declared at import.
  • References — one row per piece of evidence — every occurrence of the record in the sources gets its own row. Where the project has catalogued a modern and an early edition of the same source, the row bridges them into one scholarly reference (modern citation, then the early edition's locator and abbreviated editor); where the project has full citation styling configured, the row carries a formatted citation; and an occurrence with no published edition at all — a catalogued manuscript or fragment — still appears, shown by its own label. Quotes catalogued on an occurrence display beneath its row, and source citations the rows did not absorb appear once each, deduplicated, below the evidence list. Maturity: Active. Limit: how rich each row reads depends on what the project catalogued — locators, editions, quotes, and abbreviations are project data.
  • Browsable indexes, switchable per project — beside search, readers can browse the whole corpus from an alphabetized index, and a project can publish more than one (for a multilingual corpus, for example by transliteration or by original script), switchable from the reader. Index rows are rich (transliteration, original-script form, and a short gloss together); a character rail jumps to a letter; large letter groups page smoothly. Maturity: Active. Limit: which indexes exist, the default, and what each row shows are configured by your project.
  • Media on the record page — a published record lists its attached media — 2D images, audio, video, 3D models, and molecular visualizations — each item with its label and, where catalogued, a link to its source plus its identifier and version. Maturity: Active. Limit: only the media your project attached and published appears.
  • Dark mode and reading-first layout — selectable text, a scholarly typeface, and a darker color scheme readers can switch to, remembered in their browser. Maturity: Active.

Usability — how you use it​

What the reader is, in one paragraph​

The reader is a separate layer from your workspace. The workspace is where you and your colleagues add, edit, review, and approve. The reader is where the world reads what your project has decided to publish. The platform builds the reader from a published snapshot — a separate, read-only copy of your data, refreshed when your project publishes something new. A reader visiting a page does not reach into your workspace; they read from the snapshot. Your editing speed is not affected by outside traffic, and a reader cannot accidentally see a draft.

Browsing the corpus by index​

Readers do not have to search to explore. The reader offers a browsable index — an alphabetized list of every published entry — and a project can publish more than one. A multilingual environment commonly offers the same entries by transliteration and by original script; the reader switches between them from the controls at the top of the list. Each index row can show the transliterated form, the original-script form, and a short gloss together, so an entry is recognizable without opening it, and a character rail jumps straight to a letter. Which indexes exist and which is the default are set by your project when it publishes. See Browse entries by index.

How a record reaches the reader​

  1. Draft. You created it; the workspace knows about it; nobody else sees it (unless you preview it, below).
  2. Activated. You selected Activate. The record enters the main table and search results; other researchers can see, link, and cite it. The record is not yet public — activation never publishes by itself.
  3. Published. You — or a colleague with publish access — select Publish, for a single record or a whole table at once. The platform builds the record's published snapshot, and the public page goes live.

Publication is its own step, separate from activation. Once a record is published, its snapshot refreshes automatically when something publishable changes — an edit to a published field, a media item added to a published record, a typo fix to a published citation. You do not trigger the refresh manually.

Previewing before publication​

Use the preview to see exactly what a reader will see, before committing:

  1. Open the record in your workspace — a draft, an activated record, or anything you are working on.
  2. Open its reader preview. The preview renders the record exactly as a reader would see it — same layout, same fonts, same citations, same media rendering. During an import, the wizard's final step offers the same preview for newly imported records.
  3. Read it. Spot anything awkward.
  4. Close the preview. The record has not moved; you have only looked at it.

The preview is a real rendering, not a mockup. If a citation will look rough in the reader, it looks rough in the preview too — that is the point. See Preview an entry before publishing.

The preview reflects the current draft. The published page updates on the next publish, not instantly.

What a reader can do, and what they cannot​

A reader visiting a published page can:

  • Read and select text — lines are individually selectable for quoting and note-taking.
  • Link to a published record through its stable web address; links to individual sections within a page are best-effort.
  • Copy citations formatted in your project's citation style.
  • Follow media to its source — each attached media item appears with its label and, where catalogued, a link to its source.
  • Switch to dark mode (remembered in their browser).

A reader cannot edit, comment on, or add anything. The reader is read-only by design.

Differences between draft preview and published​

Draft previewPublished
Who can see itYou and colleagues with workspace accessAnyone with the public URL
URLNot a public URL — preview-onlyStable, citable URL
Visible on the public webNoYes
Reflects editsOn re-opening the preview after a changeOnly after the published snapshot next refreshes

In short: the preview lets you see the reader's view without committing to it. The published view is what the world actually sees.

Troubleshooting​

SymptomLikely causeFix
A record was activated but does not appear in the reader.Activation and publication are separate steps; the record has not been published yet.Publish the record, or ask a colleague with publish access to publish it. If you are unsure who publishes in your environment, contact your environment administrator.
The preview shows different formatting than the table view.The reader renders from the published layout, not from the editing surface. Some fields render differently.This is intended — the preview is the truthful render. Adjust the record to match the reader layout you want.
A citation for a media item shows placeholder or incomplete values.The media record's citation fields are missing or incomplete.Open the media record and complete its citation metadata. If the citation style itself looks wrong, contact your environment administrator.
A media item attached in the workspace does not appear on the published page.The published snapshot has not refreshed since the item was attached, or the item is not part of what your project publishes.Wait a short interval and re-check. If the item is still missing, contact your environment administrator.
You changed a published record but the change has not surfaced in the reader.The published snapshot refreshes automatically, but small changes are not instant.Wait a short interval and re-check. If the change is overdue, contact your environment administrator.
A reader reports a broken section link they previously cited.The record was restructured; the record's own URL stays stable, but links to individual sections are best-effort.Share the record's stable URL and advise the citer of the new section location. If section links break often, contact your environment administrator.
The preview is blank or shows a loading state that never finishes.The preview has not yet been built for this record, or it is failing to load.Refresh and retry. If the problem persists, contact your environment administrator.
