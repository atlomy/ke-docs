Find a record when search returns nothing

When you search for a record you know is there and nothing comes back, one of four things is usually happening. Each has a different fix.

1. The record's field is not included in search​

Search matches against the fields your environment includes in its search configuration. If the word you typed appears only in a field that is outside that set, the record will not surface — even though it exists and you can see it by scrolling the table.

What to try: type a word from a different field of the record — the label, a short description, or a name you know it carries. If that finds it, the original field is not in the search configuration.

If no combination of words finds the record, contact your environment administrator and ask which fields are included in search.

2. Accents and diacritics — what the search ignores and what it does not​

Search is forgiving about some diacritics and strict about others.

Ignored (you can leave these off when searching):

  • Breathings in polytonic Greek (smooth and rough)
  • Iota subscripts
  • Circumflex and grave accents
  • Arabic vowel marks (short vowels / harakat)

Not ignored (these still count):

  • The acute accent — in Greek and in any Latin-script language
  • The diaeresis (umlaut)

This means: typing a word with no accent at all can miss a record whose stored form carries an acute accent. If you are searching a polytonic-Greek collection and your term has an acute accent, include it. If you are not sure of the accent, try a word from another field of the same record instead.

Diacritic handling is part of your environment's search configuration, and individual fields can be set to match every mark exactly. If you have confirmed the accent is correct and search still misses the record, contact your environment administrator.

3. The transliteration field is empty or not searchable​

If your environment stores records with a native-script field, a transliteration, and a translation side by side, a search term in any of those fields can find the record — but only if those fields are searchable and have values.

If you search with a transliterated form and nothing comes back:

  1. Open the record directly from the table.
  2. Check whether the transliteration field has a value.
  3. If the field is empty, add the transliteration — or ask your environment administrator whether the transliteration field is included in search.

If the transliteration field has a value but search still misses the record, the field may not be in the search configuration. Contact your environment administrator.

4. Results show a placeholder instead of the record's name​

When a collection has no display field configured, search results for that collection cannot show a record name. Each result shows a placeholder instead — "(unlabeled …)", or the collection's name with a "no display field" notice. The records are there and your search did find them; the problem is that you cannot recognize them.

If you see this:

  1. Look for the type badge beside each result — it names the collection the result belongs to.
  2. Contact your environment administrator and ask them to set a display field for that collection. Once set, search results for that collection will show the record's name as the label.
