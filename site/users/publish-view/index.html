The Publish view: field visibility and publishing changes

Purpose​

The Publish view is the workspace tab where your table meets its public audience. Here you decide, field by field, which parts of your records appear on published pages, check the result in a reader preview, and then publish the whole table in one pass. Read this when you are preparing a collection for first publication, when you want to keep a working field off the public page, or when edits to published records are waiting to go live.

Publishing here is a whole-table action: one run builds or refreshes the public page of every ready record in the table. There is no separate publish button per record — a single new record reaches the public the same way, through a table run that refreshes what is already published and picks up what is new. How a record becomes ready in the first place — draft, activated, published — is covered in Publish a record — and why activating is not publishing.

Features​

  • Per-field visibility — every field in the table is set to Public, Workspace, or Private. The choice saves to the field's configuration the moment you make it and applies to every record in the table. Active.
  • Public fields only on published pages — when a record is published, only its Public fields are included. Fields set to Workspace or Private are left out of the public page. Active.
  • Reader preview with field highlighting — a sample of the table's records, each rendered exactly as a public reader would see it, with two-way highlighting between the field list and the preview. Active.
  • Whole-table publishing with a progress report — one action publishes every ready record, shows progress as it runs, and ends with a receipt: how many records were published, skipped, or failed. Active.
  • Whole-table visibility selector — the Whole table control at the top of the panel is a planning aid only; the control itself says the choice is not saved. Deferred.
  • Preview visitor state — shown in the view but not yet available. Deferred.

Usability — how you use it​

Opening the Publish view​

Open your collection's table in the workspace and select Publish from the view tabs (alongside Table, Details, and Relations). You can also get here from a column's header menu — Open Publish view... — or by right-clicking a row and choosing Review publish field.

The view has two halves: a panel on the left with the field visibility list, the preview row picker, and the publish action; and a reader preview on the right showing the selected record as a public reader would see it.

Setting a field's visibility​

The Fields list shows every field in the table, in display order, with its label, its internal name, its field type, and a tag for the part of the published page where it appears (or not on reader if it does not render there). Each field has a three-way choice:

  • Public — the field appears on the record's published page.
  • Workspace — the field stays inside your project's workspace. Published pages never include it.
  • Private — the field is marked for its owner only. Like Workspace fields, it never appears on a published page.

Fields are Public unless someone restricts them, so a table publishes whole until you decide otherwise.

Three things to know about changing a field's visibility:

  1. It saves immediately. There is no separate save step — pick a setting and it is stored in the field's configuration. The counts in the panel header (public, workspace, and private fields) update as you go.
  2. It is a table-wide decision. Visibility belongs to the field, not to a row. Setting a field to Workspace removes it from the published page of every record in the table.
  3. Published pages catch up at the next publish. The setting is saved at once, but a page that is already public keeps showing its last published version. Run Publish changes to apply the current visibility to every published page in the table.

If a visibility change is declined — most often because your role does not include field configuration access — the control returns to its previous setting rather than showing a saved state that did not happen.

The Whole table selector above the field list is a planning aid for thinking about the table's overall visibility. Its own label tells you the choice is not saved — field-level settings are the ones that count.

Previewing with the row picker​

The row picker lists a sample of the table's records, in default record order. A note under the list tells you exactly how many of the table's records you are looking at — open the Table view to browse all records. The sample only bounds the preview; publishing always covers the whole table.

Pick a record and the preview beside the panel shows it as a public reader would see it. The preview is produced by the same renderer as the public entry page, showing the record as currently saved. Two things connect the preview to the field list:

  • Point at a field in the list and the part of the preview where it renders is highlighted and scrolled into view. Point at a part of the preview and the matching field row lights up — useful for answering "which field is this on the page?" before restricting it.
  • Rest the pointer on the preview's content and a hint names the source column that filled that part of the page.

If the preview area reports that no public reader rendering is available for a record yet, the record cannot render a public page in its current state — most often it is still a draft.

Publishing the table​

Select Publish changes to queue the run. The button needs a workspace table selected, and it works at whole-record granularity: each ready record's public page is built or refreshed in full, applying the current field visibility.

While the run is in progress, the view reports how many records have been published so far, and how many were skipped or failed. When it finishes, the receipt gives the final counts:

  • Published — records whose public page was built or refreshed.
  • Skipped — records not ready for a public page, most often drafts. A skip is not an error.
  • Failed — records whose page could not be built. A failure does not stop the run; the platform continues through the rest of the table, counts the failures, and shows the first error message in the receipt.

The report belongs to the table you ran it on — switching to another table clears it, so a count never reads as the outcome of the wrong table.

Publishing requires publish access. If Publish changes is unavailable to you, or the run is declined with a permission message, contact your environment administrator — the permissions panel page describes how access is assigned.

Publish view preview or Details panel preview?​

Both show the same saved reader rendering, so use whichever fits the task:

  • Details panel — best while editing one record: the saved reader preview sits next to the record you are working on. See Preview an entry before publishing.
  • Publish view — best for table-level decisions: checking how field visibility plays out across several records, spot-checking a sample, and then publishing.

Troubleshooting​

SymptomLikely causeFix
A field you set to Workspace or Private still shows on the public page.Published pages keep their last published version; visibility changes wait for the next publish.Select Publish changes, then check the page again.
A visibility setting snaps back to its previous value.The save was declined — most often your role does not include field configuration access.Contact your environment administrator.
Publish changes is unavailable.No workspace table is selected, or a publish run is already in progress.Select a table from the sidebar and wait for any running publish to finish. If the button stays unavailable, your role may not include publish access — contact your environment administrator.
The receipt shows skipped records.Skipped records are not ready for a public page — most often they are still drafts.Activate the drafts and publish again. See Publish a record.
The run ends with a publish failed message.The publish job stopped before it finished, or some records could not be built — the receipt shows the first error.Run Publish changes again. If the same records keep failing, contact your environment administrator with the error message from the receipt.
The record you want to preview is not in the row picker.The picker shows a sample of the table's records, not all of them.The sample is only for spot-checking — publishing covers the whole table regardless. To check a specific record, open it from the Table view and use the Details panel's saved reader preview.
