Frequently asked questions

The task guides answer "how do I do this one thing." This page answers the questions that cross several of them — the ones that come up when something behaves in a way no single step explains. Each answer is short, and points you to the guide with the full steps.

Signing in and your account​

Sign-in says my username and password are wrong, but I am sure they are right — what happened?​

The sign-in page uses one message for every refusal, on purpose: it never confirms whether an account exists. Besides a typo (both fields are case-sensitive), the most common cause is that your membership in the project was suspended or ended — suspension takes effect immediately, even for a browser where you were already signed in. Your project administrator can restore access; nothing is deleted when access is suspended. See Sign in and manage your password.

Why can't my account open the /admin/ area?​

The operations area is for project administrators — it asks for the admin role, not just a signed-in account. Researcher work happens in the workspace, and a researcher account deliberately carries no operations access. If you believe you should have the admin role, ask your project administrator to change it in the Permissions Panel.

My old bookmark to /app/admin/… now opens my workspace — why?​

That address belonged to an early operations page that has been retired; its tasks moved into the /admin/ area. Old bookmarks are redirected to your workspace rather than left pointing at a page that no longer exists. Nothing about your data changed.

Sharing and visibility​

Why can't my colleague see the record I created?​

Two reasons are common. If the record is still a draft, only you see it in the table — a draft becomes part of the shared workspace only once you Activate it. If it is activated but your colleague is looking at the public reader, that is a separate step again: activating makes a record part of your team's workspace, while publishing makes it visible to the world. To finish a draft, see Finish a new row in the drawer; to put a record on the public site, see Publish a record.

I edited a published record, so why does the public page still show the old version?​

The public page keeps the version it was last published with — your edits sit in the workspace until the next publish, they do not flow out automatically. The same is true for a field's visibility setting or a renamed label: the public page catches up only when you publish again. Open the Publish view and select Publish changes to push the current version live. To see the steps, see The Publish view.

Who can publish in my project, and why can a colleague publish work I have not finished reviewing?​

Publishing is controlled by your role, not by the record's stage — anyone whose role includes publish access can publish any activated record, reviewed or not. The platform has no separate sign-off gate that holds a record back until a reviewer approves it. If your team needs review to come before publishing, ask your environment administrator to arrange roles so that only reviewers hold publish access. For how reviewing works today, see Reviewing your team's work.

Are my saved views private, or does the whole team see them?​

Saved views are shared. When you change the filters, columns, or sort on a view, everyone working in that collection sees the change immediately, and deleting a view removes it for everyone. If you want to experiment without disturbing your team, Duplicate the view first and work in the copy. For the full behavior, see Save your table arrangement as a view.

I sent a colleague a search link, so why do they see fewer results than I do?​

A shared search link reproduces your exact search, filters, and result page, but results always respect the person opening them — they see only the collections and records their own access allows. So fewer results for your colleague usually means they lack access to a collection you can see, not that the link is broken. If they need that collection, ask your environment administrator. To copy a link that reproduces your search, see Share a search.

Imports and your edits​

Where did my changes go after I re-imported my source?​

They are still there if the run used an edit-preserving refresh: the platform matches incoming source rows to existing records, refreshes source-managed values, and keeps the fields your team owns. Field labels and field settings you changed are also yours and are not reset by the re-import. A full refresh is different: it reloads rows from scratch and is destructive to researcher edits, so it should be planned with your environment administrator before it runs. A re-import shows you a delta: only what is new, changed, or removed since your last import, so you are not asked to re-decide settled structure. To read the delta, see Understand what changed after a re-import.

A field I renamed is showing its old source name again after a re-import — is that a bug?​

Your label is yours, and a re-import is not supposed to bring the source name back. One case looks like that but is not: in the delta view, changed items are listed under their source-side names until you confirm them, after which your label reappears in the workspace. That label guarantee is separate from record value refresh behavior: edit-preserving refresh protects researcher-owned values, while full refresh is destructive. If your label is genuinely overwritten in the workspace itself after a re-import, that is a defect worth reporting. See Rename a field label and Understand what changed after a re-import.

A field I know is in my source shows as "removed" after a re-import — why?​

The most common cause is a renamed column in your source. When a column's name changes, the platform sees the old name disappear and a new name arrive, so the delta lists one removed field and one new field for what is really the same column. Approve the new one and your label carries over to it. Your saved views adapt to a removed field rather than breaking — see What happens to saved views after a re-import.

My import stopped with a message that two columns would become the same field — what do I do?​

When two columns in your source would map to one field, the import stops before writing anything and lists every collision at once, so you can fix them together rather than one error at a time. Give the clashing columns distinct names in your source and re-import. For the full walkthrough, see Fix colliding column names during import.

Searching and reading​

Why can search find a record I can see right in front of me in the table?​

Search and the table look at different things. The toolbar filter box narrows only the rows already loaded in the active table, while search looks across collections — but only at the fields your environment has marked searchable. So a record can sit in your table yet stay out of search because the word you typed lives in a field that search does not cover. Try a word from a different field, or use full search across collections. See Find a record when search returns nothing and Choose between the quick search bar and Search & Read.

Why does the search results panel say some collections are hidden?​

Searching across a collection requires a review capability for that kind of record, and the note counts the collections your access does not cover. The records are not gone — they are held back from your search until your access includes the capability that covers them. Ask your environment administrator for the capability you need. See See why some collections are hidden in Search & Read.

Why do some search results show strings of letters and numbers instead of a name?​

A collection needs a display field — the field that names each record — for results to read as names. When no display field is set, the result falls back to an internal identifier, which is the string of letters and numbers you are seeing. This is set per collection by your environment administrator; ask them to set a display field for that collection. For how search reads across your data, see Searching and reading.

Why does my search miss a Greek term, or a transliteration that I know is there?​

Two different reasons. For Greek, breathings are set aside in matching but the acute accent still counts — a term typed without its acute accent can miss a record whose stored form carries it. For transliteration, the match works only if the transliteration field actually holds a value and is marked searchable; if it is empty on that record, the native-script form will still find it. See Working in multiple scripts and languages and Find a record when search returns nothing.

Editing and fields​

Why can't I edit this field — it has no editor or shows a "Read" badge?​

Some fields are not edited directly. A field that is computed from other fields, holds media, or links to other records shows as read-only; you change it by editing the fields it is built from, or by adding links and media after the record exists. A field can also be read-only because your role has read access but not edit access. To tell the cases apart and act on each, see Adding and editing rows.

Why can't I link my new record to another record yet?​

A link can only point to an active record, and a brand-new row is a draft until you activate it — so the relation field stays unavailable while either side is still a draft. Fill in the required fields, activate the row, and the link becomes available. To finish the draft, see Finish a new row in the drawer; to add the link, see The Relations view.

My column arrangement reverted, or a colleague's looks different from mine — why?​

Your table layout — column order, sort, filters — is stored on the active view, and views are shared, so a layout can change because a collaborator adjusted the same view, or because you are looking at a different view tab than before. The sidebar is the exception: its width and collapsed state are remembered per browser, while your pins and groups follow your sign-in. For what is remembered where, see What the workspace remembers, and how undo works.

Why does one date look friendly while another still shows the full timestamp?​

The column's field type decides how the workspace treats the value. A Date field shows a readable date in the table and uses a date picker when you edit it. A Text field that happens to contain a timestamp stays text, so it still shows the full stored string until someone changes or converts the field type. See Adding and editing rows and Table layout: sorting, column controls, and the column manager.

Why are there several copy actions in the table?​

Copying a focused cell copies one field value. Copying selected rows copies the visible columns for those rows, with column-label headers, so it can paste into a spreadsheet as a small table. A cell range copies only the rectangular cells you selected by dragging, Shift-clicking, extending with Shift and the arrow keys, using the row-gutter Select visible cells in row action, or using the column menu Select visible column cells action, without row headers. When you need cells that are not next to each other, Target cells lets you click individual cells, press Enter or Space on the focused data cell, or add visible row/column groups with the same row-gutter and column-menu commands; the status line shows Sparse cells: N, and copy/paste then uses only those targeted cells. When you paste one copied value while several rows are selected, the workspace can fill that same column for the selected rows on editable tables; when a cell range is active, paste targets that rectangle. Pasting extra rows into existing rows does not create records automatically; use Paste rows here on a regular table when the clipboard should become new draft rows. For the full distinction, see Copy and paste table values.

Collections and tables​

What is the difference between Add table and Register this table?​

They start a managed collection from two different places. Add table creates a brand-new, empty collection — you give it a name and choose what its rows represent, and then add fields and records yourself. Register this table is for a collection the platform already built from your imported data: it gives that existing collection a permanent home in your sidebar so you can rename and configure it, without changing any of its records. Use Add table when you are starting from nothing; use Register when the records already exist. See Create, rename, and delete a collection.

If I delete a collection, do I lose my records?​

No. Removing a collection only unregisters it — it takes the collection out of your sidebar but never deletes the records underneath. A collection that still holds records stays browsable as an automatic collection built from your data. The one consequence to know about: if the collection had published entries, its curated publish settings reset and the next publish falls back to defaults, while pages and citations you already published stay live. See Remove a collection without deleting its records.

How do I add a 3D model or image to a media record that has none?​

Open the record in the Media view. A record with no file yet shows Attach a file first; editors and environment admins get an Attach media button there. It opens a sheet with two choices: Reference a URL points the record at media already hosted at a web address, and Upload a file stores a file with the record. Once the file is attached the record fills in — a 3D model opens in its viewer. Referencing a URL is offered only on a record that has no version yet; to add a file to a record that already has one, upload it. The record's name and description are separate fields, edited in the collection's Details view. See Work with media in the workspace.

Publishing and citations​

I withdrew a published record, so why is it back on the public site?​

A table-wide publish includes every activated record, and a withdrawn record is still activated — so publishing the whole table can put it back. Withdrawal is held by your environment's administrators, not by the workspace publishing controls. If a record must stay out of public view, agree with whoever publishes that it stays out of table-wide publishes. See Withdrawing a published record.

A citation looks rough or shows placeholder text — is that mine to fix?​

It depends on which it is. Placeholder text means the fields the citation draws on are incomplete on that record, and completing those fields fixes it on the next publish. Rough punctuation or ordering means the collection's citation style is not the right one yet, or is still being refined — that is set by your environment administrator, not on the record. See Copy a citation for a record or media item and Citations you can trust.
