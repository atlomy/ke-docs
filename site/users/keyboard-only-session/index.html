A keyboard-only research session

Purpose​

This page walks through one full research loop without a mouse: reach a table, open a record, fill its fields, activate it, preview it, and move on. It collects the keyboard paths that other pages describe in passing and puts them in the order you would actually use them, with the exact keys for each step. Read it when you work by keyboard or screen reader and want a single route through a session, or when you want to know which parts of that route are solid today and which still need a hand from the mouse.

Accessibility is the companion reference: it lists every keyboard control and the display settings. This page sequences those controls into a working session and does not repeat the full list.

Some steps in this loop are not yet fully reachable by keyboard alone. Those gaps are listed up front, in Where the keyboard path is still incomplete, so you know before you start where you may need to reach for the mouse.

Before you start​

Two things shape what the keyboard does:

  • Mac or Windows / Linux. Shortcuts that use Command (⌘) on a Mac use Control (Ctrl) elsewhere. Where a shortcut appears below, both forms are shown.
  • Where focus is. Shortcuts that act on the whole workspace are ignored while you are typing in a text box, a text area, or a dropdown — so a stray keystroke never fires a command while you write. Step out of the field first (Tab or Escape), then use the shortcut.

The full loop by keyboard​

1. Reach a table​

When the workspace opens, focus starts in the page. Tab moves forward through the controls — the top bar, then the sidebar of tables, then the table toolbar, then the table itself. Shift+Tab moves back. A clear focus ring marks where you are; the ring is drawn when you navigate by keyboard and stays hidden for mouse clicks.

Two faster ways to reach a table:

  • Command palette — press ⌘K (Ctrl+K). A search box opens; type part of a table's name, use the Down and Up arrows to move through the matches, and press Enter to open the one you want. Escape closes the palette without choosing.
  • Sidebar — Tab into the list of tables and press Enter on a table to open it.

To give yourself more room, ⌘B (Ctrl+B) hides and shows the sidebar.

2. Find a record before you create one​

Reading before writing saves duplicate records. Two search routes, both keyboard-first:

  • Quick search — press ⌘⇧F (Ctrl+Shift+F). The search panel opens with focus in its box. Type a term; results appear grouped by collection. Use the Down and Up arrows to move through results and Enter to open the highlighted one. Escape closes the panel.
  • Search & Read — for browsing many records, open the command palette (⌘K / Ctrl+K) and choose Search & Read, or escalate to it from the bottom of the quick search panel. Your typed term carries over. Within that page, the typeahead and the results list respond to the Down and Up arrows and Enter.

How search treats accents and scripts — and why a search matched or missed — is covered in Searching and reading.

3. Create a record​

Open the new-row drawer from the command palette: ⌘K (Ctrl+K), then choose New row. (The drawer is also reachable by tabbing to the toolbar's new-row button and pressing Enter.) The drawer opens as a dialog and focus moves into it.

A state pill at the top of the drawer tells you where the record stands — "Not yet saved", "Draft saved", "Unsaved edits", and so on — so you always know what a screen reader would otherwise have to infer.

4. Fill the fields​

Inside the drawer:

  • Tab moves from one field to the next; Shift+Tab moves back.
  • Type into a text field, then Tab out — your entry is held as you go.
  • For a dropdown field, the Down and Up arrows move through the options and Enter selects one.
  • For a field that links to another record, type to search, Tab to a result, and press Enter to link it.

Required fields are marked, and the drawer will not let the record activate while a required field is empty. A line near the top names the next save step, so the path forward is always stated rather than implied.

A field whose editor has not shipped yet shows a short hint in place of an editor. The hint stays in the Tab order and is announced as disabled, so you can tell the field exists and is intentionally not editable — it is not silently skipped.

5. Activate the record​

When the required fields are filled, activate the record with ⌘Enter (Ctrl+Enter) from anywhere in the drawer, or Tab to the Activate row button and press Enter. If a required field is still empty, activation is blocked and the button stays disabled until you fill it.

To leave without activating, press Escape. If you have unsaved work, a confirmation appears first so a stray Escape does not discard a draft; confirm or cancel it from the keyboard. A draft you leave behind is kept, and the workspace offers to resume it next time.

6. Preview, then publish​

Open a record's full view to read it and preview the public page:

  • From the table, Tab to the row and press ⌘Enter (Ctrl+Enter) to open its details.
  • The details view shows every field beside a reader preview rendered with the same engine as the public reader, so you can check how the record will read before anything is published.

The path from draft to a published page — who can publish, and the per-field visibility controls — is covered in Public reader.

7. Adjust the view as you work​

Several view controls are keyboard-driven:

  • Row density — Tab to the density control and use the Left and Right (or Up and Down) arrows to move between Compact, Cozy, and Comfortable. Your choice is saved with the view.
  • Saved views — the view tabs respond to the Left and Right arrows; Enter or Space opens the focused view.
  • Sidebar groups — ⌘⇧G (Ctrl+Shift+G) creates a group, ⌘⇧E (Ctrl+Shift+E) expands all groups, and ⌘⇧C (Ctrl+Shift+C) collapses them. ⇧S (Shift+S) pins or unpins the current table.
  • Sidebar width — Tab to the resize handle between the sidebar and the table; the Left and Right arrows narrow and widen it, Home and End jump to the minimum and maximum, and Space collapses the sidebar.

Where the keyboard path is still incomplete​

The platform is being made fully keyboard- and screen-reader-driven, and some of the loop above still has gaps. The honest state today:

  • Some command shortcuts work only from the command palette, not as direct keystrokes. The palette lists hints next to several actions — filtering the current table, creating a new row, undoing and redoing a view change. Those actions are reliable when you select them inside the palette (⌘K / Ctrl+K). The single-key forms shown as hints (such as a lone slash, or a lone letter) are not all wired as global keystrokes yet, so reach these actions through the palette rather than relying on the bare key.
  • Undo and redo by keyboard. The undo and redo buttons show the hints ⌘Z and ⇧⌘Z, and the actions are always available from the command palette. Whether the bare key combination fires undo from anywhere in the workspace is still being confirmed; use the palette or the on-screen buttons if a keystroke does not respond.
  • The display-and-reading settings panel is not in the workspace. Font size, line height, font family, and background presets live on your environment's reading and browsing pages, opened with the accessibility button there or with Alt+A. Inside the workspace, use the row density control and your browser's own zoom. See Accessibility.

If a control you expect to reach cannot be reached from the keyboard, that is a bug rather than a setting. Tell your environment administrator so it reaches the people who can fix it.

Screen-reader announcements​

The workspace exposes named roles and labels to screen readers: the new-row drawer is announced as a dialog with a title, fields that are not yet editable are announced as disabled, the focus ring follows keyboard navigation, and the inline tip panel keeps focus stable when its content updates. Spoken confirmations for every action — for example, a spoken note when an inline edit saves — are still expanding, so a screen reader may not yet announce every state change out loud. The on-screen state pill in the new-row drawer names the current state in text, which a screen reader can read on demand.

Troubleshooting​

SymptomLikely causeFix
A workspace shortcut does nothing.Focus is inside a text box, a text area, or a dropdown, where workspace shortcuts are intentionally ignored.Press Escape or Tab to leave the field, then use the shortcut.
A single-key shortcut shown in the command palette does not fire on its own.Not every palette hint is wired as a global keystroke yet.Open the command palette with ⌘K (Ctrl+K) and select the action there.
The focus ring disappears.The ring is keyboard-only; a recent mouse click suppressed it.Press Tab once more to bring it back.
Escape closes the new-row drawer but a dialog appears first.You had unsaved work, so a discard confirmation is shown before anything is lost.Confirm to discard, or cancel to keep editing. Either choice is reachable by keyboard.
Activate row will not respond.A required field is still empty, so activation is blocked.Tab back through the fields, fill the ones marked required, then activate again.
You cannot find the display-and-reading settings in the workspace.That panel lives on the reading and browsing pages, not the workspace.Open a reading page and use its accessibility button or Alt+A. In the workspace, use row density and browser zoom.
