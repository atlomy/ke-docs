Accessibility

Purpose​

Your knowledge environment is built to work with keyboard navigation, screen readers, and adjustable display settings. This page walks through what is available, where to find it, and what is still on the way. Read it when you want to drive the workspace without a mouse, when you need to adjust how text is displayed, or when a control is not reachable or readable the way you expected.

Features​

  • Workspace keyboard navigation — Tab / Shift+Tab, Enter, Escape, arrow keys, and Space cover the workspace's interactive controls. The combinations follow common conventions. Active.
  • Visible focus rings (keyboard-only) — a clear outline on the focused element, drawn when you navigate with the keyboard and suppressed for mouse clicks, so the screen stays quiet for mouse users. Active.
  • Inline editing with the keyboard — Tab into a field, type, Tab out; your change is saved when the field loses focus. Enter saves it too. Dropdown fields respond to arrow keys and Enter. Active.
  • Hints on fields that are not editable yet — a field whose editing surface has not shipped shows a hint in place of an editor. The hint is part of the keyboard tab order and is announced as disabled to screen readers, so you know the field exists and is intentionally not interactive yet. Active.
  • Named workspace form controls — toolbar filters, column-header menus, Details fields, and New-row fields expose stable labels and form identities to browsers and assistive technology. Active.
  • Row density control — three row heights (Compact, Cozy, Comfortable); your choice is saved with the view you are working in. Active.
  • Accessibility control panel — font size, line height, font family, and a small set of background color presets, remembered by your browser. Available on your environment's reading and browsing pages; the workspace does not include it yet. Active. Finer-grained color customization is Deferred.
  • Accessible inline tips — the tip panel that appears alongside your work is screen-reader-friendly and keeps focus stable when its content updates. Active.

Usability — how you use it​

Driving the workspace from the keyboard​

  • Tab / Shift+Tab moves through interactive controls — toolbar buttons, filter controls, the search box, table cells, drawer fields.
  • Arrow keys move within the table grid, the saved-views menu, and the row density control. In the column manager, the arrow keys on a column's reorder handle move that column up or down the list.
  • Space selects the focused row. Hold Ctrl (Cmd on Mac) with Space to add a row to the selection or remove it.
  • Shift + Up/Down arrow extends the selection across a range of rows.
  • Ctrl+Enter (Cmd+Enter on Mac) opens the focused row's details.
  • Enter activates the focused button or saves an edit in a focused field.
  • Escape closes the active drawer, panel, or dialog.

Focused elements show a visible focus ring — a clear outline marking where you are. The ring is keyboard-only by default; clicking a control with the mouse does not draw it.

Editing inline with the keyboard​

  • Tab into a field, type, then Tab out — your change is saved as soon as the field loses focus. Pressing Enter saves it too.
  • For dropdown fields, arrow keys move through the options and Enter selects one.
  • For related-record lookups, type to search, then Tab to a result and press Enter to link it.
  • Escape closes the panel when you are done.

Fields that are not editable yet​

Some fields show a "not editable yet" hint instead of an editor. These hints are deliberately part of the keyboard tab order and are announced as disabled to your screen reader, so it is clear the field exists and is intentionally not yet interactive. They are not skipped, not invisible, and they do not pretend to be editable.

Adjusting how things look​

The table toolbar has a row density control with three settings — Compact, Cozy, and Comfortable. Pick whichever works for your eyes and workflow; your choice is saved with the view you are working in, so it is there when you come back.

On your environment's reading and browsing pages, the accessibility control panel adjusts how text is displayed. Open it from the accessibility button in the page header — announced to screen readers as "Open accessibility settings" — or press Alt+A. The panel is not available inside the workspace yet. It lets you adjust:

  • Font size — make all text larger or smaller as a percentage of the base size.
  • Line height — increase line spacing for easier reading; particularly helpful for dyslexia and low vision.
  • Font family — switch between the default font and alternatives.
  • Background color — a small set of page background presets, useful for contrast and reducing glare.

A Reset to defaults button restores the standard settings. These settings are remembered by your browser on the device where you set them. If you sign in from a different device or browser, set them again there.

Troubleshooting​

SymptomLikely causeFix
A control is not reachable with Tab.A gap in the focus order.Try Shift+Tab from a nearby control that is reachable. If the control still cannot be reached, contact your environment administrator — an unreachable control is a bug, not a setting.
Your screen reader skips a "not editable yet" field.Your screen reader is set to skip disabled controls, or the hint did not load.Turn on your screen reader's setting for reading disabled controls. If the field is still skipped, contact your environment administrator.
No focus ring appears after pressing Tab.You last interacted with the mouse — the ring is keyboard-only — or the page styling is interfering.Press Tab once more. If the ring still does not appear, contact your environment administrator.
Font size resets after you sign out.Display settings are stored in your browser; the browser's stored data was cleared, or you are in a different browser or a private window.Re-apply the setting in the accessibility control panel. These settings do not follow your account across devices.
You cannot find the accessibility control panel in the workspace.The panel lives on the reading and browsing pages; the workspace does not include it yet.Open a reading page and use the accessibility button in the page header, or press Alt+A there. Inside the workspace, use the row density control and your browser's own zoom.
Your row density choice does not stick.The choice saves to the view you have open; the save may not have completed.Set the density again with the view open. If it still resets, contact your environment administrator.
The highest-contrast preset is still not enough for your needs.The preset set is deliberately small for now; finer color customization is deferred.Use the preset closest to your needs and tell your environment administrator about the gap — reports shape what gets built next.
An inline edit does not save when you Tab out.The field is read-only for your role, or the value failed validation (look for an inline error).Correct the value if there is an error shown. For a permissions question, contact your environment administrator.

What is there, what is still arriving​

  • Workspace keyboard navigation: Active.
  • Visible focus rings: Active.
  • Row density control: Active.
  • Accessibility control panel on the reading and browsing pages (font size, line height, font family, background color presets): Active.
  • Inline editing with the keyboard: Active.
  • Discoverable hints on fields that are not editable yet: Active.
  • Accessible inline tips: Active.

Screen-reader announcement coverage — for example, a spoken confirmation when an edit saves — is still expanding.

If something is not working for you​

Accessibility problems are bugs, not configuration questions. If a control cannot be reached from the keyboard, a screen reader misreads a field, or poor contrast makes text unreadable, contact your environment administrator so the problem reaches the people who can fix it.
