Standards & Compliance Overview

Audience: Mixed — researchers, tenant administrators, super admins / platform operators, developers, stakeholders / principal investigators / grants offices, prospective tenants. Last aligned with platform state: 2026-05-25 Platform version: V2 (pre-1.0)

Purpose​

This page is the cross-audience landing zone for KE's posture toward open scholarly standards — what the platform commits to, where the substrate sits today, and what is honestly still on the way. Read this if you are a grants office or principal investigator looking for an interoperability story, a tenant administrator deciding what your tenant inherits for free, a federation partner asking what complies_with declarations the platform will accept, or a developer extending the standards layer. Each section deepens progressively; the table is the load-bearing artifact.

Standards adoption matters here because three things are true at once. Interoperability with the FAIR ecosystem is non-negotiable for an academic platform. Tenant data will outlast the platform that produced it, so the formats it lives in must be readable by tools the platform does not control. And academic citation integrity demands identifiers, vocabularies, and packaging that the wider research community already trusts — a platform-minted identifier that only the platform understands is not a citation, it is a private bookmark.

Standards are the substrate on which a tenant's interpretation rides — they are not the interpretation itself. A tenant's voice, vocabulary, and scholarly choices stay tenant-specific; the standards layer is what makes those choices legible outside the tenant's own walls.

Features​

The platform distinguishes four maturity tiers so that "we comply with X" never has to be read on faith:

  • Built — substrate ships in production code and is exercised by at least one runtime surface. Removing it would break a feature visible today.
  • Active — declared on the platform contract; partial substrate ships; not yet exercised end-to-end. Configuration is possible against the standard, but the full export or rendering pipeline is not yet wired through.
  • Validated — research-grade evidence and an Approved or Proposed ADR exist; no production substrate yet. The platform has surveyed the standard, committed to its shape in an architectural decision, and can defend the choice in front of a partner — but cannot yet exercise it.
  • Deferred — recognised as relevant; no ADR commitment and no substrate until a tenant or contract surfaces the need. Listed openly so the absence is visible rather than silent.

The selection of standards in the table is itself deliberate. KE's scope spans text, structured data, geographic and temporal modelling, persistent identity, accessibility, and data protection — five domains, each with its own established standards corpus. The table covers the standards the platform's archetype work has either committed to (via ADR), surveyed in the research base, or recognised as a near-future onboarding need. Standards outside this scope (medical imaging DICOM, financial reporting XBRL, music encoding MEI) are not listed; if a future tenant requires them, the research-then-ADR-then-substrate sequence applies before they enter this table.

Usability — how you use it​

How compliance is enforced (the structural backbone)​

Compliance here is structural, not just declarative. A platform can claim conformance with any number of standards on a slide deck; what matters is whether the runtime, the data model, and the development process let conformance fail silently or visibly. KE's posture is that drift from a declared standard should fail loudly — at commit time, at projection time, or at export time — rather than hide as documentation rot.

Five mechanisms carry that posture:

  • complies_with declarations on slot configs. Each archetype slot can declare standards it conforms to (TEI:<element>, CIDOC-CRM:<class>, GeoSPARQL:<feature>). The declaration travels with the slot through projection and export, so downstream consumers can read what a tenant's data claims about itself without inferring it. Substrate: ADR-037 + atlomyapp/slot_taxonomy/.
  • Platform-canonical standards allowlist. The set of standards a tenant may declare against lives in atlomyapp/slot_taxonomy/standards.py. Tenants cannot invent declarations against standards the platform has not surveyed; the research evidence base under docs/research/standards/ is the gate for adding one. Substrate: ADR-037 Phase A.1.
  • Engineering crosswalk. docs/research/standards/CROSSWALK.md carries the bidirectional matrix mapping each surveyed standard to each archetype, cell by cell. Per-archetype recommendation cells reference matrix cells by coordinate, never by free-text restatement.
  • In-tree fixtures and validation. SHACL fixtures are planned for the CIDOC / Linked Art export profile (ADR-030); fixtures for the TEI conformance bundle will land under docs/standards/tei/. The WADM target floor and the Entry profile validator already enforce required shapes for IIIF / WADM / CSL / JSON-LD coverage at projection time (epistemic/services/entry_profiles.py).
  • CI gates and pre-commit hooks. Tenant-specific vocabulary is structurally separated from platform code by the lint gate scripts/lint_no_tenant_vocabulary.py; pre-commit hooks (bash scripts/install_pre_commit_hooks.sh) wire the gates into developer machines; CI re-runs them on every pull request.

Per-audience pointers​

If you are a researcher. Your tenant inherits a substrate that supports stable identity, scholarly citation, and standards-grade export — you do not need to configure standards yourself. The platform exposes a stable citable identity for each published artifact, preserves your interpretive decisions across re-extraction runs, and packages your data for archival in formats your institutional repository already understands. See docs/tenant_admin/features/citation-integrity.md for the guarantee shape and docs/tenant_admin/features/voice-and-vocabulary.md for how your wording stays yours.

If you are a tenant administrator. Read docs/tenant_admin/features/standards-and-citations.md for what your tenant gets for free, what you can configure (citation style, vocabulary grounding against external authorities, archetype standards declarations), and how your data exports. The companion docs on vocabulary grounding and voice substitution carry the operational mechanics.

If you are a super admin / platform operator. The standards layer interacts with publication and projection: snapshot writes, latest-good restore, route map and sitemap generation all flow through EntrySnapshotStore. The EntryProfileValidator enforces a semantic advertising floor before snapshot/export payloads are published — no non-JSON standards full overclaims without profile-specific validators. The operations runbook at docs/superadmin/dpia-operations.md carries the GDPR side.

If you are a developer. Start with the engineering crosswalk at docs/research/standards/CROSSWALK.md and the per-standard files under docs/research/standards/<family>/. The platform-canonical allowlist lives at atlomyapp/slot_taxonomy/standards.py; the ADRs in the table above are the authoritative architectural rationale; the per-standard research files carry the cell-level evidence behind each conformance posture.

If you are a stakeholder, principal investigator, or grants office. The narrative version of this surface — why each standard matters for long-term archival, institutional adoption, and the platform's sustainability story — lives at docs/internal/program/07-standards-sustainability-and-business-model.md. That document is written for non-technical readers; this overview is the cross-audience map between the narrative and the engineering substrate.

If you are a prospective tenant. The maturity tiers in the table tell you what you can rely on today, what you can configure, and what is planned. If a standard your project requires is at Validated or Deferred, you can ask the platform team for an explicit timeline or contract; the openly-recorded gap is the basis for that conversation.

How a tenant inherits compliance​

When a tenant onboards, the substrate decisions in the table are already made. The tenant's archetypes can declare complies_with values against the allowlist; the tenant's published Entry layer projects through the same export views as every other tenant; the tenant's vocabulary can ground against the same external authorities (Wikidata, PeriodO) as everyone else.

What the tenant configures — citation style, vocabulary grounding choices, archetype standards declarations, voice and vocabulary substitution — is layered on top. The substrate is shared; the interpretation is tenant-specific.

The practical onboarding sequence for a new tenant is:

  1. Archetypes are configured against the platform's slot taxonomy (ADR-037). Slots can optionally declare complies_with values from the allowlist.
  2. Vocabulary grounding (Wikidata for entities, PeriodO for periods) is offered as a guided workflow during onboarding; the tenant admin reviews AI-proposed matches and confirms the ones that fit.
  3. A citation style is selected via TenantCSLStyle; the choice flows into citation surfaces wherever the platform renders citations.
  4. Once the tenant's Entry layer projects cleanly, the same export views that produce JSON-LD / RO-Crate / TEI for any tenant produce them for this tenant — without any tenant-specific code change.

When standards compose or disagree​

No single standard covers everything an archetype needs to express. A Place entity may carry CIDOC-CRM (E53_Place) for cultural-heritage modelling, GeoSPARQL for geometry, schema.org for search-engine visibility, Pleiades or Wikidata for external identity, and a JSON-LD context for serialisation — all at once. The preset registry (ADR-038) is designed around composition: a single preset declares complies_with against the full stack of standards it touches, and the export pipeline projects each conformance assertion alongside the others.

When two standards model the same thing with different shapes — for example, a Period as a CIDOC E4_Period versus a dcterms:PeriodOfTime versus a SKOS Concept — the platform projects against each shape from the same underlying entity rather than picking one and discarding the others. The projection layer (ADR-029) is where multi-conformance happens; a researcher does not have to choose which standard their data complies with.

When a standard is silent on something the platform needs to express, the gap is recorded explicitly in the per-standard research file, and the extension lives as a separate slot or JSONB field rather than as a falsified standard conformance.

Versioning the standards we adopt​

Standards themselves evolve. TEI is on P5; IIIF Presentation is on 3.0; CIDOC-CRM is on 7.1.3; WCAG is on 2.2. KE pins each adoption to a specific version in the per-standard research file and in the ADR that ratifies it. A tenant's exported data carries the version pin in its conformance assertions, so a future reader can resolve the exact specification the data was produced against. This is what makes a 2026 export still legible in 2036: the version pin is the contract.

Pending and deferred​

Items where adoption is recognised as relevant but not yet committed:

  • Darwin Core — no archetype currently requires biodiversity-grade taxa modelling. Promotion conditions: a tenant onboards with a specimen-bearing or species-level corpus, and the archetype evidence base passes the inclusion gate documented in the standards crosswalk.
  • ICU — folding and collation currently rely on a per-slot accent_fold policy over PostgreSQL collations and lowercase + asciifolding at the Lucene boundary. Promotion conditions: a tenant requires Unicode-grade case-folding or accent-folding rules that exceed what PostgreSQL ships; or a federation partner declares ICU conformance as a contract.
  • OpenAPI — GraphQL is the platform-canonical API surface for both the SPA and the tenant-admin shell. Promotion conditions: a harvesting or federation partner declares OpenAPI as the required contract for a specific endpoint family.
  • Full WCAG 2.2 AA audit — the accessibility substrate (control panel, scaling tokens, semantic HTML discipline) ships; a recorded end-to-end audit is queued behind the published Entry layer ratification (ADR-029) so the audit targets a stable surface rather than a moving one.
  • SHACL fixtures for CIDOC / Linked Art export — ADR-030 declares them as a minimum-contract item before ratification; the fixtures themselves are not yet drafted. Holding the ratification on the fixtures is deliberate — no public claim of CIDOC conformance until the fixtures pass.
  • TEI conformance bundle — planned to land at docs/standards/tei/ once the published Entry layer's export views are stable enough to validate against TEI Lite. The bundle will be in-tree, runnable, and version-pinned to a specific TEI release.

These items are recorded openly so a stakeholder evaluating the platform can read what the platform does not yet do alongside what it does. Conformance gaps that are known beat conformance claims that are unverified.
