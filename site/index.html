KE — Knowledge Environments

Where humanities research groups build, search, and publish structured scholarly knowledge — each in an environment of its own.

A knowledge environment is a working home for one research group's material: the records the group studies, the connections between them, the vocabulary the group uses to describe its own field, and the people who work on it together. Each environment belongs to its group — its wording, its citation style, its decisions about what is published. The foundation is shared; the interpretation belongs to the research team.

Work moves through a steady rhythm. You bring your material in — from spreadsheets and existing databases — through a guided import that shows you what it proposes before anything is written, and that preserves your own edits when the source is imported again. You shape and connect it: records carry the fields your project defines, link to one another, and hold images, audio, video, and 3D models alongside the text. You search and read it — across original script, transliteration, and translation — and when records are ready, you publish them for the world through a public reading view, with citations rendered in your project's chosen style.

The platform serves scholars, students, archivists, and editors — anyone who reads, searches, edits drafts, and cites within a project's collection — and the environment administrators who shape each environment around its group's needs. Beneath both sits a deliberate posture toward open scholarly standards, so that what a group builds stays legible to the wider scholarly world, not only inside the platform.

Researchers​

You work inside a knowledge environment — reading, searching, editing drafts, citing records. Guides to the workspace, search across scripts, drafts and editing, media and citations, accessibility, and what the platform records about you.

Environment Admins​

You configure one environment: the words the platform uses, who can do what, and how data comes in. Guides to imports, permissions, vocabulary, citations, and preparing your public surfaces.

Standards​

The open scholarly and data standards the platform builds on — citation styles, text encoding, linked data, persistent identifiers. An honest account of what ships today, what is committed, and what is still on the way.
