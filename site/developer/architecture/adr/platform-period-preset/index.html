ADR-040: Platform-canonical Period preset (platform-period/v1)

Status: Proposed (v1.2 — amended 2026-05-20 per R1c targeted stress-test refinements layered on v1.1 absorption; see docs/superpowers/plans/2026-05-20-period-place-spatial-architectural-pass.md for absorption provenance) Date: 2026-05-20 Deciders: User; architecture team Related: ADR-037 (Slot-Type Taxonomy and Tenant Vocabulary Layer), ADR-038 (Federated Preset Registry as Federation Contract), ADR-006 (Archetype Capability Contracts), ADR-022 (Anchor + ClaimCluster + ArtifactVersion + extractor framework), ADR-029 (Projection-First Published Entry Layer), ADR-030 (CIDOC + Linked Art Export Profile Choice) Companion:

  • docs/research/standards/CROSSWALK_extensions_2026-05-20.md — Phase 1.5 standards extension; Clause 1 PROMOTE verdict for TIME_PERIOD
  • docs/superpowers/specs/2026-05-20-place-period-federation-contract-paths.md — comparative paths brainstorm (Path B chosen)
  • docs/superpowers/specs/2026-05-20-phase-b-federation-contract-worked-examples.md — worked examples + the ADR-038 stub

TL;DR​

ADR-038 commits the platform to ship six initial-library PlatformPreset entries via Threshold-A discipline. This ADR ratifies the first of those six: platform-period/v1 — a federation-contract-bearing preset that gives tenants with period-like entities (named historical periods, dynasties, cultural phenomena, archaeological periods, radiocarbon ranges, fuzzy chronological intervals) a single platform-canonical shape to subscribe to.

The preset is text-centric: no spatial substrate dependency, no PostGIS / GeoSPARQL / MapView coupling. It can ratify and ship independently of ADR-039 (Place preset) and the PostGIS / GeoSPARQL substrate ADR. The multikey_kind=calendar extension to ADR-037 (added 2026-05-20 from R2 absorption B3) is the load-bearing mechanism for dual-calendar (Hijri + Gregorian) and many-calendar (dynastic + radiocarbon + Hebrew AM + BP + MYA) tenants. The Visionis CIDOC-E4 thematic axes shape is supported via the optional thematicAxes RELATION-to-ClaimCluster slot — not by polluting the platform-default required slots, but by giving thematic-period tenants a structured place to land their content.

Nine standards land in this preset's complies_with declaration (v1.1 — six original + three v1.1 additions): PeriodO (gold-authority for periods, skos:Concept + W3C Time Ontology), CIDOC-CRM E4_Period, dcterms:PeriodOfTime, W3C Time Ontology (time:Interval), TEI @period attribute + <taxonomy>/<category> (v1.1 CORRECTED from non-existent <period> element per R1b verification), SKOS Concept, plus SKOS-XL, XKOS, EDTF (ISO 8601-2), and schema.org/HistoricalEvent (v1.1 additions per profile-driven research). Subscribing entities project to JSON-LD with dcterms:conformsTo triples for all nine.

Context​

Why a Period preset, why now​

Three threads from the 2026-05-19 / 2026-05-20 architectural arc converged on the need:

  1. CROSSWALK Phase 1.5 extension verdict. The extended standards matrix (Phase 1 + 7 new rows) re-runs Clause 1 for TIME_PERIOD: 4 strong standards × 2 families (metadata + lod), PASSES — PeriodO is the decisive lod-family vote that Phase 1's family-count check had missed. Verdict line: "Previously failed family-count (1 family); PeriodO is the decisive lod-family vote. Margin thinner than PLACE (2 families vs 5); recommend treating as PASSES-but-narrow." Per the CROSSWALK extension §5, Period can move independently of Place — text-centric, no spatial dependency.
  2. Four-tenant evidence recount (ADR-037 ratification brief §2.8.e):
    • Atlas: implicit (BC/AD century-grain lifespan years for AUTHOR + treatise dating).
    • JPP: 231 periods rows + startYear/endYear + 2 junction tables (persons_periods, reports_periods); regnal / dynastic.
    • Visionis: dedicated TIME archetype with CIDOC-E4-shape rich thematics (8 thematic axes — theological debates / legal developments / scientific innovations / literary practices / etc.) + OUT companion sheet + AUTHOR TIME OF LIVING HIJRI / TIME OF LIVING GREGOR columns side-by-side (dual-calendar in authoring).
    • Integrative tenant: dynastic + Hijri + Hebrew anno mundi + BP + radiocarbon + MYA + stratigraphic — 5–6 calendar systems concurrent.
  3. Path B chosen at ADR-037 ratification. Per ADR-038, Period lands as a PlatformPreset (federation-contract layer) rather than as an archetype (Path A). The federated preset registry accommodates Visionis's E4 thematic shape via tenant fork (visionis-thematic-period specializes platform-period/v1 with monotonic addition of thematicAxes requirement) without polluting the platform-default required-slot set. Path B preserves long-tail discipline accommodation; tenants without thematic needs subscribe to the base preset; tenants with thematic needs fork with prov:specializationOf lineage (CORRECTED in v1.1 per R1b PROV-O verification — see §D8). AI-agent provenance queries (prov:specializationOf* transitive closure) traverse the lineage automatically.

Why this is not "EVENT.kind=period" by another name​

The CROSSWALK extension's JSONB-rescue test for TIME_PERIOD: "dual-calendar + fuzzy-interval modeling under EVENT.kind=period is awkward; PeriodO-compatible URIs need a stable home." Three concrete tensions:

  • PeriodO URI minting. Tenants that publish PeriodO-style period definitions need a stable IRI-bearing home for each period. EVENT rows can take an IRI but lose the SKOS-concept semantics (PeriodO models periods as skos:Concept instances in a skos:ConceptScheme, not as event occurrences).
  • Fuzzy-interval modeling. Radiocarbon BP 12,000 ± 200, "early Hellenistic," "late Yuan / early Ming transition" — these don't fit EVENT's start/end semantics cleanly. They want an Interval with calibration ranges and optional textual scope notes, which is what W3C Time Ontology + PeriodO together provide.
  • Dual-calendar interval. Visionis AUTHOR's Hijri + Gregorian column pair is direct evidence that period extent itself can be calendar-multikey. EVENT.start/end is a single timestamp pair; Period.interval under this preset is a multikey_kind=calendar slot per ADR-037, with sub-intervals per calendar.

The Period preset is therefore complementary to EVENT, not a substitute for it. Events are occurrences in time (a death, a publication, a conquest); Periods are named temporal extents (the Yuan dynasty, the Hellenistic period, MIS 5e). Many EVENTs participate in many Periods; the Period preset's time:Interval extent is the discoverable handle for that participation.

What this ADR does NOT decide​

  • Place preset (ADR-039): paired with PostGIS / GeoSPARQL substrate ADR; gated by Spec 0019 MapView design.md. Separate ADR.
  • PostGIS / GeoSPARQL substrate: orthogonal to this preset (Period has no spatial substrate requirement). Separate ADR.
  • EVENT preset definition: the EVENT preset uses time:hasTime references that may point to a Period subscribing to this preset, but EVENT's contract is a separate later ADR.
  • TIME_RANGE SlotKind: the ADR-037 §Evolution Layer-1 amendment that adds a slot-level fuzzy-interval primitive remains separate work. Subscribers to this Period preset can use existing DATE + composition discipline + the numeric_format=year modifier in the interim; the future TIME_RANGE SlotKind is a clean upgrade path.

Decision​

D1 — Preset identity​

The platform ships platform-period/v1 as a PlatformPreset (RDF type ke:PlatformPreset per ADR-038) with the canonical IRI:

https://ke.atlomy.org/presets/platform-period/v1

Preset version 1 is the initial-library ratification commitment. Evolution to v2+ follows ADR-038 D4 (monotonic addition of required slots or complies_with declarations only).

D2 — Required slots​

Every entity subscribing to platform-period/v1 MUST populate:

SlotSlotKindModifiersSemantics
prefLabelTEXTmultikey_kind=language, multilingualDisplay name (untagged label from source); SKOS prefLabel semantics. At least one language entry required.
intervalTEXT (Wave 1) / TIME_RANGE (Wave 2 post ADR-037 §Evolution Threshold-C amendment)multikey_kind=calendar, multikey_keys=["gregorian", ...] per tenant calendarsTemporal extent. Per-version structured shape per Bundle D Finding 1 + v1.2 refinements per R1c N1/N2/N3/N7. Each calendar entry carries: {value, calendar, startYear, endYear, precision, uncertainty, periodLabel, kind}. Field semantics: value — free-text interval string from source ("1271–1368 CE", "670–770 AH", "BP 12,000 ± 200", "early Hellenistic"); display source-of-truth. calendar — calendar identifier (gregorian, hijri, hebrew, julian, BP, MYA, dynastic, etc.; per Three-Tier vocabulary extensibility in ADR-038 D10). startYear / endYear — integer year bounds from best-effort backend parsing; negative integers represent BCE (e.g., −0599 for 600 BCE; per ISO 8601-2 EDTF convention); back the federation-query temporal-overlap index. precision (v1.2 NEW per R1c N2) — enum: instant / year / month / day / decade / century. Default year for integer-year intervals; instant for point-in-time events (R1c N3); day when a time:DateTimeDescription block is also populated. uncertainty — ± qualifier with units matching precision (e.g., ±200 with precision=year for radiocarbon BP). periodLabel — fuzzy textual representation when integer years cannot be inferred ("late Yuan / early Ming transition", "early Hellenistic"). kind (v1.2 NEW per R1c N3) — enum: interval (default; bounded duration) / instant (point-in-time event; startYear==endYear allowed; supports stratigraphic events, single archaeological finds, dated documents); kind=instant entries pass the §D2 required-interval-slot contract. Federation-query indexing (v1.2 refined per R1c N7) — backend computes a per-calendar normalized integer range index from startYear + endYear for SPARQL temporal-overlap queries. For solar/Julian-day-stable calendars (gregorian, julian, hebrew with year-granularity), the index is direct; for lunar (hijri) the year-length variability is approximated to ±10 days at year-granularity (acceptable for cross-tenant overlap queries; insufficient for sub-year precision — those queries fall back to per-calendar time:DateTimeDescription blocks); for radiocarbon BP, conversion to gregorian-equivalent year happens at index time with explicit ±uncertainty preserved. The index is approximate by design; precise sub-year queries traverse the full structured shape. At least one calendar entry required; multi-calendar tenants populate multiple. This shape supersedes the previous v1.0 free-text-only stringly-typed approach which Bundle D Finding 1 + the post-D.2 R1 A2 finding both flagged as wrong-v1-choice; the migration cost of fixing it later (when paying tenants land) was unbounded versus the 1-2 sprint-weeks of getting it right now. The TIME_RANGE Layer-1 SlotKind is queued in ADR-037 §Evolution Threshold-C amendment as the formal structural primitive.
periodTypeSELECTenum_options derived from the Period preset's vocabularyPeriod categorization. Initial enum: dynastic, cultural, archaeological, scientific, radiocarbon-range, geological, fuzzy-historical, other. Tenants can extend via fork.

Why these three are required:

  • prefLabel: federation discovery (structured SPARQL queries across tenants) requires a primary label per period IRI; PeriodO's skos:prefLabel is the canonical analogue. Label text is also what vanilla-RAG embedding pipelines key on (the embedded text, not the IRI), so a populated prefLabel benefits any retrieval system regardless of whether it consumes the structure.
  • interval: the temporal extent IS the defining content of a period. The multikey-by-calendar shape is the load-bearing mechanism for dual-calendar tenants; collapsing to a single calendar would force Visionis (Hijri + Gregorian) and the integrative tenant (5–6 calendars) into a single-calendar projection at write time, losing source-of-truth.
  • periodType: subscriber discoverability + JSON-LD type-disambiguation. Without periodType, downstream consumers cannot distinguish a dynastic period from a geological one; CIDOC E4 framing also benefits from explicit kind.

The thematicAxes slot is the architectural answer to the Visionis CIDOC-E4 shape: tenants that need thematic-period decomposition declare a fork (visionis-thematic-period) that promotes thematicAxes from recommended to required. The fork preserves visibility under platform-period/v1 queries via prov:specializationOf* transitive closure (ADR-038 D5; amended per R1b PROV-O semantic verification — see §D8).

D4 — Forbidden patterns​

  • canonical_type=Person, canonical_type=Place, canonical_type=Event: Period is a distinct concept; cross-archetype overloading is rejected.
  • Single-calendar interval in a multi-calendar tenant context where the source data carries multiple calendars: the platform write-block should surface a conformance violation prompting the researcher to declare the additional calendar(s) at subscription time. Initial implementation soft-fails (warning); hardened to hard-fail in Phase B.5 once Visionis + integrative-tenant migrations validate the heuristic.
  • Empty prefLabel: writes blocked. Every Period must have at least one display label.

NOT a vanilla-RAG enabler — vanilla RAG embeds the raw text and is agnostic to JSON-LD type tags; the value here is specifically for structured-consumer pipelines (search engines, GraphRAG systems, JSON-LD-aware tools), not generic LLM context augmentation.

A subscribing tenant inherits all nine declarations transitively (six original + three v1.1 additions). A fork may add (e.g., a Wikidata-aligned fork declaring wikidata:Q11514315 historical-period) but cannot remove any of the nine per ADR-038 D4 monotonicity.

D6 — Capability activations on subscription​

Subscribing to platform-period/v1 activates the following platform-shipped widgets and export surfaces (no tenant-side code required):

Workspace surface (declared via abstract capability URIs per Q8 / R1 finding A3 — NOT React widget names; ADR-038 amendment §D6 carries the URI vocabulary):

  • ke:supportsTemporalRendering — declares the platform surface that consumes a Period's per-calendar structured interval shape (§D2) into a calendar-tab-disambiguated rendering UI. Default React frontend mounts the TimeRangeWidget component (extracted from MultilingualCellEditor's data-derived multikey pattern per Q12 + Bundle D Finding 2). Headless / Python / GIS aggregator consumers mount their own renderer keyed off the same capability URI. Stub component lands in Phase B.1; full editor in Phase B.5.
  • ke:requiresCalendarConversion — declares the platform surface that bridges between subscribed calendars (Hijri ↔ Gregorian, BP ↔ Gregorian, etc.). Default frontend mounts CalendarConverterWidget; non-React consumers route URI to their own conversion logic. Available; not auto-mounted.
  • ke:supportsGazetteerLookup + standard hint periodo — declares the platform surface that offers PeriodO authority search at period-create time. Wave 1 (Phase B.5 ships): manual click — researcher clicks "Lookup PeriodO" button, types query, picks match. Wave 2 (gated on AI orchestration sprint per ADR-014-alpha): auto-suggest from source data shape — import wizard detects period-shape rows in tenant source data and offers PeriodO match candidates proactively. The capability URI declaration is identical across waves; the surface implementation evolves. PeriodO endpoint: https://n2t.net/ark:/99152/p0 (or equivalent canonical endpoint at fire time).

Public reader surface (ADR-029 projection):

  • JSON-LD output with dcterms:conformsTo enumerated per D5 (all nine declarations); time:intervalStartedBy + time:intervalFinishedBy + time:DateTimeDescription triples for each calendar; skos:prefLabel + skos:altLabel for labels; skos:broader for parentPeriod reference; dcterms:source for citation.
  • @type array: [skos:Concept, cidoc:E4_Period, dcterms:PeriodOfTime, time:ProperInterval, schema:HistoricalEvent] — multi-vocabulary typing enables structured-retrieval consumers (federation SPARQL, search-engine crawlers, GraphRAG systems, scholarly-infrastructure ingestion) to recognize the entity under whichever vocabulary they prefer. NOT a benefit for vanilla-RAG text-embedding retrieval.
  • Multi-calendar interval is emitted per Bundle D Finding 1 per-version structured shape: each calendar entry projects to a time:DateTimeDescription block with its own time:year / xsd:gYear values from the per-calendar startYear + endYear integer fields, plus an EDTF literal generated from the structured shape (using ISO 8601-2 Level 1/2 syntax). Primary calendar gregorian by default; tenants override. The textual value field is preserved verbatim as skos:note for human display. The genuinely-fuzzy periodLabel field projects to EDTF approximate-date syntax (e.g., [..323~] for "early Hellenistic"). Per-calendar Julian-day-number range index backs federation-query temporal-overlap operations.
  • schema.org/HistoricalEvent projection: subscribers also emit schema:startDate + schema:endDate from the Gregorian (or tenant-declared primary) calendar's startYear/endYear. Search-engine crawlers (Google, Bing) that index the public-reader surface consume this for entity-panel + rich-snippet rendering.

Compliance export surface:

  • TEI <taxonomy> / <category> export view in <teiHeader> + @period attribute references — CORRECTED in v1.1 per R1b verification (v1.0 declared a non-existent <period> element). The export emits a <taxonomy xml:id="ke-periods-<tenant>"> block inside <teiHeader> containing one <category xml:id="period-<id>"> per Period entity, with <desc> carrying the prefLabel and child <catDesc> per calendar. Datable elements in the tenant's TEI corpus reference periods via @period="#period-<id>". For tenants subscribed to TEI corpus export (Spec 0008 + future TEI export ADR).
  • CIDOC-XML export view — CIDOC E4_Period serialization with E52 Time-Span via P4 has time-span, populated from the per-calendar structured fields. Per v7.1.3 spec verification (this session's R1b re-critique): E4 multi-inherits from E2 + E92; spec permits discussing E4 without representing the spacetime volume — so Period preset's text-centric design is consistent with E4 conformance.
  • PeriodO-compatible JSON — for direct ingestion into a downstream PeriodO-mirror. D8 — Subscription discipline + fork rationale​

Subscription mechanics follow ADR-038 D3 generically. Period-specific guidance:

  • At subscription time: back-check existing tenant entities with period-like canonical_type (canonical_type=Period, canonical_type=Era, canonical_type=Dynasty, canonical_type=Time, etc.) against the required-slot contract. Surface conformance gaps to the tenant admin; admin closes gaps before subscription completes.
  • Tenant alias mapping: per ADR-004 tenant ontology mapping, tenants can label the preset locally — "Era" (Atlas implicit), "Period" (JPP), "Time" (Visionis), "Chronological Interval" (integrative tenant). Aliases are tenant-facing only; the canonical preset IRI stays stable.
  • Fork rationale guidance: if a tenant's period authoring requires additional required slots beyond D2, the fork rationale should name (a) the discipline / use case (e.g., "Mongol-era prosopography requires regnal-year disambiguation"), (b) the new required slots, (c) the conformance back-check evidence (how many existing entities already satisfy the new requirement). The platform validates monotonicity per ADR-038 D4 but does not enforce rationale content.
  • Fork lineage predicate (CORRECTED in v1.1 per R1b PROV-O verbatim verification — see §Compliance lineage-predicate note): forks declare <fork-iri> prov:specializationOf <parent-preset-iri> (NOT prov:wasDerivedFrom). Per the PROV-O REC §specializationOf definition: "An entity that is a specialization of another shares all aspects of the latter, and additionally presents more specific aspects of the same thing as the latter." This semantic exactly matches the platform's fork-as-monotonic-refinement model (ADR-038 D4: forks add required slots; cannot remove). prov:wasDerivedFrom is a transformation/update semantic that does NOT imply the child shares aspects of the parent — wrong predicate for the federation contract.

The Visionis CIDOC-E4 thematic-period fork serves as the canonical fork example documented in the Phase B.5 spec — its rationale: "CIDOC-E4 Period-as-cultural-phenomenon shape; subscribers in Islamic legal-theological tradition need 8 thematic axes (theological debates / legal developments / scientific innovations / literary practices / etc.) as required structured slots, not optional free-text."

Alternatives Considered​

Use EVENT.kind=period and skip the preset​

Coalesce period-like entities under the existing EVENT archetype with a discriminator value kind=period. Tenants set kind=period on event rows; downstream consumers filter accordingly.

Why not chosen: the CROSSWALK extension JSONB-rescue test for TIME_PERIOD documents three concrete tensions (PeriodO URI minting, fuzzy-interval modeling, dual-calendar interval shape) that EVENT.kind=period handles awkwardly. PeriodO is a skos:Concept aggregator, not an event registry; the modeling lift to absorb it under EVENT loses external interop. ADR-037 §C5 PLACE / TIME_PERIOD deferral notes already anticipated this: the JSONB-rescue test was failing as evidence accumulated; the preset path resolves it without an archetype migration.

Promote TIME_PERIOD to a new archetype (Path A)​

File an ADR-006 amendment promoting TIME_PERIOD to a new archetype with a capability contract. Existing EVENT rows with kind=period migrate to TIME_PERIOD archetype rows.

Why not chosen: ADR-038 §Alternatives walks the Path A vs Path B comparison in full. The summary: Path A forces uniformity (every period-like entity must fit one platform-default shape) at the cost of long-tail accommodation. Visionis's E4 thematic shape either pollutes the platform-default required slots or sits awkwardly as a tenant-config override against an ADR-006 contract. Path B (this ADR, via ADR-038) gives Visionis a fork with prov:specializationOf lineage (CORRECTED in v1.1 per R1b PROV-O verification — see §D8); platform-default stays clean; long-tail accommodated. Plus no data migration in JPP + Visionis.

Ship Period preset jointly with Place preset​

Bundle ADR-039 (Place) + ADR-040 (Period) + the PostGIS / GeoSPARQL substrate ADR into a single architectural milestone.

Why not chosen: the CROSSWALK extension explicitly notes (§5) that Period can move independently of Place because it has no spatial substrate dependency. The Place preset is gated by Spec 0019 MapView design.md (currently only a brainstorm exists), so bundling forces Period to wait on Place's substrate decisions for no architectural benefit. Period text-centric shape is well-evidenced now; ratification + Phase B.5 fixture authorship can proceed in parallel with the Place / PostGIS work.

Defer Period preset until Phase A.2 frontend lands​

Hold Period preset authoring until ADR-037 Phase A.2 (frontend rewrite consuming the slot-taxonomy foundation) ships, so the workspace surface for period editing is real before the preset's required-slot contract commits.

Why not chosen: the preset's contract is platform-canonical and stable; Phase A.2 consumes it, not the other way around. Drafting the preset now lets Phase A.2 + Spec 0020 (Phase A.1 backend) target the canonical contract during their implementation; deferring would force them to target a moving spec. The G3 demonstration fixtures (D7) are the ratification gate; they can land alongside Phase B.5 work without blocking the ADR's authoring.

E4 multi-inheritance §Compliance note (v1.1 — R1b v7.1.3 grounding)​

Per CIDOC CRM v7.1.3 §E4 Period (verbatim): "E4 Period is a subclass of E2 Temporal Entity and of E92 Spacetime Volume." The First Order Logic axioms are E4(x) ⇒ E2(x), E4(x) ⇒ E92(x). This means E4 is intrinsically spatial in the CIDOC ontology. However, the same spec section explicitly permits: "By virtue of this multiple inheritance, it is possible to discuss the physical extent of an instance of E4 Period without representing each instance of it together with an instance of its associated spacetime volume." This Period preset's text-centric v1 design relies on the spec's permissive language; the optional spatialExtent slot (§D3) carries the spacetime volume when tenants populate it. Tenants subscribing to both Period and Place presets (post ADR-039) produce composed projections where the spacetime volume is populated via the linked Place entity. CIDOC E4 conformance is therefore honest at v1 — not "we ignore spatial" but "the spec endorses this representation choice."

E4 ⊃ E5 §Compliance note (v1.1 — R1b inversion correction)​

Per CIDOC CRM v7.1.3 §E4 Period (verbatim): "E4 Period is a superclass of E5 Event." The First Order Logic: E5(x) ⇒ E4(x) (Event is a kind of Period; Period is the parent). This means §D4's "Period is a distinct concept; cross-archetype overloading is rejected" stance — specifically forbidding canonical_type=Event on entities subscribing to platform-period/v1 — is consistent with the CIDOC hierarchy, not in conflict with it. The platform's archetype boundaries are about authoring shape + entity identity (a Period is its own thing; an Event is its own thing), not about ontological subclass relationships. CIDOC's hierarchy is preserved in the JSON-LD projection's @type array (subscribed entities project both E4_Period AND E2_TemporalEntity types per multi-inheritance).

Projection-conditional PeriodO conformance §Compliance note (v1.1 — per user UX validation)​

PeriodO Technical Overview lists four mandatory things for gazetteer inclusion: "To be included in the gazetteer, a period must: give the period a name; impose some temporal bounds on the period; have some implicit or explicit association with a geographical region; and have been formally or informally published in some citable source." These are PeriodO's gazetteer-inclusion rules — NOT the platform's Period entity validity rules. The platform's required slots (§D2 — prefLabel + interval + periodType) define what makes a valid Period entity. PeriodO conformance is declared as projection-conditional, gracefully-degrading:

  • When all four PeriodO-required items are populated (name = prefLabel; temporal bounds = interval with structured startYear/endYear; spatial association = spatialExtent from §D3; citable source = sourceCitation from §D3) → projection emits a full PeriodO gazetteer-shape JSON record + the full dcterms:conformsTo triple for PeriodO.
  • When any of the four are NOT populated → projection emits the always-present subset (skos:Concept + dcterms:PeriodOfTime + time:ProperInterval triples) and the PeriodO-specific shape gracefully degrades. The dcterms:conformsTo triple for PeriodO is still emitted with a ke:conformanceLevel="partial" qualifier.

Researchers doing rough-authoring are NEVER blocked. A scholar entering "first century BC" as a fuzzy author-lifespan reference creates a valid Period entity with name + interval + type + nothing else. The projection emits the always-present subset; the PeriodO-specific shape is partial. No error, no warning beyond a quality-signal badge that says "Standards conformance: PeriodO partial — add spatial + citation for full." Tenants doing authoritative period publishing (a curated dynastic gazetteer) populate the full set and gain full PeriodO conformance via the projection. This matches the user-value commitment in docs/superpowers/plans/2026-05-20-period-place-spatial-architectural-pass.md §0.

Lineage-predicate §Compliance note (v1.1 — R1b PROV-O verbatim verification)​

v1.0 used prov:wasDerivedFrom for fork lineage. Per the W3C PROV-O REC §wasDerivedFrom (verbatim): "A derivation is a transformation of an entity into another, an update of an entity resulting in a new one, or the construction of a new entity based on a pre-existing entity." This is a transformation/update/construction semantic that does NOT imply the derived entity shares aspects of the source.

Per PROV-O §specializationOf (verbatim): "An entity that is a specialization of another shares all aspects of the latter, and additionally presents more specific aspects of the same thing as the latter." This is exactly the platform's fork-as-monotonic-refinement semantic per ADR-038 D4 (forks add required slots; cannot remove). v1.1 corrects all fork-lineage declarations to use prov:specializationOf. Note: PROV-O does not declare specializationOf as transitive or asymmetric (those are not axioms in the REC); the federation engine's traversal of specializationOf chains assumes transitivity at the application layer.

  5. MultiKeyDimensionEditor generalization timeline: the data-derived multikey UX pattern that this preset's interval slot consumes is currently embedded in MultilingualCellEditor.tsx. Extraction as a reusable component is a frontend deliverable, not in this ADR's scope. When does that ship? Recommend: alongside Spec 0019 timeline UI design.md (Phase 7 of this architectural pass).

  6. Standards-evolution discipline for v1.1's nine complies_with declarations: each declaration carries a version (e.g., periodo ARK base implies current; W3C Time CRD 2022-11-15; CIDOC-CRM v7.1.3; TEI P5 4.11.0). When upstream standards update, what's the platform's response? Recommend: versioned complies_with URIs (e.g., cidoc-crm/v7.1.3); existing tenant subscriptions stay on their pinned version; new subscriptions get latest; curator review at six-month boundaries (next: 2026-11-20) OR on tenant-requested upgrade OR on Threshold-A promotion candidate surfacing.
