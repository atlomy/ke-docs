Entry Surface Configuration

Purpose​

The Entry Surface role configuration is the record that defines a public reading surface for your environment: which of your records become public pages, what the public route looks like, how the index is ordered, and what title each page carries. It is the configuration behind the readiness review — the readiness panel reviews the controls that would go public, while this record declares the surface itself.

Read this when you want to understand what a public Entry role controls before asking your platform operator to change one, when you are reviewing why a record did or did not appear on the public surface, or when you need to read the meaning of the fields on the role configuration record. The companion review surface is Entry Surface Readiness.

Self-serve and operator-mediated​

The role configuration record lives in your environment's admin area and is owned by your environment — your platform operator's seeding never overwrites a role you have edited. Inspecting a role and understanding its settings is something you do yourself.

Applying a change so that it takes effect on the public surface — launching a role, promoting public controls, or rebuilding published pages — is operator-mediated. Recording a readiness decision does not promote anything, and editing a role configuration does not by itself republish the public surface. The safe sequence is: understand the record (yourself), then ask your platform operator to apply and rebuild. The fields below are written so you can read and reason about a role before that conversation.

What a role configuration controls​

A single role configuration declares one public reading surface. Its fields fall into five groups, matching how they are arranged in the admin record.

Role identity​

  • Role key — the platform-stable name for the role, such as primary. It is internal and stable; it does not change when you rename the public-facing noun.
  • Display noun — the reader-facing noun for what this role publishes (for example, "entry" or "record"). This is the word readers see, separate from the internal role key.
  • Active — whether the role is in use. An inactive role declares no public surface.

Selector — which records become public​

The selector decides which of your records this role publishes, without reclassifying those records:

  • Archetype — the record family (the kind of records, such as people or works) the role draws from.
  • Canonical type — an optional narrowing to a single type within that family. Left empty, the role takes the whole family.
  • Primary selector — marks this selector as the primary one for the role.
  • Allow multiple primary selectors — when off, a role must have exactly one primary selector; turning it on lets a role publish from more than one primary selector.

Blast radius of editing a live role's selector. A selector edit applies lazily. Already-published pages keep serving their last good version until the next publish or rebuild. At the next rebuild, any record the edited selector no longer matches is removed from the public surface — whether or not that record is itself still published. An invalid selector set fails before any pages are written, so a bad selector cannot silently corrupt the surface; it stops the rebuild instead. Because a selector edit can remove live public pages, treat selector changes as the change you most want to review with your platform operator before it is applied.

Public identity sources​

These three settings are dotted source paths (for example, data.title) that point at a value in each record. They derive the public-facing identity of each page:

  • Slug source — derives the public route for each record. A record where the slug source resolves to nothing has no public identity and is skipped by the page builder — it will not get a public page.
  • Sort key source — derives the value the public surface sorts records by.
  • Index key source — derives the value the browsable public index is keyed on.

The public route for a page takes the form /entry/<role key>/<route reference>, where the route reference comes from the slug source.

Rules​

The rules group carries finer selector and display settings:

  • Selector rules — additional selector and display rules. Two keys you will encounter: display_label_source sets which value becomes the public page title, and seed records how the default for the role was derived (it documents the starting point, so you can see whether a setting is a seeded default or your own edit).
  • Route aliases — additional route segments the surface accepts for this role, for example an older path a reader might still have bookmarked. These are accepted routes only; they do not change which record family a record belongs to.
  • Search engine — an optional search-engine choice for this role. Left as the default, the role uses your environment's search profile. The other choices select a specific search backend for this role only; the role key stays local to your environment.

Versioning​

  • Config version — bump this when the public Entry Surface behavior for the role changes. Changing it signals that stale published pages should rebuild at the next publish.
  • Record version and content hash — read-only bookkeeping the platform maintains for the record.

How a change reaches the public surface​

Editing the role configuration record stores your intent; it does not redraw the public pages on its own. When a published surface is rebuilt, a change to the role's selector, public identity sources, display noun, route aliases, selector rules, config version, or active flag is what causes the builder to recompute the affected pages. A record whose slug source resolves to nothing is skipped; a record the selector no longer matches is removed. Your platform operator runs the rebuild — see the operator-mediated note above.

What to check before requesting a change​

  • For a selector change: confirm which records currently match and which would match after the edit, because records that stop matching are removed from the public surface at the next rebuild.
  • For a public identity change: confirm the dotted source path resolves to a value on the records you expect to publish; a path that resolves empty means no public page for that record.
  • For a route alias change: confirm you are adding a route a reader might use, not asserting a record type.
  • Before launch: confirm the readiness review reads clean — see Entry Surface Readiness — then hand off to your platform operator to apply and rebuild.
