Citation Integrity and DOI Safety

Purpose​

When a researcher in your environment ratifies a binding set on a media artifact — saying "these terms correctly describe these regions of this artifact" — and a DOI is minted on top of that ratified state, the platform guarantees the cited data cannot silently change. A re-extraction run against a citable Edition is refused outright: it stops before any binding is touched. False-positive bindings a reviewer rejects stay rejected across re-runs. Read this when evaluating the platform for a peer-reviewed publication or a DOI-minted scholarly artifact, or when answering an institution's editorial, DPO, or library question about how citation integrity is enforced.

This page explains the guarantees in plain language. The actions that produce the ratified state live on the Media Review Surface.

Features​

  • Three-layer artifact identity — every cited artifact carries three kinds of identity: a citable identity (the publishable artifact name plus an optional DOI, Zotero, or institutional handle); platform-internal identifiers that are never published; and the artifact's own natural self-description as the machine selector — glTF node names for 3D models, IIIF region selectors for images, TEI xml:id values for texts. When a specific part is cited, the printed locator is the resolved scholarly label that part maps to (e.g. the term a mesh region is bound to), not the raw selector string; the selector stays internal as the machine pointer · Maturity: Active · Limit: the platform never rewrites the artifact file at ingest; internal identifiers and raw selectors stay internal.
  • Working versions and citable Editions — every binding set belongs to an artifact version that is either a Revision (a working copy that can still change) or an Edition (frozen, citable, peer-review-targetable) · Maturity: Active · Limit: an environment may carry many Revisions of the same artifact; promoting one to an Edition is a deliberate, separate action.
  • Explicit promotion to Edition — promoting a Revision to an Edition is a single confirmed action on the Media Review Surface; once promoted, the version is protected against re-extraction · Maturity: Active · Limit: requires the admin role.
  • Edition freeze against re-extraction — the platform refuses any ingest run that would change the bindings of an Edition; the run stops with an error before any binding is touched · Maturity: Active · Limit: structural — not a warning, not a log entry.
  • Durable rejection (Strip) — when a reviewer strips a false-positive binding, the platform keeps the record as a tombstone — a retained marker of the rejection — instead of deleting it, so later re-extraction recognises the rejection and refuses to re-propose the same binding · Maturity: Active.
  • Reviewer-edit preservation in working versions — bindings a reviewer has approved or amended are marked as reviewer-owned; re-extraction never overwrites them, even while the version is still a Revision · Maturity: Active · Limit: bindings no reviewer has touched remain refreshable in a Revision.
  • Per-action attribution — every review action records who acted, what the action was, when, and an optional note; this record is stored with the binding and is never deleted or rewritten by re-processing · Maturity: Active · Limit: the trail lives on each binding record; the platform does not keep a separate consolidated audit log of these review actions.

Usability — how you use it​

The guarantees described here are produced by actions on the Media Review Surface; this page describes what those actions mean for citation.

  • Pin a citation-ready Revision as an Edition: open the record from the workspace Media view, confirm the bindings are correct on its detail pane's Mesh→term tab, then on the Version tab pin the revision as an Edition with an Edition label (requires the admin role); a confirmation step precedes the pinning.
  • Attach a DOI to an Edition (forthcoming — no workspace control yet): the platform does not mint DOIs. DOI adoption — binding an existing, externally-minted DOI (from your institutional library, a repository system, or a Crossref or DataCite membership) to the media object so the platform records it and surfaces it in the citation — is a gated capability whose workspace control is not yet built; it lands when DOI adoption is enabled for an environment. By default, and until then, the Edition is fully citable by its stable canonical identity, no DOI required — adoption adds a DOI to an already-citable Edition, it never gates citeability.
  • Strip a false-positive binding durably: in the workspace Media detail pane's Mesh→term tab, choose Strip on the wrong binding and confirm. The rejection is permanent across re-extractions: future runs recognise it and do not re-propose the binding.
  • Update the artifact file under an active citation: ask your platform operator to ingest the updated file under a new version label. The original Edition's data — and any DOI adopted on it — stay intact; the new Revision is reviewed on its own merits and can be pinned as a new Edition later.

The platform does not:

  • Mint DOIs. DOI minting belongs to your institution's library or repository system; the platform provides the stable target.
  • Host published papers. The platform holds the cited data, not the publication.
  • Count citations. How often a published Edition is cited is a question for your institution's bibliometric tools.
  • Embargo by date. There is no embargo or paywall workflow. If you need an embargo, hold the promotion to Edition until you are ready to publish, then promote.

Troubleshooting​

SymptomLikely causeFix
A re-ingest of an artifact file was refused with a message that the version "is frozen for citation"The artifact version is an Edition; the platform refused to change itThis is the guarantee working as designed. Ask your platform operator to ingest the file under a new version label; the original Edition stays intact.
A reviewer stripped a binding last week, but the latest re-extraction proposed it againThis should not happen — a stripped binding is preserved as a rejection across re-runsContact your platform operator, identifying the artifact and the affected binding. Durable rejection is a structural guarantee; a recurrence is a platform defect.
Promote to Edition is missing or disabledThe artifact has not been ingested with a version yet, or your role lacks adminConfirm the artifact has been ingested (ingest creates the first version); verify your role assignment in the Permissions Panel.
You need to embargo an Edition until publicationThe platform does not implement embargo workflowsKeep the version a Revision in a private workflow until the embargo lifts, then promote. The platform does not restrict access to Editions by date.
An audit reviewer asks for a formal audit log separate from the per-binding attributionPer-binding attribution is durable but lives on each binding recordThe per-binding attribution records — who acted, what the action was, when, and any note — are the audit trail, and re-processing never deletes or rewrites them. The platform does not keep a separate consolidated log of binding review actions; if the reviewer needs the records gathered into one export, contact your platform operator.
