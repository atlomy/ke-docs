Vocabulary Grounding

Purpose​

When your records bring in vocabulary the platform does not already recognize — today, calendar names appearing in your data and standard identifiers declared on your field definitions — the platform collects each new value in a review queue instead of guessing what it means. Grounding is that review step: you decide, value by value, whether each one becomes a recognized part of your environment's vocabulary, with a label of your choosing and optional links to recognized scholarly and data standards. Read this when you are working through the review queue after an import, when you want AI help proposing matches against public reference sources, or when a researcher reports that a vocabulary value was reviewed incorrectly.

Features​

  • Vocabulary review queue — every watched vocabulary value first seen in your data appears in a review list, with its category, how often it occurs, and its review status (shown as Ungrounded, Tenant-declared, Promoted, or Rejected). Maturity: Active. Limit: the platform currently watches two categories — calendar names in your records and standard identifiers on your field definitions; queue rows are created by the platform from your data, and you cannot add rows by hand.
  • Declare a value — accept a value into your environment's vocabulary, with an optional display label (leave it blank to use the value itself) and optional links to standards from the platform's recognized list (TEI, CIDOC-CRM, IIIF, CSL, and others). Maturity: Active. Limit: within one vocabulary category, two declared values cannot share the same label.
  • Reject a value — mark a value as not belonging to your vocabulary, with an optional reason kept for the audit record. Maturity: Active.
  • AI grounding suggestions — when enabled for your environment, the platform can propose a grounding for a queued value: a suggested label, suggested standards, and — where a match exists — a reference to a public authority record. Wikidata supplies candidates for entities and concepts; PeriodO supplies candidates for historical periods, including each period's time range and regional coverage as the authority describes them. Maturity: Built. Limit: suggestions cover common reference-data categories only (calendars, standards, place types, period types, media kinds, languages, scripts); there is not yet a button in the review queue for requesting one — suggestions are requested through the platform's administrative API, and the review queue shows a notice when suggestions are available but not yet switched on for your environment.
  • Privacy screening for external lookups — before any value is sent to an external reference source, the platform classifies it. Only values that are clearly public reference data are looked up externally; values that resemble personal or private data are never sent outside your environment. Maturity: Active. Limit: the screen is deliberately cautious — a blocked lookup costs you a manual check, never a data leak.
  • Decision capture on AI suggestions — each decision you take on an AI suggestion (accepted as-is, edited before applying, rejected) is recorded as a structured signal for audit and quality measurement. Maturity: Active. Limit: these records stay within your environment.
  • AI usage controls — AI suggestions are switched off by default for each environment, and usage is capped by a rolling token budget. When the budget runs out, suggestions pause and manual review continues unaffected. Maturity: Active. Limit: the budget has a platform-defined maximum.
  • AI availability notice — a fixed, platform-written notice on the review queue tells you when AI suggestions are available but switched off for your environment. Administrators who manage permissions see a link to the settings page; other reviewers see guidance to ask their administrator. Maturity: Active. Limit: the wording is platform-controlled and cannot be customized.

Usability — how you use it​

The review queue lives in your environment's admin interface, alongside the other vocabulary surfaces.

  • Work the queue after an import: open the ungrounded vocabulary list. Filter by category or by review status to focus on what is new. Each row shows the value, its category, and how many times it occurs in your data.
  • Declare one or more values: select the rows, choose the declare action, and confirm on the follow-up page. You can set a shared label (or leave it blank so each value becomes its own label) and pick any standards the values comply with from the provided list. Declared values stop appearing as unreviewed in future imports.
  • Reject values that do not belong: select the rows, choose the reject action, and optionally record a reason on the follow-up page. The reason is kept with the audit record.
  • Turn AI suggestions on: AI suggestions are off until enabled for your environment. When the feature is available, the notice at the top of the review queue links administrators with permission-management access to the settings page; see Permissions Panel. Enabling AI suggestions and adjusting the token budget are administrator-only actions; if you do not find these controls on the settings page, contact your platform operator.
  • Trust re-imports: re-importing your data never resets a review decision. A value you have already declared or rejected keeps its status; the platform only updates how often it occurs.

Every grounding decision — declare or reject, manual or AI-assisted — is written to the platform audit log with the acting administrator, the value, and the outcome.

Troubleshooting​

SymptomLikely causeFix
The declare action refuses your labelAnother declared value in the same vocabulary category already uses that label (capitalization does not matter)Choose a different label, or leave the label blank so each value is labeled as itself.
A standard you want to link is not offered in the listThe standards list is platform-managed; only recognized standards can be attachedDeclare the value with the standards available now. To request an addition to the list, contact your platform operator.
An AI suggestion arrives without an external reference, or marked as reduced confidenceThe privacy screen blocked the external lookup, or the public source returned no match — the suggestion was produced from the value aloneThe suggestion is still usable. Review it manually before accepting, or reject it and declare the value yourself.
AI suggestions stop arriving partway through a sessionYour environment's rolling token budget is used up, or AI suggestions were switched off — either in your environment's settings or platform-wideManual review keeps working. Check your environment's AI settings; if the feature is unavailable platform-wide, contact your platform operator.
A value you already reviewed shows as unreviewed after a re-importThis should not happen — re-imports never reset review decisionsContact your platform operator with the affected value and the import date.
You want to change the wording of the AI availability noticeThe notice is platform-controlled and cannot be edited per environmentShare environment-specific guidance with your reviewers through your own channels rather than working around the notice.
