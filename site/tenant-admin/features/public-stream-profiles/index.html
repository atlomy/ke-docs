Public stream profiles (retired)

Retired 2026-07-11. Public stream profiles and their field bindings were a V1-compatibility mechanism and have been removed — the admin surfaces are gone and the underlying records were dropped. How a published page is composed is now governed by the current publication mechanisms: the Appearance selector rules, readable web addresses, index declarations, and the published-record projection. See Entry Surface Configuration, Standards and Citations, and Search Exposure for the surfaces that replace it. The text below is retained for historical reference only and no longer describes a live surface.

Purpose​

This page is the reference for the configuration records that decide how a published record is laid out on the public reader: which field becomes the page title, which becomes the description, which carries the citation, which drives the page's web address and its position in an index. Those decisions live in a public stream profile and its field bindings. Read this when a published page shows the wrong field in a heading, when a record's citation or evidence is missing from the public layout, when you are reviewing what an import seeded for a new collection, or when planning a change to how a public page is composed.

What you change yourself, and what the platform maintains​

The public reader's layout is governed almost entirely by records the platform seeds and maintains. A dedicated environment-admin editor for these records does not exist yet, so changes today are made for you by your platform operator.

  • Admin-readable. You can read every stream profile and its field bindings, see which fields fill each part of the public page, and tell apart a binding the platform seeded from one that was authored by hand. This page is written to let you read and verify those records confidently.
  • Operator-mediated. Creating a profile, adding or changing a field binding, editing a route alias, or switching a profile off is done with your platform operator. Bring them the specific change — "the title should come from this field, not that one" — and they apply it in the platform's administration records.
  • Platform-maintained. Most profiles and bindings are seeded automatically when imported data is materialized, derived from your environment's public roles and collection metadata. A re-seed refreshes only the parts it owns; any binding authored by hand is reported and never overwritten.

Features​

  • Stream profiles — one profile defines a single public stream: a presentation contract that turns your data into a public-reader page. A profile carries a stream key (its unique name), an optional public role it presents, an optional concept family and record-type narrowing that scope which records it serves, an active flag, and the contracts described below. Most environments need only the seeded default; an environment can have more than one stream. Maturity: Active. Limit: the stream key is unique per environment, so a second stream needs its own key.
  • Field bindings — each binding maps one part of a public page to where its content comes from in your data. A profile has many bindings — one for the title, one for the description, one for each citation part, and so on. A binding names the part it fills (its semantic role, below), how it resolves the value, and the value itself or the path to it. Maturity: Active. Limit: a profile allows at most one binding per field key, so two parts cannot claim the same key.
  • Semantic roles — the named parts of a page — every binding declares which part of the public page it fills. The available parts cover the page's structure: the main display label and a secondary label; a description, with shorter and longer variants; a translation set and a transliteration set for multi-script records; the citation label and the citation quote; locators, including a page locator and a line locator; author and treatise facets; media links; the page's route reference, sort key, and index key; an active flag; and a passthrough for content carried through unchanged. Maturity: Active. Limit: the role list is fixed by the platform; a binding chooses from it rather than inventing a new part.
  • How a binding resolves its value — a binding draws its content one of five ways: from a single field, from a path inside a record's stored data, from a fixed constant, from a computed value, or by composing several inputs into one. The resolution kind decides what the binding's payload must contain, and the platform checks the payload before a public page is built from it. Maturity: Active.
  • Seed evidence on every binding — each binding records whether it was seeded by the platform or authored by hand, the strategy that last produced it, the share of sampled records that strategy covered (from none to all), and the machine-readable reasons from the last seeding attempt. This is how you tell an automatically derived binding from a hand-authored one, and how you judge whether a seeded binding fit your data well. Maturity: Active.
  • Identity, projection, and adapter contracts — a profile carries three contract blocks. The identity contract holds the public-facing semantics of the page — its route, label, sort, and index behavior. The projection contract is the shape the platform's page assemblers consume. The adapter contract is temporary compatibility data for transitional readers, not a permanent part of how a page is described; it exists to bridge older reading surfaces during the move to the public reader. Maturity: Active. Limit: the adapter contract is transitional and is not treated as the authoritative description of the page.
  • Route aliases — extra web-address segments a stream will answer to, alongside its primary route. Useful when an environment has published a record under more than one path and wants old links to keep resolving. Maturity: Active. Limit: editing a profile's identity or selector changes which records the stream can serve; pages already published keep their last good content until the platform rebuilds them.
  • Active flag and provenance retention — switching a profile or a binding off removes it from the public build without deleting it. An inactive record is kept for provenance and ignored by the page loaders. Maturity: Active.

Usability — how you use it​

Public stream profiles live in your environment's administration records. Each profile lists its stream key, the role and concept family it presents, whether it is seeded or hand-authored, its active flag, and how many field bindings it carries. Opening a profile shows its three contract blocks and its bindings together, so you can read the whole page composition in one place.

Because a dedicated environment-admin editor does not exist yet, the steps below describe what you read and verify yourself, and what to bring to your platform operator to apply.

  • Read what fills each part of a page. Open the profile and read its bindings. Each binding's part name (its semantic role) and field key tell you which piece of data fills the title, the description, the citation, and so on. A profile with a title binding, a description binding, and citation bindings composes a standard reading page.
  • Tell a seeded binding from a hand-authored one. Read each binding's seed evidence. A binding marked seeded was derived automatically; its coverage figure tells you how much of your data the strategy fit. A binding marked manual was authored by hand and will survive any re-seed.
  • Check why a part is missing or wrong. If a public page shows the wrong field in a heading, find the binding for that part and read its field key — it points at the data the platform is pulling. If a coverage figure is low, the seeded strategy fit only some records, which is the likely reason a part is blank on others.
  • Plan a layout change. Decide the exact change in terms of parts and data — "the description should come from this field" — and bring it to your platform operator. They edit the binding (or add one) in the administration records; the public page picks up the change on the next rebuild, not instantly.
  • Verify after a change applies. Confirm the binding now names the field you intended, that the profile is active, and then preview a published record to see the part filled correctly. The published page reflects the change only after its snapshot rebuilds.

Changing a profile's identity or selector changes which records a stream can serve going forward. Records already published keep their last good content until the platform rebuilds and reindexes them — a layout change is never instantly retroactive.

Troubleshooting​

SymptomLikely causeFix
A published page shows the wrong field in its heading or description.The binding for that part names a different field key than you expect.Open the profile, find the binding for that part, and read its field key. Bring the corrected field to your platform operator to apply, then preview a record to confirm.
A part of the page (citation, description, locator) is blank on some records but not others.A seeded binding's strategy fit only part of your data — its coverage figure is below full.Read the binding's seed evidence. A low coverage figure means the part is expected to be blank where the strategy did not apply; supplying the missing data, or a hand-authored binding from your operator, fills it.
A binding I expected the platform to keep was changed after a re-seed.Only seeded bindings are refreshed by re-seeding; a binding must be marked manual to survive.Read the binding's seed-evidence flag. If a binding should never be overwritten, ask your platform operator to author it as a manual binding.
An old public link stopped resolving after a layout change.The profile's route changed, and the old path is not listed as a route alias.Ask your platform operator to add the old path to the profile's route aliases so old links keep resolving.
A profile was switched off but its records still appear published.Switching a profile off removes it from future builds; pages already published keep their last good content until the platform rebuilds.The change takes effect on the next rebuild and reindex, which your platform operator runs. Confirm with them when the rebuild has completed.
Two parts of a page seem to fight over the same content.A profile allows only one binding per field key; the conflict is in how the bindings are composed.Read the bindings and their parts. Bring the intended single source for each part to your platform operator to resolve.
