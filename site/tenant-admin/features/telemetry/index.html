Telemetry and Privacy

Purpose​

The platform collects a deliberately small set of operational signals about how it runs — never a copy of your research content. This page tells you what those signals are, where they live, how long they are kept, what you control, and what to tell an institutional DPO or IRB. Read it when onboarding under a Data Processing Agreement, when an institution's information-security team asks the standard onboarding questions, or before enabling any optional capture. The default posture is capture-off: AI-call, import-run, and review-decision capture record nothing until switched on for your environment. The one exception is tip-activity capture, which starts on — with its own off switch — so that unhelpful tip categories can pause automatically.

Features​

  • AI-call records — for each AI call the platform makes on your environment's behalf, a structured record: the task, the model and its version, token counts, latency, a refusal or escalation flag, and the verdict of a prompt-injection screening check. The record carries one-way hashes of the prompt and response — never the raw text. A call whose prompt matches a high-risk personal-data pattern (such as an identification number) is dropped entirely: no record, no hash, no count. Maturity: Active.
  • Import-run records — for each import run: the source format, entity counts before and after, error categories (never error values), and duration. By construction these records contain no personal data: raw source rows, filenames, staged file paths, document and table names, and credential references are deliberately not recorded. Maturity: Active.
  • Review-decision records — structured signals from suggestion review: reject, undo, edit-after-apply, and feedback-button events, each with a structured reason code. The record carries a keyed fingerprint of the suggestion's shape, not its content, and never records free-form reviewer comments. Maturity: Active.
  • Tip-activity records — which efficiency tips surfaced and whether they were accepted, aggregated into acceptance ratios that drive the automatic pausing of unhelpful tip categories. Tip records carry no researcher identity at all — which tip appeared and what happened to it, never who. Automatic pausing acts on a tip category only once it has at least five recorded responses (the default minimum sample), so it never reacts to a single researcher's actions. Supports the Efficiency Tips surface. Maturity: Active.
  • Search-quality records — planned aggregate measures of search health (zero-result rates, hashed query patterns with small-count suppression). This signal class is not capturing yet: today the platform stores no search-query telemetry at all, raw or hashed. Maturity: Deferred.
  • Per-environment capture switches — capture is controlled per signal class for each environment, and every switch starts off except the tip-activity pair, which starts on. Enabling review-decision capture additionally requires a recorded privacy acknowledgment (who acknowledged, when, and which version of the privacy notice) before the switch will take effect. Maturity: Active.
  • Environment telemetry panel — a dashboard showing your environment's own signals: counts per signal class, a suggestion-quality summary, and any automatically paused suggestion categories. It reads only your environment's records and never displays raw source rows, filenames, file paths, credentials, or tokens. Maturity: Active.
  • Telemetry export for access requests — a self-service, audit-grade export of your environment's telemetry is planned but not yet available. Today, your platform operator produces the export for you on request. Maturity: Deferred.

Usability — how you use it​

  • Read what is happening in your environment: open /admin/monitoring/telemetry/. The panel shows your environment's signal counts by class, the suggestion-quality summary, and any paused automatic-suggestion categories. It is scoped to your environment only; cross-environment comparisons exist only on the platform operator's side.
  • Turn capture on or off: capture switches live on your environment's platform record and are changed through the platform's administrative console — contact your platform operator to change your capture posture. Each signal class is switched independently; everything except tip-activity capture starts off.
  • Enable review-decision capture: this switch requires a privacy acknowledgment to be recorded first. The acknowledgment (person, timestamp, notice version) is stored with your environment's record and is enforced — the switch cannot be turned on without it.
  • Control efficiency-tip capture: tip surfacing and tip-event capture each have their own switch on your environment's record. Both are currently on by default. Tip-event capture continues while both switches are on, even where other capture classes are off — this keeps the automatic pausing of unhelpful tips working. See Efficiency Tips.
  • Honor a data-access request (GDPR Article 15): contact your platform operator, who produces a telemetry export scoped to your environment.
  • Honor an erasure request (GDPR Article 17): see the right-by-right table below; route the request through your platform operator.

Where the data lives​

Each environment's data lives in its own separate compartment of the database — its own database schema. The database itself enforces that isolation, and telemetry honors it: telemetry tables refuse to be read from outside an environment's schema.

DataWhere it livesWho can read it
AI-call records (structure and hashes)Your environment's schemaYou; platform team (read-only, for support)
Import-run recordsYour environment's schemaYou; platform team (read-only)
Review-decision recordsYour environment's schemaYou; platform team (read-only)
Tip-activity recordsYour environment's schemaYou; platform team (read-only)
Cross-environment operational metrics (cost, latency, model name)Platform-level storage; never your contentPlatform team only

Cross-environment metric rows identify an environment by an internal identifier and carry model name, token counts, cost, and latency — never content.

Retention policy​

The platform's default retention policy per data class:

DataDefault retention
AI-call records (structured shape)30 days verbatim, then 12 months as hashed aggregate
Import-run records12 months
Validation and abuse audit records (security-relevant)2 years (GDPR Art. 17(3)(e) — defence of legal claims)
Confirmed prompt-injection material (quarantined)90 days; extendable under legal hold for specific incidents

These defaults are calibrated against ICO and EDPB operational-log guidance, ISO/IEC 27001:2022 control A.8.15, and EU AI Act logging requirements. For retention questions beyond this table — including shorter environment-specific retention — contact your platform operator.

GDPR rights​

RightHow the platform handles it
Access (Art. 15)Your platform operator produces a telemetry export scoped to your environment, with an accompanying manifest.
Rectification (Art. 16)Not applicable to operational telemetry. For environment content, rectification flows through the standard editing surfaces.
Erasure (Art. 17)User-identifying telemetry rows are pseudonymized in place. Small-count-suppressed aggregates are retained as anonymous data (Art. 17(3)(d)).
Restriction (Art. 18)Verbatim capture for a specific person can be suppressed on request while anonymous aggregates continue.
Portability (Art. 20)Same export format as Article 15.
Object (Art. 21)Per-person suppression on request; an environment-level objection turns the signal class off entirely.
Automated decision-making (Art. 22)The platform does not make automated decisions with legal or similarly significant effects on data subjects. AI output is advisory; researcher review gates any change to your data.

Lawful basis is Legitimate Interest (Art. 6(1)(f)), with a documented assessment per signal class available from your platform operator. A data-protection impact assessment precedes production capture and is refreshed annually; environment-specific documentation is available on request.

Third-party exposure​

Third partyWhat they see
AI model vendorUnder the platform's standard routing, AI calls run inside a cloud provider's hosted model service, and the model vendor does not receive your data. Confirm the routing in force for your environment with your platform operator.
Cloud provider hosting the model serviceRuns the model on each call; provider-side invocation logging is kept off, so the provider retains only the call envelope (model, role, timing) — not prompts or responses.
Platform teamRead-only access to your environment's schema for support; access to platform-level operational metrics (cost, latency — never your content).
Anyone elseNothing. No third-party analytics, advertising, or telemetry services.

Offboarding​

When an environment offboards, its database schema is dropped — all telemetry payload in it is deleted, including every aggregate stored there. Cross-environment operational metrics (cost and latency at the platform level) are retained for cost-history continuity; they identify the environment only by an internal identifier and contain no content. On request, that metric trail can be deleted too; the trade-off is the loss of platform-level cost-trend history for the periods your environment participated in.

SymptomLikely causeFix

Review-decision capture also requires the privacy acknowledgment to be recorded first.

The telemetry row records the outcome only — no credential material is ever in a telemetry record.

You want to verify no raw search queries are storedRaw query text is not captured at all in the current platform — the search-quality signal class is not yet capturing in any formNothing to configure.
