Standards and Citations

Audience: Tenant administrator. Last aligned with platform state: 2026-06-12 Platform version: V2 (pre-1.0)

Purpose​

This page is the operational view of the platform's standards substrate from inside one environment. It tells you what your environment inherits without configuring anything, what you can configure on top, what exports produce, and what stays your environment's interpretation. Read this when an institutional reviewer asks "what standards does our KE comply with?", when a federation partner asks whether a specific export format is supported, or when you are designing or revising an archetype slot's complies_with declaration.

The cross-audience overview is at ../../standards/README.md; this page is for someone running an environment.

Features​

What you inherit without configuration​

The substrate ships with each environment. None of these capabilities require per-environment engineering, custom code, or a separate contract.

  • Stable citable identity for published artifacts — substrate: ADR-022 + ADR-023 + Spec 0008; backing standards: DOI, RO-Crate, JSON-LD; maturity: Active; limit: see Citation Integrity for the structural guarantee shape.
  • CSL-based citation framework — substrate: Spec 0008 + customers/models.py TenantCSLStyle; backing standard: Citation Style Language; maturity: Active; limit: a CSL style file is portable XML; uncommon styles are platform-team-installable.
  • IIIF-compatible media with W3C Web Annotation selectors — substrate: ADR-022 + ADR-024; backing standards: IIIF, W3C Web Annotation; maturity: Active; limit: each artifact's locator is its natural self-description (glTF node name, IIIF region selector, TEI @xml:id).
  • JSON-LD output for the published Entry layer — substrate: ADR-029 (projection-first published Entry layer); maturity: Active; limit: emits dcterms:conformsTo triples for every standard the relevant slots declare against.
  • Wikidata grounding capability — substrate: Spec 0026 + customers/services/vocabulary_derivation.py; maturity: Active; limit: grounding is offered, never auto-applied; see Vocabulary Grounding.
  • PeriodO grounding for historical periods — substrate: Spec 0026 (PR 8c7a5969, 2026-05-23); maturity: Active; limit: scoped to PeriodO's regional/temporal model; non-matching periods stay ungrounded.
  • ORCID attribution preservation — substrate: ADR-023 + ADR-025; maturity: Built (substrate); limit: contributions travel with ORCID identifiers through the platform and into export bundles.
  • WCAG 2.2 AA accessibility substrate — substrate: accessibility control panel (spa/src/components/AccessibilityControlPanel/); maturity: Active; limit: baseline accessibility commitments; tenants do not opt in per-tenant.
  • GraphQL API contract — substrate: ADR-003 + atlomyapp/graphql/schema.py; maturity: Active; limit: type-safe contract; integrations consume a documented schema rather than an undocumented REST surface.
  • GDPR-aligned telemetry — substrate: ADR-016 + Spec 0018; maturity: Active; limit: redact-before-log, hash-chained; the platform-wide DPIA + per-environment DPIA on request; see Telemetry.
  • Platform-canonical place and period presets — substrate: ADR-038 + ADR-039 (Place preset) + ADR-040 (Period preset) + ADR-041 (spatial substrate); maturity: Built (substrate); limit: subscribing to a preset brings its full complies_with declaration with it.

What you can configure on top​

  • CSL citation style (TenantCSLStyle) — per-environment; applies wherever the platform renders citations. The tenant-admin surface accepts scholar-approved citation proposals.
  • Vocabulary grounding (Wikidata, PeriodO) — confirm or reject AI-proposed groundings through the Vocabulary Grounding workflow; confirmed matches carry an external identifier and a short display label pulled from the authority.
  • Place and Period preset bindings — subscribe to platform-place/v1 and platform-period/v1 when your environment carries place-like or period-like entities. The subscription brings the preset's full complies_with declaration.
  • Archetype complies_with declarations — slots on your archetypes declare conformance against the platform-canonical allowlist of standards. The declarations are checked at commit time against the allowlist; they flow through projection and export. Substrate: ADR-037 (slot-type taxonomy).
  • Voice and vocabulary substitution — independent of standards but composes with them; your wording is yours, the structural conformance underneath is the platform's. See Voice and Vocabulary.

Touch heuristics:

  • CSL style — once at environment setup; revisit when adopting a new venue or institutional style guide.
  • Vocabulary grounding — during onboarding, and whenever a new vocabulary entry is added.
  • Place / Period preset bindings — once when the archetype design lands.
  • Archetype complies_with declarations — when designing or revising an archetype slot. Structural metadata, not a runtime toggle.
  • Voice substitution — whenever the platform's default wording feels foreign to your researchers.
  • Reference evidence and citation rules — once per published entry type, when designing how its References section should read (see next section).

Reference evidence and citations on published entries​

When a published entry shows a References section — one row per piece of evidence, each with a scholarly citation — two declarations on the entry type produce it. Both are configuration, not code; the platform supplies the machinery, your environment supplies the meaning.

  • The attestation walk — where your evidence lives and how it joins. You declare which record type holds the occurrences of an entry (the passages, witnesses, specimens, or find records), which link connects them to the entry, and which of your columns carry the locators (page, chapter, line, quote) and the source details (author, work, editor). Every column name in the declaration is your environment's vocabulary; the platform never assumes any of it. Substrate: ADR-020, ADR-061.
  • Citation rules — how citations are built and styled. You declare which of your columns map to the standard citation fields (author, title, editor, date, page), and which CSL style renders them. The result is a properly formatted citation per evidence row, plus standalone source citations beneath the evidence list. Substrate: Spec 0008, ADR-061.

Two behaviors worth knowing when designing the declarations:

  • Editions are optional. An occurrence with no published edition — a researcher's own catalogued manuscript or fragment — still appears as a full evidence row. Evidence is never gated on the existence of a published edition.
  • Paired-edition bridging. When an occurrence is catalogued against more than one edition — say a modern critical edition and an early printed one — the row bridges them in the classics convention: the modern citation first, then = volume.page (abbreviated editor) for the paired edition. The abbreviations and locators are your data; the bridge composition is the platform's.

Citation appearance (punctuation, abbreviated author forms, locale) is managed through CSL styles, not by editing rendered text: pick or refine a style through the citation style configuration, where scholar-approved proposals are reviewed and applied (the same surface described under "Pick a CSL citation style" below). If a citation looks rough, the fix is a style refinement — see the troubleshooting table.

What exports produce​

The published Entry layer is the export surface. When your environment publishes Entries, the platform produces:

  • JSON-LD for each published Entry, with dcterms:conformsTo triples for every standard your archetype's slots declare against. Substrate: ADR-029.
  • RO-Crate bundles packaging the Entry plus its anchors, attestations, claim clusters, and media binaries — JSON-LD inside, BagIt-compatible packaging around. Substrate: ADR-023 + ADR-029.
  • Public-reader CSL citations — each Entry surface includes a preferred citation rendered against your CSL style.
  • CIDOC / Linked Art profile export — substrate: ADR-030; maturity: Built (substrate); limit: profile export is a structural projection, not a runtime conversion.

The export views are platform-canonical; your environment does not require a custom export pipeline. What is tenant-specific is the data, the wording, and the archetype shape — not the export contract. Some export capabilities are still maturing; the ../../standards/README.md carries the explicit maturity tier per item.

Usability — how you use it​

The day-to-day operator paths:

  • Pick a CSL citation style at environment setup: open the tenant-admin CSL style configuration; choose Chicago, APA, MHRA, a journal-specific style, an institutional in-house style, or upload a CSL XML file via the platform team if uncommon.
  • Subscribe to a Place or Period preset: declare the subscription on the relevant archetype. The preset's complies_with declaration flows through automatically.
  • Declare an archetype slot complies with a standard: edit the slot's complies_with field in your archetype configuration. The declaration is checked at commit time against the platform-canonical allowlist; rejections surface inline.
  • Ground vocabulary against Wikidata or PeriodO: use the Vocabulary Grounding workflow. Confirmed matches carry an external identifier; export bundles include the link.
  • Hand a federation partner a JSON-LD or RO-Crate sample: the published Entry layer is the export surface; the bundle is the portable format. Any institutional repository that accepts RO-Crate ingestion accepts a bundle produced by the platform.
  • Answer "what standards does our KE comply with?": point to ../../standards/README.md. The cross-audience overview is written for that conversation.

Refining a citation style (the review workflow)​

When the seeded style renders roughly for your material, a style is refined through a three-step review path, so no one person silently changes how every citation reads:

  1. Propose. A proposed style (or a refinement of the active one) is generated from sample citations you want to match. The proposal is measured against the environment's pinned citation regression cases and assigned a risk class (low / medium / high) by blast radius — how many existing references it would change.
  2. Scholar review. A scholar reviews the proposed style and its diff. Low blast radius can be approved directly; higher blast radius requires a closer look before approval.
  3. Admin apply. Once scholar-approved, an administrator applies it. The style is stored in the tenant database (no code deploy), and references re-render on the next publish.

Styles are versioned and never auto-applied by the platform; AI never mutates a published citation directly. The workflow is operable through the platform's citation endpoints today; a dedicated researcher-facing screen for proposing and reviewing styles is in progress (a design pass gates it). The regression cases that gate the workflow are described in the engineering guide Citation style testing & regression.

A published entry shows no References sectionThe entry type has no attestation walk declared — the platform does not guess where evidence livesDeclare the walk on the entry type: the occurrence record type, the connecting link, and the locator/source columns.
Evidence rows appear but without formatted citationsNo citation rules declared for the entry type, or the chosen citation style was not foundAdd citation rules mapping your columns to the standard citation fields, and confirm the style name matches an installed style exactly.
Citations render with awkward punctuation or separatorsThe active CSL style has not been refined for your materialRefine the style through the citation style configuration (scholar reviews, admin applies). Do not hand-edit rendered text — the next refresh would overwrite it.
An internal reference code appears inside a rendered rowA locator or source-detail declaration points at a linking column rather than a plain-text columnRe-point the declaration at the human-readable column; linking columns belong only in the join declaration.
