Standards and Citations

Purpose​

This page is the environment administrator's working view of the platform's standards support. It tells you which standards-backed capabilities your environment inherits without configuring anything, what you can configure on top, what published entries expose to the outside world, and what stays your environment's own interpretation. Read this when an institutional reviewer asks "what standards does our knowledge environment comply with?", when a federation partner asks whether a specific export format is supported, or when you are deciding which standards a field in your environment should declare conformance with.

The cross-audience overview is the Standards & Compliance Overview; this page is for someone running an environment.

Features​

What you inherit without configuration​

These capabilities ship with every environment. None of them require per-environment engineering, custom code, or a separate contract. Each carries a maturity tag; the standards overview records the audited maturity of every standard the platform engages with.

  • Stable, citable identity for published artifacts — a published artifact carries a stable public identity that an external identifier service (your library's DOI minter, for example) can target. The platform does not mint DOIs itself, and data cited against a frozen Edition cannot silently change · Maturity: Active · See Citation Integrity for the guarantees in detail.
  • Citation framework built on Citation Style Language (CSL) — citations render through CSL styles, the same open format reference managers and journals use. A catalog of more than seventy standard styles ships with the platform (Chicago and APA among them), and each environment holds its own active styles · Maturity: Built · Limit: a style that is not in the catalog can be installed on request — contact your platform operator.
  • Per-entry citation export in CSL JSON — every published entry's citation is available in CSL JSON, the interchange format reference managers read · Maturity: Built.
  • Media locators that follow IIIF and W3C Web Annotation conventions — a cited region of an image, audio/video segment, or 3D model is addressed by the artifact's own natural self-description (an image region selector, a 3D-model node name, a text passage identifier), never a platform-invented code · Maturity: Built · Limit: locator support ships today; dedicated standard viewers are not yet integrated. See Citation Integrity.
  • Vocabulary review backed by shared authorities — vocabulary values first seen in your imported data queue for review rather than being guessed at, and AI suggestions (when enabled) can reference public authority records: Wikidata for entities and concepts, PeriodO for historical periods. Suggestions are advisory only — a reviewer declares or rejects every value · Maturity: Active (the review queue); the authority-backed AI suggestions are Built · See Vocabulary Grounding.
  • Typed, self-describing API — integrations read published data through a documented GraphQL API: a typed contract a partner can inspect, not an undocumented internal surface · Maturity: Built.
  • Privacy-aware telemetry — activity records are redacted before they are stored and kept in a tamper-evident chain, supporting a defensible data-protection posture under GDPR · Maturity: Active · See Telemetry.
  • Accessibility controls in the reading interface — readers can adjust font size, line height, color presets, and font family. The platform targets WCAG 2.2 AA; a full audit against that target has not yet been recorded · Maturity: Active.

What you can configure on top​

  • Citation style — per environment and per entry type. Adopt a style from the shipped catalog, or refine one through the review workflow described below. Applying a style requires the administrator role; styles are versioned, and the platform refreshes published citations after a style is applied · Maturity: Active.
  • Vocabulary grounding — declare or reject queued vocabulary values through the Vocabulary Grounding workflow, with a display label of your choosing and optional links to standards from the platform's recognized list · Maturity: Active.
  • Field standards declarations — a field definition in your environment can declare which standards its content conforms to, chosen from the platform's approved standards list. Declarations are validated when you save the field definition — a standard outside the list is rejected with a validation message — and the declarations are visible through the API · Maturity: Active.
  • Voice and vocabulary substitution — independent of standards but composes with them: your wording is yours, the structural conformance underneath is the platform's. See Voice and Vocabulary · Maturity: Active.
  • Reference evidence and citation rules — declared once per published entry type; described in the next section · Maturity: Active.

How often you touch each of these:

  • Citation style — once at environment setup; revisit when you adopt a new venue's or institution's style guide.
  • Vocabulary grounding — during onboarding, and after any import that brings in vocabulary values your environment has not seen before.
  • Field standards declarations — when designing or revising a field definition. They are part of the field's definition, not a day-to-day switch.
  • Voice substitution — whenever the platform's default wording feels foreign to your researchers.
  • Reference evidence and citation rules — once per published entry type, when deciding how its References section should read.

Reference evidence and citations on published entries​

When a published entry shows a References section — one row per piece of evidence, each with a scholarly citation — two declarations on the entry type produce it. Both are configuration, not code: the platform supplies the machinery, your environment supplies the meaning.

  • The evidence declaration — where your evidence lives and how it joins. You declare which record type holds the occurrences of an entry (the passages, witnesses, specimens, or find records), which link connects them to the entry, and which of your columns carry the locators (page, chapter, line, quote) and the source details (author, work, editor). Every column name in the declaration is your environment's vocabulary; the platform never assumes any of it.
  • Citation rules — how citations are built and styled. You declare which of your columns map to the standard citation fields (author, title, editor, date, page), and which CSL style renders them. The result is a formatted citation per evidence row, plus standalone source citations beneath the evidence list.

Two behaviors worth knowing when designing the declarations:

  • Editions are optional. An occurrence with no published edition — a researcher's own catalogued manuscript or fragment — still appears as a full evidence row. Evidence is never gated on the existence of a published edition.
  • Paired-edition bridging. When an occurrence is catalogued against more than one edition — say a modern critical edition and an early printed one — the row bridges them in the classics convention: the modern citation first, then = volume.page (abbreviated editor) for the paired edition. The abbreviations and locators are your data; the bridge composition is the platform's.

Citation appearance (punctuation, abbreviated author forms, locale) is managed through CSL styles, not by editing rendered text. If a citation looks rough, the fix is a style refinement — see the review workflow below and the troubleshooting table.

What published entries expose​

When your environment publishes entries, the outside world can rely on:

  • A rendered citation on each published entry, produced against your active CSL style · Maturity: Built.
  • The same citation in CSL JSON at a stable address per entry, ready for import into reference managers · Maturity: Built.
  • Typed API access through which an integration partner reads published data under the same visibility rules as the reading interface · Maturity: Built.

Machine-readable semantic export formats — JSON-LD representations of published entries and research-object packaging for archival hand-off — are committed in the platform's architecture but not yet available. The standards overview records the current maturity of each format; point a federation partner there rather than promising a format ahead of its maturity tag.

Exports are produced by the platform; your environment does not build or maintain a custom export pipeline. What is yours is the data, the wording, and the entry-type design — not the export machinery.

Usability — how you use it​

The day-to-day operator paths:

  • Pick a citation style at environment setup: the platform's citation-style service lets you browse the shipped catalog and adopt a style per entry type. A dedicated configuration screen is not yet available — the service is operated through the platform's administrative citation endpoints, and your platform operator can run the selection with you. A style that is not in the catalog can be installed on request.
  • Declare a field's standards conformance: edit the field definition and choose from the platform's approved standards list. A declaration outside the list is rejected with a validation message when you save.
  • Review new vocabulary values: work the review queue through the Vocabulary Grounding workflow. When AI suggestions are enabled, a suggestion can reference a matching Wikidata or PeriodO record to inform your decision.
  • Answer "what standards does our knowledge environment comply with?": point the asker to the standards overview. It is written for exactly that conversation, and its maturity tags distinguish what ships today from what is committed but still on the way.

Refining a citation style (the review workflow)​

When the active style renders roughly for your material, a style is refined through a three-step review path, so no one person silently changes how every citation reads:

  1. Propose. A proposed style (or a refinement of the active one) is generated from sample citations you want to match. The proposal is measured against your environment's pinned citation test cases and assigned a risk class (low / medium / high) based on how many existing citations it would change.
  2. Scholar review. A scholar reviews the proposed style, its differences from the active style, and its risk class before approving.
  3. Admin apply. Once scholar-approved, an administrator applies it. The style is stored in your environment — no code change is involved — and the platform refreshes published citations after the style is applied.

Styles are versioned and never auto-applied by the platform; AI assistance never changes a published citation directly. The workflow runs through the platform's administrative citation endpoints today; a dedicated review screen is not yet available. Your platform operator can walk the three steps with you.

Troubleshooting​

SymptomLikely causeFix
A published entry shows no References sectionThe entry type has no evidence declaration — the platform does not guess where evidence livesDeclare it on the entry type: the occurrence record type, the connecting link, and the locator and source columns.
Evidence rows appear but without formatted citationsNo citation rules declared for the entry type, or the chosen citation style was not foundAdd citation rules mapping your columns to the standard citation fields, and confirm the style name matches an installed style exactly.
Citations render with awkward punctuation or separatorsThe active CSL style has not been refined for your materialRefine the style through the review workflow (scholar reviews, admin applies). Do not hand-edit rendered text — the next refresh would overwrite it.
An internal reference code appears inside a rendered rowA locator or source-detail declaration points at a linking column rather than a plain-text columnRe-point the declaration at the human-readable column; linking columns belong only in the join declaration.
A style your researchers need is not pre-loadedCSL is an open standard; the style file is portable XMLContact your platform operator to install it. Provide the CSL file or a published style URL.
A standards declaration is rejected when you save a field definitionThe declared standard is not on the platform's approved listChoose a standard from the list, or ask your platform operator to evaluate adding the one you need. The list is deliberately narrow: every entry on it is one the platform can stand behind.
A vocabulary value cannot be declared or rejected from the review queueThe acting account lacks the review permission for grounding decisionsAsk an environment administrator to take the decision, or to grant your role the review permission — see Permissions Panel.
A federation partner needs a machine-readable export formatSemantic export formats are committed but not yet availablePoint the partner at the maturity tags in the standards overview, and raise the need with your platform operator. Neither you nor the partner should have to take a conformance claim on faith.
