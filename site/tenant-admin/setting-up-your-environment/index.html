Setting up your environment: from vocabulary to first published records

Purpose​

This tutorial walks a new environment from empty to its first published records. It sequences the work — what to do first, what can wait, and which steps your platform operator performs for you — and hands you off to the feature page for each task when you are ready to act. Follow it once, in order, when you are standing up a fresh environment. Each later visit, you will go straight to the feature page you need.

The platform ships generic. Setup is how you make one environment speak your domain's language, decide who can do what, bring in your data under review, and put your first records in front of readers.

Before you start​

You need an environment that already exists and an account in it that holds the administrator role with permission management. Creating the environment and your first administrator account is platform-operator work, not something you do from inside the environment. If you are reading this without an environment to sign in to, that is the step to request first.

Two divisions of labor recur throughout this tutorial, so it helps to name them now:

  • Admin self-serve — you do it yourself from your environment's admin area.
  • Operator-mediated — you prepare and review the work, but your platform operator runs the step that takes effect. This is true for creating contributor accounts, applying a citation style, and launching the public surface.

The tutorial flags which kind each step is as you reach it.

Stages 1 and 2 can overlap — you can name concept families and compose roles in either order. Stage 3 depends on having concept families named, because the import wizard works in your category names. Stage 4 depends on having records to publish, so it comes last.

Stage 1 — Voice and vocabulary​

The single highest-leverage move in setup is naming your concept families — the categories your records belong to, such as Person, Place, or Text. Most of what a researcher reads is the platform's standard wording with those category names substituted into it, so a good set of labels improves hundreds of phrases at once before you write a single custom phrase.

A fresh environment already speaks in neutral English and substitutes your category names into its phrases automatically. You do not start from blank screens; you start from a working baseline and shape it.

  1. Name your concept families. This normally happens while you confirm your first import (Stage 3), and the labels can also be edited afterwards in your environment's admin area. If you are setting up before you have data to import, you can still set the labels directly.
  2. Watch for the wording review notice. When concept-family labels are missing or look machine-generated, the Permissions Panel shows a read-only notice with the flagged labels. Fix them; the notice returns if your labels change again, so treat it as a running check rather than a one-time gate.

Full detail on how wording resolves, role renaming, and the one phrase you cannot edit yourself (an individual platform phrase, which is operator-mediated today): Voice and Vocabulary.

Grounding vocabulary against external authorities​

Separate from the platform's own wording, your records bring in vocabulary the platform does not recognize — today, calendar names appearing in your data and standard identifiers declared on your field definitions. The platform collects each new value in a review queue rather than guessing. Grounding is where you decide, value by value, whether each becomes a recognized part of your environment's vocabulary, with a label you choose and optional links to recognized scholarly and data standards.

You do not need to ground anything to keep working — the queue is there when you are ready, usually after your first import. AI grounding suggestions, which can propose a label and a match against public reference sources, are switched off by default and turned on per environment; only the tenant administrator role can confirm a grounding.

When to come back to this: after Stage 3, once an import has populated the queue. Full detail: Vocabulary Grounding.

Stage 2 — Roles and contributors​

Decide who can do what before people arrive, so a new contributor lands in a role that already fits.

Every environment ships with four standard roles — Reviewer, External collaborator, Researcher contributor, and Project admin — and you compose custom roles from a fixed set of permission building blocks when the standard roles do not fit. Renaming a standard role changes its displayed label only, never what it allows.

  1. Open the Permissions Panel at /admin/permissions/. It opens on the Contributors tab; the other tabs are Roles, Project rules, Wording, and Project history.
  2. Compose the roles your team needs. On the Roles tab, combine the permission building blocks and name the role. A role assignment can be narrowed to specific record types or fields, so a guest collaborator can be scoped to one part of your project.
  3. Set your project rules. On the Project rules tab, decide whether high-impact saves need a reviewer who did not author the change, and how destructive changes are confirmed. These rules tighten what a role allows; they never grant more.
  4. Invite your contributors — operator-mediated. Contributor accounts are created by your platform operator. Once a person appears in the Contributors section, you assign their role there. So the sequence is: prepare the role, ask the operator to create the account, then assign the role when the person shows up in the panel.

A new contributor's account starts able to review proposals, apply low- and medium-risk changes, undo recent changes, and manage saved import setups — but not apply high-risk changes, configure fields, or manage permissions. Adjust from there with a role or a scoped assignment.

Full detail: Permissions Panel for composing and assigning; User and Access Management for the standard role catalog and how accounts are created.

Stage 3 — Your first import​

The Data Import Wizard is the supported path for getting data into an environment. It reads your source, proposes how each table and column should land, routes every proposal through a two-step approval, and writes records only when you press Start import in the final step. Nothing reaches your environment before that.

Treat the first run as a practice run. The wizard's fifth step is a full dry run that writes nothing, so you can see exactly what would happen before committing.

  1. Start in the sidebar at Imports & AI → Imports. The wizard accepts an Excel workbook, a MySQL dump or live connection, a set of CSV files, or a Coda document. Start from an upload route — the plain admin "Add" form does not read a source.
  2. Choose how involved you want to be. Step 3 asks the autonomy question — Hands off, Stay informed, Ask if unsure, or Confirm everything — saved per import. For a first import you do not yet know well, a more involved setting holds more suggestions for your decision. High-risk and restructure suggestions wait for explicit review whatever the setting, and every automatic decision comes with an undo window.
  3. Read the practice run (Step 5). Issues are grouped by cause rather than by row, with a good/warning/error breakdown. This is where a misread table or a field you did not expect surfaces — before any record is written.
  4. Approve in two steps. A reviewer approves each suggestion, then an administrator applies it. If a suggestion's risk level shifted while it waited for review, the apply step blocks until you acknowledge the new level.
  5. Start the import (Step 6) once every suggestion is approved or rejected. Watch the per-table progress, read the completion receipt, and use Open your workspace to see the results.

Naming your concept families (Stage 1) happens naturally here: confirming an import is the usual moment you give each category its display label.

Full detail, including the four source types, background imports for large files, and re-import behavior: Data Import Wizard.

Stage 4 — First published records and the readiness review​

Once your data is in and your researchers have a working set of records, the last setup stage prepares the public surface external readers will see.

Before your environment exposes a public Entry surface, the readiness review lets you review the filter and autocomplete controls that would become public, record a decision about each one, and confirm or defer each candidate. It is a review, not an apply step: recording decisions stores your review state and never changes the public surface by itself. The standalone review panel was retired with the transitional /app/admin/ shell; until its replacement ships in the admin tier, the review runs through your platform operator.

  1. Request the readiness report. Your platform operator runs the readiness audit for your environment and shares the report with you. The report is read-only — producing it never changes your environment's configuration.
  2. Work through each role's checklist. The report lists blockers, reviewed fields, candidate fields, and compact-control warnings — fields whose values are too long to show well in a compact public filter. Decide per field, with a reason for each decision, and send your decisions to your operator, who records them; each stored decision is durable and appears in the next report.
  3. Hand off for launch — operator-mediated. When the report reads clean, your platform operator performs the public launch. The report's "ratification unchanged" line confirms that review work never alters your environment's search configuration as a side effect; confirming that configuration and launching the surface are separate, explicit steps run with your operator.

Full detail: Entry Surface Readiness, and Public reader for what readers see once the surface launches.

Common stumbling points​

SymptomLikely causeFix
A researcher-facing phrase still shows generic platform wordingThe concept family the phrase mentions has no display label, or no entry exists for that phraseSet the concept-family label first — it fixes most generic-sounding phrases. For a specific phrase that still needs different wording, contact your platform operator. See Voice and Vocabulary.
A new contributor cannot be assigned a roleTheir account has not been created yetContributor accounts are operator-mediated. Ask your platform operator to create the account; assign the role once the person appears in the Contributors tab.
The "Add" form in the import area opened a bare form that does nothingThe plain admin "Add" form does not read a sourceClose it and start from an upload route at Imports & AI → Imports.
A re-import overwrote a researcher's manual editsThe run replaced the rows instead of doing an edit-preserving refreshRestore from your environment's backup and re-run as an edit-preserving refresh. If your operator runs imports for you, ask them to use the edit-preserving mode. See Data Import Wizard.
The readiness report reads clean but the public surface has not appearedRecording decisions does not launch the surfaceThe launch is a separate step. Confirm with your platform operator that it was run. See Entry Surface Readiness.
A vocabulary value you reviewed shows as unreviewed after a re-importA re-import should never reset a review decisionThis should not happen — contact your platform operator with the affected value and the import date.

Checklist summary​

  • Stage 1 — Voice and vocabulary (self-serve): name your concept families; act on the wording review notice; plan to work the grounding queue after your first import. → Voice and Vocabulary, Vocabulary Grounding
  • Stage 2 — Roles and contributors (roles self-serve; accounts operator-mediated): compose the roles your team needs, set project rules, then have accounts created and assign roles. → Permissions Panel, User and Access Management
  • Stage 3 — First import (self-serve): start from an upload route, choose an autonomy level, read the practice run, approve in two steps, then start the import. → Data Import Wizard
  • Stage 4 — First published records (review and launch operator-mediated until the admin-tier review UI ships): work the readiness review per role through your operator, then the operator performs the launch. → Entry Surface Readiness
