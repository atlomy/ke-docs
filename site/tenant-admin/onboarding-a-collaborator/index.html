Onboarding a new collaborator

Purpose​

A new person is joining your environment mid-project — a research assistant, a co-investigator, a visiting scholar, or a short-term contributor who needs access for a few weeks. This page is the start-to-finish sequence for getting them from "no account" to "working in the right tables with the right role." It also marks clearly which steps are yours to do and which only your platform operator can do, so you know where to wait and where to act.

Read this when someone new is arriving. For the new collaborator's own first session, hand them the researcher tutorial — Getting started — once their access is ready.

The sequence at a glance​

Onboarding has two owners. The account itself is created by your platform operator; everything about what the collaborator can do inside your environment is yours to configure on the Permissions Panel.

StepWho does itWhere
1. Decide the role and scopeYouPlan ahead — no UI yet
2. Request the accountYou ask; operator createsOutside your environment
3. Assign the roleYouPermissions Panel → Contributors
4. Prepare the workspaceYouWorkspace views and sidebar
5. Brief the collaboratorYouPoint them at the researcher tutorial
6. Verify their accessYou and the collaboratorPermissions Panel + a check from their side

You can do steps 1, 3, and 4 yourself. Step 2 depends on your platform operator. Steps 5 and 6 involve the collaborator directly.

Usability — how you use it​

1. Choose or compose the role before they arrive​

Decide what the person should be able to do before the account exists, so you can assign a role the moment they appear in your Contributors list.

Every environment ships with a standard role catalog: Reviewer, External collaborator, Researcher contributor, and Project admin. Match the person to the closest standard role:

  • A reviewing scholar who reads and comments but should not save changes → Reviewer.
  • A guest from outside the project making small additive changes in one area → External collaborator.
  • A team researcher doing routine editing → Researcher contributor.
  • A co-director who needs configuration and role-management authority → Project admin.

If no standard role fits, compose a custom one from the platform's fixed set of permission building blocks on the Permissions Panel's Roles tab, and name it. You combine existing permissions; you cannot define new ones. See User and Access Management for the standard catalog and Permissions Panel for composition.

A new contributor starts with a baseline set of permissions: they can review suggestions, save routine (low- and medium-impact) changes, undo recent changes, and manage saved import setups. They do not start with the ability to apply high-impact changes, configure fields, or manage permissions — those are granted deliberately, by assigning a role that includes them. Keep this in mind when matching a visiting or temporary collaborator to a role: the safe default already withholds the high-authority actions.

You can also narrow where a role applies. A role assignment — or a custom role — can be scoped to specific record types or fields, so a collaborator working on one collection does not gain reach across the whole environment. This is the right tool for a visiting scholar who should touch one area only.

2. Request the account​

Contributor accounts are created by your platform operator, not from inside your environment. Send the operator the person's details and ask them to create the account. This is the one step you cannot complete yourself; until the operator finishes, the person will not appear in your Contributors list.

3. Assign the role​

Once the operator confirms the account exists, the person appears in the Contributors tab of the Permissions Panel at /admin/permissions/. Open their row, pick the standard or custom role you decided on in step 1, and — if appropriate — set a scope on the same row to limit the role to specific record types or fields.

If your environment has a project rule requiring a second contributor to confirm high-impact changes, that rule already applies to the new collaborator; you do not configure it per person. Project rules are environment-wide and sit on top of every role.

4. Prepare the workspace​

Make the collaborator's first session land them somewhere useful rather than on a raw table.

  • Shared default view. Each table has one default view — the view that opens when anyone first visits that table. If your team has agreed on a working view (its filters, sort, and columns), set it as the default so the new person sees the same starting point as everyone else. The default is shared across the team, so this one action prepares the workspace for the collaborator without any per-person setup. See Save a view.
  • Sidebar organization. The sidebar lists your environment's tables. If your environment groups or orders tables for clarity, that organization is already in place for the new collaborator; there is nothing per-person to configure.

5. Brief the collaborator on day one​

Tell the collaborator three things and then let the product teach the rest:

  • Their work is safe to explore — drafts do not publish by accident, and recent changes can be undone.
  • Reading comes before writing — they can search and read existing records before editing anything.
  • Help is built in — the contextual help is available without leaving the workspace.

For everything beyond that, point them at the researcher tutorial: Getting started. It walks a new researcher through their first week — signing in, finding the team's default view, reading, and making safe first edits — so you do not have to write your own orientation.

6. Verify their access — including a check from their side​

Confirm two things before you consider onboarding done.

  • From your side: on the Permissions Panel's Contributors tab, the collaborator shows the role you assigned, with the scope you intended.
  • From their side: ask the collaborator to open the search and reading area and look for a note about hidden collections. The platform shows a banner — "N collections are hidden — they require a review capability you don't hold" — whenever a person's role does not include the review capability some collections require. If the collaborator sees that banner and you intended them to have full access, their role is too narrow; widen it on the Contributors tab. If you intended to restrict them, the banner confirms the restriction is working. This from-their-side check catches scope mismatches that the Contributors tab alone can hide.

Visiting and temporary access​

A visiting or short-term collaborator is onboarded the same way, with two adjustments:

  • Prefer a scoped role. Limit the role to the record types or fields they need rather than granting environment-wide reach. The baseline new-contributor permissions already withhold high-impact actions; a scope narrows reach further.
  • Plan the wind-down. When the visit ends, change or remove their role on the Contributors tab. Removing the account itself is a platform-operator action, like creating it — ask the operator to deactivate or remove the account when access should end entirely.

Troubleshooting​

SymptomLikely causeFix
The new person does not appear in the Contributors tabThe account has not been created yetContributor accounts are created by your platform operator. Confirm the operator has created the account; until then there is no row to assign a role to.
You assigned a role but the collaborator cannot act on content they shouldTheir role assignment carries a scope that excludes that contentOpen their row on the Contributors tab and check the scope. Widen it or remove it.
The collaborator sees a "collections are hidden" noteTheir role does not include the review capability some collections requireIf they should have access, assign or compose a role that includes that capability. If the restriction is intended, this is working as configured. See Permissions Panel.
No standard role matches what the collaborator needsThe standard catalog is fixedCompose a custom role from the available permission building blocks on the Roles tab. If the permission you need does not exist in the platform's set at all, contact your platform operator.
You need to remove a departing collaborator's account entirelyAccount removal is a platform-operator actionChange or remove their role on the Contributors tab to cut access immediately, and ask your platform operator to deactivate or remove the account itself.
